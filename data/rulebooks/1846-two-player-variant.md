# 1846: The Race for the Midwest — Two Player Variant

## Table of Contents

*1846 Two Player Variant, by Tom Lehmann. While 1846 is intended for three to five players, this official two player variant modifies the setup, Private Company distribution, and a handful of play and game-end rules.*

## Setup

**2.2** Initial Bank Size: **$7,000**. Give each player **$600** from the bank.

**2.3** Randomly use two Private Companies from each of the blue-square and orange-circle groups, removing the others. Randomly remove one corporation from each of the two groups below, placing one of its tokens in the indicated city *hex*. (This token has no effect until a tile is laid in that hex and upgraded to a green tile, see below.) Also place a token in the removed corporation's home city.

| Corporation | City hex |
| --- | --- |
| Grand Trunk | Toledo |
| New York Central | Cleveland |
| Erie | Erie |
| Pennsylvania | Fort Wayne |

| Corporation | City hex |
| --- | --- |
| Baltimore & Ohio | Cincinnati |
| Chesapeake & Ohio | Cincinnati |
| Illinois Central | Terre Haute |

**2.4** Use **5** Phase I, **5** Phase II, **3** Phase III, and **4** Phase IV trains.

## Private Company Distribution

(This replaces the normal 1846 distribution procedure.) Place all Private Companies in play face up between the players. Randomly select a first player and give that player the Priority Deal card. The *other* player *must* buy any one of the Private Companies. Players then alternate, either buying a Private Company or passing, until either:

* **All Private Companies are bought:** Begin the first stock round with the Priority Deal player.
* **Both players pass** in succession with *two or more* Private Companies remaining: Do two Operating Rounds (collect Income and operate purchased Independent RRs). Return to this step.
* **One Private Company is left** unbought and a player passes: Reduce the price of this last Private Co. by $10 and offer it to the other player, who may either buy it or pass. On each subsequent pass, lower its price by another $10 and offer it to the other player until a player buys it. If its price falls to $0, the player to whom it is offered must take it. Begin the first stock round with the Priority Deal player.

## Play

Play proceeds normally, except:

* Two cities (determined above) each gain a blocking token once their tile is upgraded to green.
* **5.36** A player is limited to **70%**, not 60%, shares in a given corporation.
* **5.41** The certificate limit is **19**. If one or more corporations close, it becomes 16.
* **6.84** The total number of Phase IV trains in the game is now **4** (not unlimited).
* **6.86** In Phase IV, if there are no trains available, a corporation may not buy another corporation's *last* train and is not required to purchase a train.

## Game End

**10.1** Assuming no bankruptcy, the game end is triggered either by the bank running out of money, in which case the game ends normally, or by buying the last Phase IV train, in which case the game ends after the next complete pair of Operating Rounds (if it doesn't end sooner).

*EXAMPLE: If the last Phase IV train is bought during OR 9, then the game will either end after OR 12 or, if the bank runs out of money during OR 9 or OR 10, after OR 10.*

# La Granja: Deluxe Master Set Solo Rule Book

## Table of Contents

*Solo mode for La Granja by Dávid Turczi and John Albertson. Additional development by MiKe and ode.*

*Led by a really mean boss donkey, a whole herd of donkeys sets out in the area of Alpich to start their own business. A farm run by donkeys?! That is wild! But yes! As a human farmer you are in real trouble now, my friend. The donkey farm has unlimited power since donkeys are incredibly strong! And since they are donkeys themselves they deliver the heck out of their crops! All the marketeers fear those mean donkeys and take whatever good they offer - just to be left alone! It is your task to not let those donkeys become the leaders of La Granja someday. Are you strong enough to take on a whole donkey herd and claim the beautiful estate yourself?*

## Components

* 18 Burrobot cards
* 15 Donkey King's Challenge cards with 9 reminder tiles
* 2 double-sided Bustling Town overlay cards
* 1 Leon's Donkey Farm board

In these rules "Leon", "it", or "its" will refer to the automated solo opponent, while "you" and "yours" will refer to the lone human player.

## Setup

1. Place the game board in the center of the table with the 2 player side facing up (1).
2. You receive a player board (A), 25 player markers of 1 color (2A), 1 set of donkey tiles (2B), 1 victory point, and 1 silver (2C). Leon receives its own board (B), 25 player markers of 1 color (2A), 1 set of donkey tiles (2B), 1 victory point, and 1 silver (2C).
3. Place a player marker (of the corresponding color) on the trade commodity space (3) on both your and Leon's board.
4. Remove the following cards as they are not supported when playing solo: Village Elder (F005), Tenant (F026), Customs Officer (F109), Organizer (F125). Shuffle the remaining farm cards and deal four to yourself and place a farm card in each of Leon's market barrows (4).
5. The rest of the farm cards are placed face down next to the main board (5).
6. Prepare 6 stacks of roof tiles - one for each round of the game by shuffling all roof tiles of the same number together. Each tile has a round number on it (6).
7. Place two roof tiles on each roof space from 2 to 6 (randomly selected from the matching numbered ones).
8. Place two #1 roof tiles above the row of roof tile stacks (8).
9. All roof tiles that were not placed on the main board are returned to the game box.
10. Place two craft markers on the respective craft buildings plus one victory point each on the player ownership marker areas next to the buildings (10).
11. Roll a die. The number rolled determines where the building order marker 1 is placed. Afterwards the die is rolled 2 more times. The number rolled determines which buildings are occupied by building number order markers 2 and 3. If a building already has a marker, the die is rolled until a building without a marker has been determined (11).
12. Randomly determine the start player and give them the turn order marker with the number 1 (12). The other player receives the marker with the number 2. Return the remaining player order markers to the game box.
13. Set aside 5 dice (13). Any remaining dice are returned to the game box.
14. The player with turn order marker #1 places their player marker on the number 2 stall in the middle of the market (14). The other player places a player marker on the adjacent number 3 stall.
15. Place each player's siesta marker on the space at the bottom of the siesta track with the starting player's marker on top (15).
16. Victory points, silver, and exhaustion markers are placed next to the main board and form a general supply (16).
17. After a few plays, if you find yourself consistently defeating Leon consider adding a few of Donkey King's challenge cards at this point.
18. Shuffle Leon's Donkey tiles and place them in a face down draw pile on its board (18).
19. Prepare Leon's Burrobot deck: Set aside the 3 cards with the challenge icon (BB16-BB18). Shuffle the remaining 15 cards and place them to the right of its board in a face down draw pile (19).
20. Create a row of Burrobot cards by drawing three cards and place them face-up, in a row, to the right of the draw pile (20).
21. The game can start now.

## General Concepts

### Leon's Resources

On its board, Leon collects silver, goods, trade commodities, purchased roof markers, and victory points. It drafts dice and utilizes its donkey tiles. When it gains a good or a trade commodity, place one of its player markers in the appropriate area, and when it spends a good, return a marker to the supply.

Leon does not distinguish between goods (pigs, cured meat, olives, grain, grapes, food, wine). When it needs to deliver a good of any type, take a player marker from any of its farms (regardless of type), or from the goods area of its board (if no goods are on farms). If it needs to deliver a trade commodity, take a player marker from its trade commodity area on its board. If it needs to deliver a silver, discard a silver coin from its board and place a player marker from the supply instead.

At certain points, Leon might require resources to be converted. It may make 1 conversion per payment/delivery to get the correct resource. Only goods from Leon's player board may be converted (goods from his farms can not be converted). See the table below for what legal conversions it can make. If it has multiple options, Leon picks the first available one (in the order shown in the table) that satisfies its need completely. Leon keeps any excess resulting from the conversion. If it cannot satisfy a need by a single conversion, it does not convert at all.

| If Leon is missing... | It can spend this instead |
| --- | --- |
| Silver | (A) 1 good, to gain 2 silver, OR (B) 1 trade commodity to gain 4 silver. |
| Good | (A) 2 silver to gain 1 good, OR (B) 1 trade commodity to gain 2 goods. |
| Trade commodity | (A) 5 silver to gain 1 trade commodity, OR (B) 3 goods to gain 1 trade commodity. |

### Burrobot Cards

While Leon is only a donkey, the wisdom of the herd makes it do mighty things. Luckily, you can observe the herd and anticipate its moves, as it was a rational human being.

* **1 (A)** — Section used to activate other Burrobot cards. *In this case the rightmost one (furthest from the draw deck).*
* **2 (B)** — Section used to decide which roof tile to buy. *In this case, the left one.*
* **3 (C)** — Section used to draft dice and select targets for delivery. *In this case number five, and looking upwards (six, one, two, etc.).*
* **3 (D)** — Section used to select new targets in the Transportation Phase. *In this case craft buildings, and Leon scores 1 extra victory point if first player.*
* **4 (E)** — Icon used by the Colchonera, Long Distance Trade, and Trade Board modules.

Whenever the rules instruct you to activate a Burrobot card to tuck it somewhere, follow this procedure:

1. Flip the top card of the draw pile.
2. The top (1A) of the newly revealed card will show three cards in a row. These icons relate to the three cards in the Burrobot row. Take the indicated card from the row - not the revealed card - and tuck it into the next empty slot on the bottom side of Leon's board. For example if the newly revealed card indicates the rightmost icon, tuck the rightmost card (the card furthest from the draw deck).
3. Cards tucked in the first slot should have their top one section (1A) covered, cards tucked in the rest of the slots should show their top two sections (1A, 2B) covered.
4. Then, slide the two remaining cards in the Burrobot row to the right, to close any gaps if necessary. Then place the recently revealed card on the leftmost slot (next to the draw pile). Continuing the above example, the two cards previously on the row are slid to the right, and the new card becomes the leftmost card of the row.

## Gameplay

You play your turns in each phase as normal. The following section details how to resolve Leon's turn in each phase. Play in turn order, unless stated otherwise.

## Farm Phase

### 1. Play a Farm Card and Draw New Card(s)

You take your turn in this step as normal. When it is Leon's turn to play cards, determine how many cards Leon will play:

* Leon refills each empty market barrow slot, and plays one more card.

**Exception:** Leon does not play cards in the first round (while you play two cards normally).

Leon ignores hand limit icons on farm extensions, as it does not have a "hand of cards".

#### Card Play Procedure

Repeat the following procedure the determined amount of times:

1. Draw a card from the main farm draw deck.
2. If Leon has an empty market barrow space on the top of its board, tuck it there.
3. Otherwise...
   * a. If Leon has more farm extensions than fields showing the same resource as the drawn card: Tuck the drawn card on the left side of its board, to become a field.
   * b. If Leon has as many fields showing the same resource as the drawn card: Tuck the drawn card on the right side of its board, to become a farm extension.

**Note:** as a consequence of these rules, the first non-market barrow card it plays will be a farm extension. Leon does not pay for farm extensions.

*Example: In the Farm phase Leon refills the market barrow slot currently open on its board (A), then draws a new card. Since it is an olive farm, which it has none of (it only has a wheat farm (B)), tuck it on the left side of Leon's board (C). In the Farm phase of the next round (assume for simplicity that nothing else changed since), it simply draws one card. Since it is another olive farm (D), and it has one of that already and one farm extension too, the new card becomes a farm extension - tuck it on the right side of Leon's board.*

### 2. Take Income

Leon receives silver income as normal.

### 3. Growth on Fields and Pig Offspring

Place a player marker on each of Leon's empty fields. If Leon has an empty pig den space on a farm extension, cover that space with an exhaustion marker and Leon gains 1 good and 1 silver onto its board.

### 4. Purchase Roof Tiles

When it is Leon's turn to purchase a roof tile, activate a Burrobot card to tuck it in the first slot on the bottom of its board (as described earlier). This card will indicate one of:

* **Purchase left tile:** It purchases the indicated roof tile, by spending silver equal to round number if possible, (or by converting 1 good or 1 trade commodity, if such conversion would give it enough silver). If you already purchased the indicated tile, it purchases the other. If it does not have enough silver (even by converting 1 good or 1 trade commodity), it does not purchase a roof tile.
* **Purchase right tile:** Same as above, but with preferring the other tile.
* **Do not purchase:** It does not take a roof tile. When playing with the appropriate challenge, on cards with the challenge icon, it receives 2 silver and 1 victory point (as indicated).

If it has no free roof tile spaces left on its board, resolve as if "Do not purchase" was indicated.

If it purchases a roof tile, place it on the leftmost empty roof tile space on its board. It will gain 1 good onto its board and the indicated number of victory points.

## Revenue Phase

When it is Leon's turn to draft a die, activate one Burrobot card to tuck it in the second slot on the bottom of its board (as described earlier). This card will indicate a die number; Leon drafts a die of that value.

If no dice of that value remain, pick the next higher (up arrow) or lower (down arrow) depending on the arrow indicator, circling around between 1 and 6 if necessary.

Then, depending on the value of the die actually drafted, Leon receives the following revenue:

* **Die 1** — Leon gains 1 silver and 1 good onto its board.
* **Die 2** — In rounds 1-5: Leon plays 1 card. In round 6: Leon immediately performs a free delivery using the recently revealed card for target selection.
* **Die 3** — Leon gains 2 goods onto its board.
* **Die 4** — Leon gains 4 silver.
* **Die 5** — Leon advances 1 space on the siesta track.
* **Die 6** — Leon immediately performs a free delivery using the recently revealed card for target selection.

*Example: You have already taken a die during this Revenue Phase, so 4 dice remain on the board. The newly tucked card (A) shows a die of number five, with an up arrow. Unfortunately, there are no dice with value 5 (B) or 6 (C) on the board, so Leon "wraps around", selecting a die with value 1 (D). As shown on its board (E), drafting a die of 1 gives one good and one silver to Leon (F).*

When it is Leon's second turn to draft a die, activate another Burrobot card to tuck it in the third slot on the bottom of its board (as described earlier) and repeat the above procedure, including gaining revenue again.

Finally, Leon also gains revenue for the leftover die (in turn order, as usual).

## Transportation Phase

### 1. Choose a Donkey Tile

You choose your donkey tile first. You may place it face up, Leon is not smart enough to read it after all. Then, reveal a random donkey tile from Leon's draw pile.

At the start of the Transportation Phase of the fourth round shuffle all of Leon's donkey tiles to create its draw pile anew.

### 2. Advance on the Siesta Track

In turn order you and Leon advance on the siesta track according to the chosen donkey tiles (you may use other bonuses, such as craft markers, as usual). If Leon has a (face-down) craft marker, remove it from the game, and advance Leon one step on the siesta track.

Then, rearrange turn order according to the normal rules.

### 3. Carry Out Free Deliveries

When it is Leon's turn to deliver, it performs all of its possible free deliveries (the same way a human opponent would). First, activate one Burrobot card to tuck it in the fourth (last) slot on the bottom of its board (as described earlier). If the revealed card shows a badge, and Leon is ahead in turn order, it receives 1 victory point.

Select a target to deliver to (as described below), and deliver there (using goods, silver, and trade commodities from its board and farms accordingly) as many times as possible (including making a conversion if necessary). Remember, Leon always spends goods from its fields before spending goods from its board.

If it fully supplies the target, resolve the appropriate effects (see below), and select a new target to continue with. Repeat the procedure until Leon runs out of resources or has used all of its free deliveries.

If Leon cannot make a delivery (for lack of resources) despite having free deliveries left over, it receives 2 silver, and forfeits all remaining free deliveries.

#### Delivery Target Selection

If there is a market barrow or a craft building, where Leon has delivered at least once, but has not yet finished it, that is Leon's target. If Leon has no current target: look at the most recently tucked card below its board (usually this is the fourth card: when delivering during the Transportation Phase). In this case it does not matter where it has delivered most recently.

If the tucked card shows a market barrow, it is going to deliver to the market barrows.

* If it has two available market barrows to select, use the one with a value matching the die indicator of the tucked card.
* If no barrow of that exact value is available, pick the next higher or lower value one, depending on the arrow indicator, circling around between 1 and 6 if necessary.
* If both barrows have the same value, pick the left one.
* If it has no market barrows available, resolve as if the tucked card showed craft buildings.

If the tucked card shows craft buildings, it is going to deliver to an open craft building.

* If the building indicated by the die value is available (it is open, and Leon has not yet fully supplied it), select that building.
* If the building is not available, select the next higher or lower numbered building, depending on the arrow indicator.
* If there are any available buildings you have not fully supplied yet, Leon considers buildings you have fully supplied also not open.
* In the rare case of Leon having already fully supplied every craft building, resolve as if the tucked card showed market barrows.

If you need to select multiple targets during the same phase, start from the same position (i.e. it will select the next available craft building clockwise/counter-clockwise from its first selection, etc).

#### Supplying a Craft Building

If Leon fills each space on a craft building's row, it scores victory points the same way you would:

* Victory points equal to the current game round,
* An additional 1 victory point if it supplied this craft building before you,
* And another 1 victory point if this causes the opening of another craft building.

Leon receives the corresponding craft marker. Place it on its board, face down. Leon ignores the ability of the craft marker.

#### Supplying a Market Barrow

If Leon fills each space on a market barrow, it places one marker in the market on the main board on a space matching the value of the card.

* In the rare case that all such places are occupied, it will return one of your player markers from a space of that value, and replaces it. In the even rarer case when all the markers occupying such places are Leon's, it will place a marker on the next highest empty space. It gains victory points equal to the value of the market barrow card (not the space). It does not return any of your player markers adjacent to it in this case.
* If multiple options of the same value are available, select one with more of your player markers adjacent to it.
* If still multiple options are available, choose one farthest from other player markers of its own.
* If still multiple options are available, choose randomly.
* Leon placing a marker returns your adjacent, smaller valued markers as usual.
* Leon scores 1 victory point for each player marker returned to you.
* Leon gains one trade commodity, and victory points equal to the card's value, same as you would.

Discard the filled card (returning player markers from it to its supply).

### 4. Purchased Deliveries

When it is Leon's turn to resolve purchased deliveries, if there is a market barrow or a craft building, where Leon has delivered at least once, but has not yet finished it, it will continue delivering there using its extra deliveries (paying 1 silver each), until it runs out of resources to deliver or extra delivery options.

Leon may purchase as many extra deliveries as indicated on its farm extensions plus one (as indicated on its board).

**Important:** It will not convert goods/trade commodities into silver when paying for a delivery, if the conversion makes the delivery itself impossible.

It only selects a new target in this step, if it can finish it in this step, considering both its remaining resources and remaining available extra deliveries. Otherwise it simply forfeits its remaining extra deliveries.

## Scoring Phase

Both you and Leon score normally for markers in the market and for the position on the siesta track, as described in the normal rules.

Then, discard all 4 Burrobot cards tucked under Leon's board, and place it in a face up discard pile near its board. At the end of the third round the Burrobot deck will be empty. Take all twelve discarded Burrobot cards (but not the three cards in the row), shuffle them together and place them to the left of the row to form a new face down draw deck.

## Game End

You sell your left-over resources as described in the normal rules, then score for every 5th silver you have left. Leon scores using the following rates:

* 1 victory point for every 2 goods left over on its board (excluding goods on its fields);
* 1 victory point for every 5 silver it has left over;
* 2 victory points for each trade commodity it has left over.
* 1 victory point for each craft marker remaining on its board.

You win if you have scored more victory points than Leon. If you tie or Leon scores more than you, then a donkey was smarter than you and you lose.

## Donkey King's Challenges

If you wish to increase the difficulty of facing off Leon, you may wish to accept the challenges of the Donkey King itself! Feel free to select these challenges randomly, or pick the ones you enjoy more.

The cards are marked with one or two donkey icons, to indicate relative difficulty. Once you can win the game without challenges, we recommend adding 2 one-donkey challenges or 1 two-donkey challenge. The more total worth of challenges you take on, the harder the game will be.

Challenges can be used with all the modules, except where explicitly noted otherwise.

#### Refilling Building Cards

When drawing a new building card, always place it left of all the other building cards on the offer.

#### Discarding a Building

When this revenue effect is resolved, and there is at least one building card on the offer, roll an unused die:

* **Roll of 1, 2, or 3:** discard the rightmost building card from the offer.
* **Roll of 4 or 5:** discard the second building card from the right from the offer. If there is only one card on the offer, discard that one.
* **Roll of 6:** discard the leftmost building card from the offer.

Leon never builds buildings (it is a donkey, after all), and ignores the upper ability of the building cards.

#### Supplying a Market Barrow (Bustling Town)

When Leon supplies a market barrow, and is about to place a marker on the board, the presence of buildings (you have built) affect its priorities:

* If it is possible to place adjacent to a building with a Dominance scoring condition, ignore all other possible places when selecting a place.
* Otherwise, if it is possible to place adjacent to a building with a Presence scoring condition, ignore all other possible places when selecting a place.

### Colchonera

**Changes in setup:** Place one of Leon's player markers on its silver delivery space on the Colchonera board.

**Changes in gameplay:** When Leon performs free deliveries during Step 3 of the Transportation Phase (maximum once per round) check the 3rd tucked Burrobot card. If it shows a Colchonera icon on its bottom, then instead of the first free delivery, place one of Leon's player markers (without spending anything from its board) to the rightmost empty space on the Colchonera board it can legally place, based on its siesta track position.

Because Leon is not spending goods it does not receive any immediate rewards for delivering to the Colchonera board.

*Example: At the beginning of Step 3 of the Transportation Phase, Leon has 3 free deliveries (A), and is missing one delivery to finish a craft building (B). It is on the second section of the siesta track (C). Because the third tucked card shows a Colchonera icon (D), it uses its first delivery (E) to block the right-most space it can (the pig, in this case), without spending anything. Then, it reverts to the priorities described in the core solo rules: using its second delivery to finish supplying (F) the craft building (spending 1 silver as usual), and then looking at its fourth tucked card to pick a new target (G) for its last free delivery.*

If it cannot place a marker on the Colchonera board, ignore the icon, and proceed normally.

**Changes in scoring:** At the end of the game, Leon scores points for its markers on the Colchonera board the same way you would.

### Counselors & Outlaws

**Changes in setup:** During setup, you may choose either a counselor board or an outlaw board for yourself. Leon does not receive either.

**Changes in gameplay:** During the revenue phase, if Leon has the option of choosing between a gray and a white die of the same value:

* If you have a counselor board, it chooses the gray die.
* If you have an outlaw board, it chooses the white die.

Whenever it drafts a gray die, place an exhaust marker in the trade commodity area of its board. Once it has two markers there, discard both, and Leon gains a trade commodity. (In other words, Leon gains a trade commodity for every 2nd gray die it drafts.)

If you have an outlaw board, it also must spend a silver if able (including making a conversion if necessary) whenever it drafts a gray die.

*Example: You're playing with an outlaw board. This round, Leon starts the drafting. Its second tucked card instructs it to take a die of 5 (A), so it prefers to leave you with the gray die, taking the white die (B). Then, you draft another die (a three, in this case) (C). Its third tucked card instructs Leon to take a die of six (D), but none is available, so following its down arrow, it decides to draft a die of 5 (E). Since only a gray die remains there, it takes the gray die (F), discarding one silver (G), and placing an exhaust marker on its trade commodity area (H). Since it already had another exhaust marker (I), it discards both, gaining a trade commodity additionally (J).*

### Donkey Song

**Changes in setup:** Set the Donkey Song tiles up as described in the multiplayer rules of this module, but make sure to randomize the position of each tile within the sections.

**Changes in gameplay:** You interact with the Donkey Song tiles normally, as described in the multiplayer rules of this module.

When it is Leon's turn to purchase a Donkey Song tile in the Scoring Phase, if its marker is high enough on the siesta track to reach at least one available Donkey Song tile, resolve the following:

* If Leon has at least one silver: it pays one silver, and discards the first available Donkey Song tile of its current section, and gains double the victory points shown on the siesta track.
* If Leon does not have at least one silver: it still discards the first available Donkey Song tile, but receives no benefits beyond the original victory point reward for the siesta track position. Leon does not use conversions to gain a silver for this purpose.
* If there are no tiles in its current section, Leon discards the first available tile from a section below.
* If none of the sections below has a tile left, no tiles are discarded.

Leon does NOT use the Donkey Song tiles, merely removes them from the offer.

To determine the "first available" tile use the arrangement numbered 1-2 / 3-4 within each section.

### Grand Markers

**Changes in setup:** Place one of Leon's grand markers on the market during setup (same as yours, as described in the multiplayer rules of this module), place the other of its grand markers on Leon's board.

**Changes in gameplay:** Leon never places grand markers on delivery spaces (on craft buildings or market barrows).

Whenever Leon completes a craft building, it (additionally) gains 1 victory point per grand marker it has on its board.

Whenever it completes a market barrow, if it has a grand marker on its board:

* If Leon would place a player marker to the market adjacent to at least one of your grand markers that is on a space of lower value, place its grand marker instead of a regular one (thus returning the grand marker to you).
* If Leon would place a player marker to the market that would return 2 or more of your regular markers, place its grand marker instead of a regular one (to double the victory point reward for these returns).
* Otherwise, place a regular player marker for Leon, as described in the normal rules.

All changes from the multiplayer rules of this module still apply:

* Grand markers can only be returned by other grand markers.
* You and Leon score 2 victory points per grand marker you have on the market during the Scoring Phase.
* You and Leon score 2 victory points per player marker (including a grand marker) returned to the other by the placement of a grand marker.

*Example: If your markers are in the market showing the (yellow) configuration shown, and Leon has just finished supplying a market barrow:*

* *If it was a value 2 (A), it places on one of the indicated spots (since both are adjacent to two of your markers), depending on the position of its other markers, or chosen randomly.*
* *If it was a value 3 (B), it places on the indicated spot (adjacent to three of your markers).*
* *If it was a value 4 (C), it places on the indicated spot on the right, returning your marker from the value 3 space above it, scoring 1 additional victory point.*
* *If it was a value 5 (D), it places on one of the indicated spots (since both are adjacent to two of your markers), depending on the position of its other markers, or chosen randomly. If it chooses the higher space, it will return your marker on the value 4 space (scoring 1 victory point). However, if it chooses the lower space, it can return your grand marker, therefore it places its own grand marker there, returning yours, scoring 2 additional victory points.*
* *If it was a value 6 (E), it places on the indicated spot on the right, returning both your adjacent markers from the value 4 and 5 spaces around it. Since it can return two of your markers, it would place its own grand marker instead, to score a total of 4 additional victory points (for returning both your markers).*

### Hired Hands

**Changes in setup:** During setup, replace the "4 donkey" tile with the "4 siesta steps" tile for Leon (the same as for yourself). Place both of Leon's wooden workers of its color on its board.

**Changes in gameplay:** When drafting a revenue die (while having at least one of its workers available on its board), Leon places a worker on an open craft building (that Leon hasn't supplied yet) or a market barrow with a number matching the most recently tucked card, preferring whichever matches the target selection of the same card (if both are available). If the matching building is not open or Leon already supplied it, and Leon has no market barrow with a value matching the number, the worker is not placed.

*Example: The second tucked card instructs Leon to draft a value 5 die (A). Since there are no value 5 ones, it proceeds to look towards higher ones first, ending up selecting a value 6 die (B). Since the sixth craft building is open, and Leon has not yet supplied it, it places one of its available workers on it (C).*

Whenever selecting a (new) delivery target, if one or both of its workers are in play (i.e. not on its board), Leon considers any market barrow/craft building without its workers to be unavailable (i.e. it will select a target with a worker).

**Note:** delivering to another target that Leon has already started delivering to is still a higher priority, workers only matter when selecting a new target. However, if both worker meeples are on Leon's board, then proceed with target selection as normal.

When Leon is delivering to a target with a worker in the Step 3 of the Transportation Phase, perform an additional free delivery to that target first, then retrieve the worker in question to Leon's board before continuing with its regular free deliveries.

*Example: Leon has four free deliveries (A). Since it has an unfinished craft building delivery, it uses the first to deliver there (B). Then, normally it would use the 4th tucked card to look for a new target (C) (fourth craft building in this case), but since it has a worker on the sixth craft building, it selects that as the new target first. It retrieves the worker to its board (D), gaining an additional delivery, so it can supply the craft building using three deliveries (E) and still has one free delivery left over. At this point it reverts to looking at the 4th tucked card for instructions (C), and delivers to the fourth craft building once (F).*

### Ladies of La Granja

**Changes in setup:** Randomly select 3 Ladies of La Granja cards. Choose one for yourself, then randomly select one of the remaining two. Flip the selected one to its back side (where it shows the ability for Leon) placing it next to its board, and return the third card to the game box.

**Changes in gameplay:** Leon's Lady of La Granja card provides it with a special ability, which is active during the whole game.

**Clarification:** When using together with the Broken Roofs module, Carmen will only buy leftover regular roof tiles, not a broken one.

### Long Distance Trade

**Changes in gameplay:** When it is Leon's turn during the Scoring Phase, check the 4th tucked Burrobot card. If it shows a Long Distance Trade icon on its bottom, then place one of Leon's player markers (without spending anything from its board) to the Long Distance Trade board as shown below:

* During rounds 1-4, Leon delivers to the first empty space (starting from the silver, then proceeding left to right, first on the top row, then the bottom).
* During rounds 5 and 6, Leon delivers to the last empty space (i.e. starting with the cured meat space, then proceeding to the wine, etc.)

Because Leon is not spending any goods it does not receive any immediate rewards for delivering to the Long Distance Trade board.

**Changes in scoring:** Leon will score 1 victory point for every marker it has more than you on the long distance trade board.

*Example: You have 2 markers on the Long Distance Trade board but Leon has 4. It scores 2 victory points during final scoring. You can still sell left-over goods, as described in the multiplayer rules for this module.*

### New Farm Cards

These cards can be added to the game without any additional rule changes (except the cards listed during setup, which need to be removed).

Like you, Leon is required to spend 2 or 3 silver to deliver to a market barrow space with such a requirement.

Like you, Leon cannot use a market barrow without a delivery icon to deliver to the market; it receives the shown additional victory point(s) instead.

### Trade Boards

**Changes in setup:** Give both yourself and Leon a random trade board, and set it up as described in the multiplayer rules of this module.

**Changes in gameplay:** You can deliver to Leon's trade board as described in the multiplayer rules of this module. When Leon would gain a modifier marker, simply remove it from play and give Leon 2 silver instead.

When Leon performs free deliveries during Step 3 of the Transportation Phase, check the 2nd tucked Burrobot card. If it shows a trade board icon on its bottom, then Leon makes its first delivery to your trade board instead. When able to pay both in silver and in goods for a delivery on a trade board, Leon prefers to pay in goods; place one of Leon's trade markers on the next space of your trade board. For this delivery Leon receives the uncovered victory points immediately, and you receive the modifier marker.

Leon will deliver to your trade board only once per round. If it does not have sufficient resources (or no icon is showing on the 2nd tucked Burrobot card), then Leon makes a delivery as normal.

## Player Aid Summary

### Card Play Procedure

For each card Leon gets to play, repeat this procedure from the start:

1. Draw a card from the main farm draw deck.
2. If Leon has an empty market barrow space on the top of its board, tuck it there.
3. Otherwise...
   * a. If Leon has more farm extensions than fields showing the same resource as the drawn card: tuck the drawn card on the left side of its board, to become a field.
   * b. If Leon has as many fields showing the same resource as the drawn card: tuck the drawn card on the right side of its board, to become a farm extension.

### Delivery Target Selection

If there is a market barrow or a craft building, where Leon has delivered at least once, but has not yet finished it, that is Leon's target. Otherwise:

If the tucked card shows a market barrow, it is going to deliver to the market barrows:

* Same value, or else higher/lower (circling around)
* If tied, pick left one
* If none, deliver to craft buildings instead

If the tucked card shows craft buildings, it is going to deliver to an open craft building:

* Matching numbered one, or else higher/lower (circling around)
* If there are any available buildings you have not fully supplied yet, Leon considers buildings you have fully supplied also not open.
* If none available, deliver to market barrow instead.

### Supplying a Craft Building

* Leon scores victory points equal to the current game round,
* An additional 1 victory point if it supplied this craft building before you,
* And another 1 victory point if this causes the opening of another craft building.
* If playing with Grand Markers: +1 victory point per grand marker on its board.

Leon receives the corresponding craft marker. Place it on its board, face down. Leon ignores the ability of the craft marker.

### Supplying a Market Barrow

* Leon places one marker in the market on the main board on a space matching the value of the card.
* If multiple options of the same value: more of your player markers adjacent, or else farthest from other player markers of its own, or else choose randomly.
* Return your adjacent, smaller valued markers, Leon scores 1 victory point for each.
* Leon gains one trade commodity, and victory points equal to the card's value.
* If playing with Grand Markers: place the grand marker if it would return your grand marker or 2+ regular markers, and score an additional 1 victory point for each of your markers returned.

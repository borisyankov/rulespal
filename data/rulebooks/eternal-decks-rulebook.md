## Story

Humanity has been plagued by a phenomenon in which their lifespans are shortening. In order to solve this mystery, the Explorers journey to the Eternal World. There they encounter mystical creatures known as the Eternals. Will the Explorers be able to solve the mystery of life and return to their original world?

## Game System

Eternal Decks is a cooperative game system where each Stage is a different game. Within each Stage, players will take turns playing cards to the Game Board in order to refill their decks and achieve the different objectives for each Stage.

### Game Overview

At the start of a game, each player has five "Number Cards", which represents the life energy flowing within them. On your turn, you must play at least one card before play proceeds clockwise to the next player. If you cannot play a card on your turn, the cycle of life will fail and everyone will lose the game.

To maintain the cycle of life, you need to acquire new cards from the mysterious "Eternals". Eternals can be found sleeping at the end of each row within the "Field" (the three rows in the middle of the Game Board). If a player manages to play a card at the end of a Field row, they will connect with the Eternals and "Revive" one of them. Only the player who Revives the Eternal will receive the eight cards from that Eternal.

Upon Revival, a "Curse" will be activated by the Eternal. For example, the Phoenix Curse means any card with the number 1 cannot be played to the Field.

To break the Curse of an Eternal, you must generate a "Jewel" and offer it to the Eternal. To generate Jewels, you must use one to three cards from your hand and play them to the "River" (the row at the very bottom of the Game Board).

Communicating with your teammates is an important part of the game. For that purpose, each player has two "Communication Discs". You may place the Discs wherever you want to play your cards next. By placing them, you can indirectly communicate the information of your hand to your teammates.

To keep the cycle of life going, you must make sure that all of your cards do not run out. Communicate and "Discuss" who should be the next player to get a new deck.

There are six "Stages", A/B/C/D/E/F. In Stage A, you win by gaining four "Stars". In the other Stages, you win by gaining four "Keys". The conditions for acquiring Keys will vary depending on the Stage.

Let the journey of Eternal Decks begin!

## The Secret of Life Energy

There are five energies (Number Card suits) that can be harnessed within the Eternal World. They are Passion, Calm, Harmony, Challenge, and Inspiration. There are also Rare Cards, Ability Cards, and Skull Cards. In Stage A, you will only need to worry about the Number, Rare, and Ability Cards.

### Cards That Are Playable On Your Turn

**Number Cards**

There are five colors (red, blue, green, yellow, purple) and nine numbers (1-9). Number Cards can be played to the Field or the River. They can also be used to generate Jewels.

The five suits are: Passion (red), Calm (blue), Harmony (green), Challenge (yellow), Inspiration (purple).

Number cards are the source of energy that supports the life activities of living organisms.

**Two-Color Cards**

These are Number Cards with two colors: "red/green" or "blue/yellow." When played to the Field, the card can be oriented in either direction. When generating Jewels, you choose which color you are using.

**Rare Cards**

Rare Cards are special Number Cards. They can be played as any of the five colors and/or any of the nine numbers. Rare Cards can be next to each other in the Field. When used to generated a Jewel, you can use more than one Rare Card.

Rare is a special being who watches over the natural world.

**Ability Cards**

When you play an Ability Card, the effect of that card is activated. Depending on the Ability Card it will be played to the Stage Card Area, Field, or Discard Area. (Ability Cards — see page 18.)

### Decks

A "Deck" refers to a set of cards that are combined together into one group. There are the Eternal Decks, Starting Decks, Random Deck, and Gatekeeper Deck. In Stage A, you will only need to worry about the Eternal and Starting Decks.

**Eternal Decks**

Each eight card deck is unique to the specific Eternal. The card backs feature the icon for that specific Eternal.

**Starting Decks**

Five cards, numbered 1-5, for each player.

### Anatomy of an Eternal Card

Eternal, Face Down:

- **Eternal, Face Down** — Letter - Number, Eternal Icon
- **Eternal Name** — English/Japanese
- **Eternal Curse** — Impacted location & Cursed number/color
- **Eternal Ability** — Ability information
- **Eternal Deck** — Cards in the Eternal's Deck

Eternal, Face Up: the revived Eternal's illustrated side, showing its deck contents and Curse.

## Taking A Turn

On your turn, you must perform one of the following three actions:

1. Play a Card
2. Generate a Jewel
3. Give a Card to a Teammate

### 1: Play a Card

#### 1-1: Play a card to the Field

- Choose the top, middle, or bottom row of the Field and play one card to the leftmost empty space of that row.
- You cannot play multiple cards to the Field during the same turn.
- Once a card is played it cannot be moved or rotated, until the row is cleared.
- There are placement rules when playing cards to the Field.

**Common Placement Rules** (applies to all rows)

- Cards of the same color cannot be played next to each other.
- Cards of the same number cannot be played next to each other.

**Field Card Placement Rules** (applies to a specific row)

- In addition to the common placement rules, there are specific rules for each Field Card.
- [MOUNTAIN] You must play cards in ascending order. (From lowest to highest number.)
- [CAVE] You must play cards in descending order. (From highest to lowest number.)
- [CAMP] There are no additional placement rules. Only the common placement rules apply.

**Curse of the Eternals**

After an Eternal is Revived, placement restrictions will be added to the Field. For example, after the Phoenix is Revived, cards with the number 1 cannot be played to the Field.

**Two-Color Cards**

- When playing Two-Color Cards, they may be rotated right-side up or upside down. They cannot be rotated after being played.
- A Two-Color Card may not be adjacent to a card of the same color on its left side when played to the Field. Additionally, a Two-Color Card may not be adjacent to a card of the same color on its right side when played to the Field.

**Rare Cards**

- When playing a Rare Card, you can ignore the placement restrictions of the "Common Placement Rules" and "Field Placement Rules".
- Please note that when the Witch's Curse is activated, "Rare Cards cannot be played to the Field".
- When playing a Rare Card, it is not considered to be any color or number. For example, if a Rare Card is played first on MOUNTAIN Field row, it does not have to be considered to be the number 1. As a result, you can ignore the placement restrictions of the Phoenix Curse that says "Number 1 cards cannot be played to the Field".
- After being played, Rare Cards can be considered to be any of the five colors or nine numbers (1-9). For example, if a Rare Card is played first on MOUNTAIN Field row, then the Rare Card is considered to be a 1 (at minimum, but can be higher) and the next card that can be played would be a number 2 or higher.
- [Special rule] You can play any number of Rare Cards to the right of the number 9 on the MOUNTAIN Field row.
- [Special rule] You can play any number of Rare Cards to the right of the number 1 on the CAVE Field row.

### Field Cards

Difficulty is shown from EASY (1 diamond) to HARD (5 diamonds).

**CAMP** (1 diamond) — There are no additional placement rules. Only the common placement rules apply.

**MOUNTAIN** (3 diamonds) — You must play cards in ascending order. (From lowest to highest number.)

**CAVE** (3 diamonds) — You must play cards in descending order. (From highest to lowest number.)

**FOREST** (2 diamonds) — You cannot play consecutive numbers. Ex: 3 or 5 cannot be played next to 4.

**LAKE** (2 diamonds) — You must play cards in the following order: red -> blue -> green -> yellow -> red... May start with any color. Purple can be a substitute for another color. Common Placement Rules: Purple cannot be adjacent to another purple.

**GRASSLAND** (2 diamonds) — You must play cards numbered 1/2/3/4/8/9. (5/6/7 cannot be played.)

**RIVERSIDE** (3 diamonds) — You must play cards numbered 1/3/5/7/9. (Odd numbers only.)

**VALLEY** (4 diamonds) — You must play cards numbered 2/4/6/8. (Even numbers only.)

**DESERT** (5 diamonds) — You must play cards with consecutive numbers. Ex: 1 or 3 must be played next to 2. 1 and 9 are not consecutive numbers.

**SWAMP** (5 diamonds) — You must play cards in the following order: red -> blue -> green -> yellow -> red... and you must play cards numbered 1/2/3/4/5. May start with any color. Purple can be a substitute for another color. Common Placement Rules: Purple cannot be adjacent to another purple.

If a Two-Color, Rare, or purple card is played first, no color has been determined yet.

Example: a red/green card is played first. At this point, the first played color has not been determined. Meaning, blue (which comes after red) or yellow (which comes after green) can be played. Yellow is played. From here, the cards must be played in the following order: red -> blue -> green -> yellow -> red.... In this example, a purple was played instead of a blue. A rare was played instead of green.

### Revival of the Eternals

As soon as someone plays a card in the space directly to the left of the Eternals, one Eternal in that row will be Revived. The player who played the last card in that row selects any one of the Eternals they want to Revive in that row. The selected Eternal is flipped face up and placed in the leftmost empty space of the Revival Area. The eight cards from the selected Eternal's deck are placed, face down, on the bottom of that player's Player Deck.

*You do not need to shuffle the eight cards before placing them under your Player Deck, since you shuffled them during Setup.*

Clear all played cards in the row by moving them to the Discard Area, face up. Finally, the Eternals that were not selected are moved to the rightmost spaces in that row, if needed.

*If you selected the leftmost Eternal, there is no need to move any Eternals to the right.*

[Hint] Placing the 9th Eternal in the Revival Area will gain you a Star (see page 17).

Example: The Yellow player plays the yellow 5. The played card is directly to the left of the Eternals, so one Eternal will be revived. The Yellow player selects the Mermaid. The Mermaid Eternal is flipped face up and placed on the leftmost empty space of the Revival Area. Since the Skeleton is already in the Revival Area, the Mermaid is placed to the right of the Skeleton. Next, the Mermaid's deck of eight cards is placed face down at the bottom of the Yellow player's Player Deck. The four cards in the row that were used to revive the Mermaid are moved together to the Discard Area, face up. Finally, since there is space between the Phoenix and the King, the Phoenix is moved over to the right.

### Curse of the Eternals

When an Eternal is Revived, a Curse will be activated that impacts all players. While a Curse is active, players will no longer be able to play the corresponding cards of that type (number, color, Rare) to the Field. Cursed cards can still be used to generate Jewels or be played to the River. To remove a Curse, you will need to perform the action 'Generate a Jewel' and give it to the Eternal (see page 15).

**Curse of the A Eternals**

- PHOENIX — Number 1 cards cannot be played to the Field.
- MERMAID — Number 4 cards cannot be played to the Field.
- KING — Number 6 cards cannot be played to the Field.
- FAIRY — Number 9 cards cannot be played to the Field.

**Curse of the B Eternals**

- GHOST — Number 5 & 7 cards cannot be played to the Field.
- SKELETON — Number 2 & 8 cards cannot be played to the Field.
- WITCH — Rare cards cannot be played to the Field.
- VAMPIRE — Purple cards cannot be played to the Field.

**Curse of the C Eternals**

- MEDUSA — Red & red/green cards cannot be played to the Field.
- MINOTAUR — Blue & blue/yellow cards cannot be played to the Field.
- KRAKEN — Green & red/green cards cannot be played to the Field.
- MUMMY — Yellow & blue/yellow cards cannot be played to the Field.

### Update the Field card within the row

The Field rows will not be updated in this Beginner Mode play of Stage A. Depending on the game mode, four Field cards may be placed randomly in one of the rows during setup. When you clear the row, during the 1st-3rd laps, the topmost Field card will also be moved to the Discard Area. The new Field card on that row is flipped face up and the new placement rules are now applied to that row.

*Game modes — page 22. Game mode Setup Table — Stage Description Rulebook, page 01.*

### 4th Lap Bonus

Eternals will be Revived on the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd lap. A Bonus will be gained on the 4th lap.

**Top Row: CAMP Bonus**

- The CAMP Bonus Card is immediately played to one of the other two rows in the Field.
- This changes the placement rules for the rest of that row to CAMP.
- Once you clear the row, the CAMP Bonus Card is moved to the Discard Area along with the other played cards.

Example: The Yellow player plays a yellow 9 to the top row. This clears the row for the 4th time and triggers the 4th Lap Bonus. The Yellow player immediately plays the CAMP Bonus Card to the middle row. From this point on (three spaces remaining), the placement rule for CAVE is changed to CAMP.

**Middle Row: Heart Recovery Bonus**

The 4th Lap Bonus for the middle row is to recover all spent Hearts (moving them from right to left). *Hearts — page 16.*

**Bottom Row: Gain a Star**

The 4th Lap Bonus for the bottom row is to gain a Star. *Stars — page 17.*

### Closing Rows

Once a Field row has been cleared four times, cards can no longer be played to that row. To indicate that the row is closed, the played cards are not cleared and the Field Card is placed face down in the Bonus Area.

*The Field Card in the middle row is moved to the Discard Area instead (otherwise it would cover the three Hearts).*

You have gained the 4th Lap Bonus, the CAMP Bonus card. The row is now closed. Keep the played cards in the row and place the Field Card in the Bonus Area, face down.

#### 1-2: Play a card to the River

There are no placement rules when playing a card to the River. Cards affected by a Curse can also be played to the River. For example, you can play a 6 to the River even if the King's Curse is active.

When the 5th card is played to the River, you immediately gain a River Bonus from the River Bonus Area.

- You add one River Rare Card to your hand as a River Bonus.
- If there are no River Rare Cards, you gain a Game Over Card and everyone immediately loses the game.
- After gaining the River Rare Card, move the five played cards to the Discard Area, face up.

Example: The Red player plays the red 9 to the River. The 5th card has been played to the River, so the Red player adds one River Rare Card to their hand. The five played cards are moved to the Discard Area, face up. The River is now empty.

Example: The Red player plays the red 9 to the River. The 5th card has been played to the River, but there are no River Rare Cards left. The Red player receives the Game Over Card and everyone immediately loses the game.

#### 1-3: Use an Ability Card

Most Eternal Decks include Ability Cards. When you play an Ability Card, play the card directly to the Discard Area and resolve the effect of that card. You may play an Ability Card into the River without activating its effect.

*The Ability Cards for the A Eternals (only during Stage A) are played to the Stage Card Area instead of the Discard Area (see page 18).*

*The Ability Card for the Minotaur will be played to the Field instead of the Discard Area.*

### Check the Discard Area

Checking the Discard Area is not prohibited, but it is recommended to limit your checks to the following:

- Any of the cards from a player's Starting Deck
- A specific card (before checking, announce the specific card you are looking for, such as a River Rare or the Phoenix Red 9)

*Optional advanced rule: Checking the Discard Area is prohibited at all times. Cards should be discarded face down.*

### 2: Generate a Jewel

You will use cards from your hand to generate Jewels and give them to the Eternals to remove their Curses.

- Cards affected by a Curse can still be used to generate Jewels.
- If there are no revived Eternals, you cannot generate a Jewel.
- If you have already given Jewels to all of the currently Revived Eternals, you cannot generate a Jewel.

**Jewel Recipes**

There are eight recipes for generating Jewels.

- Each recipe can only be generated once.
- You will gain both a Jewel and a Star for the "1/5/9 cards" recipe. *Stars — page 17.*

The eight recipes are:

1. Any two cards
2. Any three cards
3. Three cards of the same color
4. Three cards of different colors
5. One card with the number 1 or 9
6. Two cards of the same number
7. Three cards of the same number
8. One card each of the 1/5/9 cards

Notes:

- Two-Color Cards: Only use one color of your choice.
- Rare Cards: Can be any color and number. Two or more Rare Cards can be used in a single recipe.
- Ability Cards: Can be used in the recipe "Any two (three) Cards".

**Give Jewels to the Eternals**

- Take the Jewel matching the recipe you just completed and give it to any Eternal in the Revival Area that does not already have a Jewel.
- If there are multiple Revived Eternals without a Jewel, you can choose between any of them.
- Place the Jewel so that it covers the icon of the Eternal's Curse.

**Cards are played to the River**

- All cards used to generate a Jewel are played to the River.
- If there are five (or more) cards played to the River, you will gain the River Bonus as normal.
- It is possible to have more than five cards end up in the River after generating a Jewel. No matter how many cards are in the River, they are all moved to the Discard Area.

Example: The Blue player uses the cards "blue 5", "red/green 2", and "red/green 4", to generate a Jewel with the recipe: "Three cards of different colors". The Blue player can give the Jewel to the Skeleton or the King in the Revival Area. The Minotaur has a Jewel, therefore, it cannot be given another. They decide to give the Jewel to the Skeleton. This removes the Skeleton's Curse of "Number 2 & 8 cards cannot be played to the Field". There are now six cards in the River, so the Blue player adds one River Rare Card to their hand and moves the six played cards to the Discard Area.

### 3: Give a Card to a Teammate

Spend a Heart to give any card from your hand to any other player, face down. When spending a Heart, move a Heart token from left to right. A Heart to the the right of the arrow indicates it has been spent.

[Hint] The Skeleton Ability Card recovers 1 Heart (moves Heart from right to left).

[Hint] The 4th Lap Bonus in the middle row of the Field recovers all spent Hearts.

## Refilling Your Hand and Ending Your Turn

After performing an action, draw cards from your Player Deck until you have three cards in hand. If you already have three (or more) cards in hand, you will not draw any cards and you will not discard down. If you have no cards left in your Player Deck, you will not draw cards, leaving you with less than three cards in hand.

Play continues with the next player in clockwise order taking their turn.

Examples:

- The Red player has two cards in their hand after playing one card to the Field. They draw one from their Player Deck. They now have three cards in hand.
- The Blue player uses all three of their cards in hand to generate a Jewel. They only have two cards in their Player Deck. They draw them both, leaving their hand with only two cards and their Player Deck empty.
- The Green player uses a red 1 Number Card to generate a jewel. This was also the 5th card played to the River. The Green player adds a River Rare Card to their hand. This brings their hand to three cards again. So, they do not draw cards from their Player Deck.
- The Yellow player has four cards in their hand. They spend one Heart to give one card to the Red player. The Yellow player still has three cards in hand. So, they do not draw cards from their Player Deck.
- The Red player has one card in hand and no cards in their Player Deck. They play their card to the Field to Revive the Phoenix. Normally, they would put the Phoenix Deck at the bottom of their Player Deck, but their Deck was empty. So, they immediately make the Phoenix Deck their Player Deck and refill their hand with three cards.

## Losing and Winning

All players lose:

- If a player cannot take one of the three actions on their turn.
- If a player draws the Game Over Card from the River Bonus Area.

All players win:

- When the 4th Star has been gained.

If the win condition and the lose condition occur during the same player's turn, the win condition takes priority. Example: While playing Stage A, you gain the Game Over Card from the River Bonus Area at the same time that you Generate a Jewel (Recipe 1/5/9) to gain your fourth star.

## Gaining Stars

There are six Stars in Stage A. To win, you must gain four of them. When you gain a Star, place it in the area marked with the four Star icons in the Stage Card Area.

**Give Jewels to the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd Eternal in the Revival Area**

**Give Jewels to the 4th, 5th, and 6th Eternal in the Revival Area**

**Revive all Eternals** — You will gain a Star when you place the 9th Eternal in the Revival Area. *Jewels are not needed to gain this Star.*

**Generate a Jewel with the 1/5/9 recipe** — You will gain a Jewel and a Star. [Reminder] Rare Cards can be used as any number. You can also use multiple Rare Cards.

**Complete the 4th lap on the bottom row** — You will gain a Star as a 4th Lap Bonus.

**Play the three A Eternal Ability Cards** — Once you play the 3rd A Eternal Ability Card to the Stage Card Area, you will gain a Star. *This Star can only be earned during Stage A.*

## Ability Cards

**A ETERNALS** — In Stage A only, Ability Cards are played to the Stage Card Area and you gain a Star after the 3rd Ability Card is played. In other Stages, no Star can be gained. Instead, you gain a Star Bonus each time an A Eternal Ability Card is played.

**GHOST** — Reveal all cards in player's hands. Players can discuss them until the end of the current turn. Return revealed cards back to player's hands.

**SKELETON** — Recover 1 spent Heart (move from right to left). If all 3 Hearts are unspent (left), playing the Ability Card will do nothing.

**WITCH** — Generates any one Jewel without using Number/Rare Cards. Cannot be used for the 1/5/9 recipe.

**VAMPIRE** — No Ability Card.

**MEDUSA** — If there are any Rare Cards are in the Discard Area, put one back in the River Bonus Area.

- *You may always check the Discard Area before using an Ability Card.*
- *The Rare Card does not have to be a River Rare Card. For example, you can use a Ghost Rare Card.*
- *In Pro Mode, you may also place any Purple card into the River Bonus Area instead of a Rare Card.*

**MINOTAUR** — Exchange this card with any card in the Field. The Minotaur card is treated as a Rare Card. Cannot be exchanged for the "CAMP" Bonus Card gained from the 4th Lap Bonus.

**KRAKEN** — Add any two cards currently in the River to your hand. *If there is only one card in the River, you can only add one to your hand.*

**MUMMY** — Draw three cards from any Player Deck and add them to your hand.

- *After drawing and looking at the 1st card, you can choose to draw the 2nd card from the same or different deck. Same option for the 3rd card.*
- *As long as there are cards in a player's deck, you must draw a total of three cards. If there are fewer than three cards among all players' decks, only draw the number of cards remaining.*

## Verbal Discussion

It is prohibited to directly talk about the information of any Number Cards in your hand, but it is recommended to talk indirectly.

**(X) You cannot specifically talk about the "number" or "color" of cards in your hand.** You cannot say something like "I have a number 1 in my hand" or "I have a yellow card".

**(Check) You can talk about information other than Number Cards in your hand.**

- You can talk about the Rare Cards or Ability Cards in your hand.

Allowed (Check):

## Communication Discs

You can freely place or return discs at any time to show your intentions. Want to make the game harder? Try playing with no Verbal Discussion, just Communication Discs!

Place Communication Discs on spaces to indirectly communicate to your teammates where you think you can play the cards in your hand.

[Note] Communication Discs can be used at any time. This means you do not have to wait until your turn to use them, they do not have a limit on the amount of times they can be used, etc. Players may move their own discs as many times as they like, as well as wherever and however they like.

Examples:

- The Red player's initial hand was red 1, 3, and 5. They placed both of their discs on spaces where they think they can play the red 1 and red 5.
- The Blue player only has the blue 1 in their hand. They place both discs on the same space to show their strong desire to gain a Deck.
- The Green player places two discs on the "LAKE" Field row to show that they have green and yellow cards. Because they have cards from the Phoenix deck in hand, we can assume that they are green 3 and yellow 6.
- The Yellow player only has a yellow 8. During their turn, they want to play the yellow 8 to the River to gain a Rare Card. Then on their next turn, they want to play the Rare Card on the final space of the "LAKE" Field row to gain a Deck.

(1) (2) (3) (4) If you can play the cards in this order, you will be successful.

### Stage C — Labyrinth

**Escape from the Labyrinth**

*With the knowledge of how Keys work, you now find yourself lost in a Labyrinth. Enlist the help of the Labyrinth Eternals to solve the puzzles inside and find the Keys before you are trapped forever.*

**Setup**

4. **1 LABYRINTH Field Card** — Place the LABYRINTH Field Card face up in the top row of the Field Card Area. All four laps will be the LABYRINTH.

Shuffle the four Level 3 cards face down and place one on top of the Level 4 card. In the same way, place one Level 2 card on top of the Level 3 card and one Level 1 card on top of the Level 2 card. Using the same above steps, create another stack of four cards. Arrange both stacks of Puzzle Cards on the Puzzle Area Card. Turn both Level 1 Puzzle Cards face up. Be careful not to rotate the puzzle cards upside down. They must be solved in the exact (right-side up) orientation as shown on the card!
6. **3 C Eternals and their Decks** — Prepare three random C Eternals in the top row of the Field.

*Setup the Jewels, Stars, Hearts, 4th Lap Bonuses, Starting Decks, and Communication Discs as normal. Setup Field Cards and other Eternals according to the Difficulty Mode — pages 01-02.*

**How to gain the four Keys**

To win, you need to complete four puzzles. To complete a puzzle, you need to play cards to the different Field rows in such a way that matches the Puzzle Card exactly.

- Puzzle Cards cannot be viewed in any other orientation. The diamond(s) showing the puzzle level should be in the top left corner.
- When playing Two-Color Cards, you choose which color is being used for a puzzle, regardless of the card's orientation in the Field.
- Rare Cards, the Minotaur Ability Card, and the CAMP 4th Lap Bonus Card can be used as any color in a puzzle. You can even complete the puzzle by replacing the Minotaur Ability Card with a card that was already played incorrectly.
- You can only declare a puzzle is completed the moment after you play the final card that completes the puzzle. If you realize it is completed later, it will not count. Make sure everyone does not miss the completion of a puzzle!
- If an Eternal would be Revived at the same time a puzzle is completed, complete the puzzle first.
- When a puzzle is completed, move the Key, corresponding to the puzzle level, on the Puzzle Level Card to the Key Acquisition Area.
- Move both the completed Puzzle Card and the other Puzzle Card of the same level to the Discard Area. You do not need to complete both puzzles of the same level!
- Flip the two Puzzle Cards of the next level face up.

Example: The Red player plays a red 1 to the bottom row in the Field. This causes them to complete the Level 1 puzzle, which required playing three red cards in the same column.

When using the D Eternals (Skull Cards), simply playing the Skull Card to the Field does not count as completing the puzzle. You must play a Number or Rare Card over the Skull Card for it to count.

Example: The Blue player used the Minotaur's Ability Card to replace the green 7 card that was already played to the bottom row in the Field. The Minotaur's Ability Card can be any color. The Blue player says it is red, which causes them to complete the Level 4 puzzle and win the stage!

**Labyrinth Field Card**

**LABYRINTH** (4 diamonds) — Numbers must be symmetrically opposite. Colors do not need to be symmetrical. If the amount of cards played (in the row during that lap) is even, the two middle cards will have the same number. Cards of the same color cannot be adjacent.

In the example row: the first and last card are the same; the second and second to last card are the same; the third and third to last card are the same.

### Stage D — Gate Keeper

**Fight the Gatekeeper**

*After escaping the Labyrinth, you approach a large gate. An even larger figure blocks your path. Work with the Positive Eternals to defeat The Gatekeeper.*

**Setup** (GK = Gatekeeper)

In Stage D, the GK takes a turn after the last player takes their turn (see page 12).

1. **3 Stage Cards** — Place the Star Stage Card and the two Stage D cards (GK HP and GK Deck) as shown above.
2. **4 White Star Skull Cards** — Place the four White Star Skull Cards on top of the Star Stage Card.
3. **2 GK Discs** — Place one on the 20 HP space of the GK HP Card. Place the other on the 1st space in the top row of the Field.
4. **4 Keys** — Place the four Keys on the 15/10/5/0 HP spaces of the GK HP Card.
5. **1 Mountain Field Card** — Place the MOUNTAIN Field Card in the top row of the Field Card Area. All four laps will be MOUNTAIN.
6. **16 GK Cards, 1 Game Over Card** — To prepare the GK Deck, follow these steps:
   - Place a Game Over Card (different from the one in the River) on top of the GK Deck Area.
   - Shuffle the GK 2nd Half Deck (purple card back) face down and place it on top of the Game Over Card.
   - Shuffle the GK 1st Half Deck (white card back) face down and place it on top of the 2nd Half Deck.
   - *For 3-player games, all eight cards are used in the 1st Half Deck.*
   - *For 2-player and 4-player games, only six cards are used (remove the two 3-player only cards).*
7. **3 D Eternals and their Decks** — Prepare three random D Eternals in the top row of the Field.

The starting player is the red player. If there is no red player, then the blue player starts. If there is no blue player, then the green player starts.

*Setup the Jewels, Stars, Hearts, 4th Lap Bonuses, Starting Decks, and Communication Discs as normal. Setup Field Cards and other Eternals according to the Difficulty Mode — pages 01-02.*

**How to gain the four Keys**

To win, you need to reduce the Gatekeeper's hit points (HP) to 0.

- By playing a Number Card on top of a Skull Card of the matching color, you can deal damage to the GK equal to that number.
- You will gain a Key from the GK for every 5/10/15/20 damage you deal.
- After gaining a Key, the GK's HP will return to 20. Any remaining damage is ignored when the HP resets.

If you play two cards in succession, due to the effect of the Pegasus Curse, resolve the damage from the first card before resolving the damage from the second card.

Example: The GK's HP is currently at 20. The Red player plays a red 9 on the red Skull Card. This deals 9 damage to the GK, which would bring the GK's HP to 11. The Red player moves the HP disc 9 spaces. However, after 5 damage the disc reaches a Key. The Red player places the Key in the Key Acquisition Area. Because a Key was gained, the GK's HP disc returns to 20. The 4 remaining damage that was done is ignored.

Rare Cards can be played on top of a Skull Card, but it will not do any damage to the GK.

Skull Cards are a new card type that appear in Stage D. There are five colors of Skull Cards (red, blue, green, yellow, and purple). They can be found in the GK's Deck and D Eternal Decks. Skull Cards are played to the Field (see page 11).

**D Eternals**

Each D Eternal has three Skull Cards within their Deck. Each D Eternal Curse has a positive impact on the players while it is active.

**PEGASUS** — On your turn, you may play one or two cards to any Field row and/or the River. *Two in Field / Two in River / One in Field, one in River.* *If you play two cards and gain a Bonus or Revive an Eternal, do so after playing both cards.* *After playing a fifth card in the River, you cannot play a sixth card.*

**BARONG** — When playing cards to the Field, up to two cards of the same color can be adjacent. *The Common Placement Rule "cards of the same number cannot be adjacent" applies.* *Field Card Placement rules must be followed.* *This applies to all three rows and can happen multiple times within a single row.*

MOUNTAIN Example: red 3 -> red 4 -> blue 5 -> green 7 -> green 9. *The Common Placement Rule for LAKE and SWAMP is that "Purple cards cannot be adjacent", but BARONG allows for up to two Purple cards to be adjacent.*

**JINN** — At the end of your turn, refill your hand with cards from your Player Deck so that you have four cards (instead of three).

**KYUBI** — When playing cards to the Field, up to two cards of the same number can be adjacent. *The Common Placement Rule "cards of the same color cannot be adjacent" applies.* *Field Card Placement rules must be followed.* *This applies to all three rows and can happen multiple times within a single row.*

RIVERSIDE Example: red 3 -> yellow 3 -> blue 9 -> red 5 -> blue 5

**Playing a Skull Card from your hand**

- Skull Cards in your hand are played to the Field just like Number Cards.
- Skull Cards cannot be adjacent to the same color Skull Card or Number Card.
- Skull Cards are subject to the restrictions of an Eternal's Curse. For example, you cannot play a green Skull Card if the Kraken curse is active.
- Skull Cards can be played to the right of a Skull Card that is already in play.
- It is possible to have multiple Skull Cards in a row.

*You can place Skull Cards to the right of the number 9 in MOUNTAIN and to the right of the number 1 in CAVE.*

**Playing a Number Card on top of a Skull Card**

- Number Cards can only be played on top of a Skull Card of the same color.
- Two-Color Cards can be played on top of a Skull Card of either color.
- Rare Cards can be played on top of a Skull Card of any color.
- You can play the CAMP 4th Lap Bonus Card on top of a Skull Card. It will deal 0 damage to the GK.
- When there are multiple Skull Cards in a single Field row, you must play on top of them in order from left to right.
- Number and Rare Cards cannot be played to the right of a Skull Card. Meaning, you must play Number and/or Rare Cards on top of all Skull Cards in a row before playing more Number or Rare Cards in that row.

**Additional notes about Skull Cards**

- Skull Cards cannot be played to the River.
- Skull Cards cannot be used to generate Jewels.
- If all of the cards in your hand are Skull Cards, you will lose the game if you cannot play one of them to the field. However, you can spend a Heart to give a Skull Card to another player.
- You can talk about having a Skull Card in your hand.

The Minotaur Ability Card can be exchanges with Skull Cards. If there is a Number Card on top of a Skull Card, you must take the Number Card instead. The bottom Skull Card will remain in place and the Minotaur Ability Card will go on top of the Skull Card.

**Star Bonus - White Skull Cards**

When you earn a Star, you add one White Skull Card from the Star Stage Card to your hand. There are only four White Skull Cards. After gaining the last White Skull Card, there are no additional Star Bonuses. (Can be used on Skull Cards located anywhere on the Field.)

White Skull Cards can only be played on top of colored Skull Cards that have already been played to the Field. You can play any colored Number Card on top of a White Skull Card.

**Gatekeeper's Turn**

After each player takes a turn, the GK takes their turn. Flip the top card of the GK Deck face up.

If it is a Skull Card:

- Play it to the leftmost empty space on the top row of the Field. Follow the same placement and Curse rules as if you were playing the Skull Card from your hand.
- If it cannot be played to the top row, play it to the middle row.
- If it cannot be played to the middle row, play it to the bottom row.
- If it cannot be played to the bottom row, or because there is an active Curse that is stopping it from being played to any row, move it to the Discard Area.

If it is an Ability Card:

- Move it to the Discard Area after activating its effect. Once the GK has played one card, their turn ends.

In a 2-player game, the GK plays after both players take two turns. After each player's first turn, the top card of the GK Deck is tilted 90 degrees. After each player's second turn, the GK plays a card.

The GK Deck is comprised of sixteen cards (fourteen in 2-player and 4-player games). After the last card from the Deck is played, the next turn will require the Game Over Card to be played and everyone loses the game.

There are two GK Discs. One is used to track the GK's HP. The other is placed on the Field as a helpful reminder of where the next Skull Card from the GK Deck will be played to. Usually, it is placed to the right of the most recently played card in the top row of the Field.

Example GK Turn: The card on top of the GK deck is flipped face up and it is a blue Skull Card. There is already a blue 2 in the top row of the Field. It cannot be played there since they are the same color. The Skull Card is moved to the middle row, but there is a blue 9, so the Skull Card cannot go there either. The Skull Card is moved to the bottom row, but the Field Card there is LAKE. A yellow 3 was played first in that row. This means the next card needs to be red and the Skull Card is blue. Therefore, the Skull Card cannot be played in the bottom row. The Skull Card is moved to the Discard Area. This ends the GK's turn.

If the Baron's Curse is active, up to two cards of the same color can be adjacent in the same row. In that case, the blue Skull Card would have been able to be played in the top row of the Field. (Even if Baron's Curse is removed later, a blue card can still be played on top of the blue Skull Card.)

**Gatekeeper Ability Card**

After the GK activates an Ability Card, move it to the Discard Area.

**Generate "Two/Three cards of the same number" Jewel** — If there is no Jewel on the matching "XX" or "XXX" space, the card does nothing. If the GK generates a Jewel, the GK must give it to an Eternal in the Revival Area. The leftmost Eternal in the Revival Area, without a Jewel, will have priority for getting the Jewel. **How to gain the four Keys**

To win, all players must complete the required number of tasks.

4 players: Each player is dealt three Task Cards. After you complete a task, place the Task Card face up on the left side of the Task Area Card. Then, move the Key matching your color one space to the right. After you complete a second task, place the Task Card on the Task Area Card and move your Key to the right again. At this point, it will be in the Key Acquisition Area, which is the goal. The remaining Task Card you have will not be used, so place it face up in the Discard Area.

3 players: Each player is dealt three Task Cards. The remaining General Task Cards become the General Task Deck. Completing a task works the same as a 4-player game. However, once your Key reaches the Key Acquisition Area, do not discard your final Task Card. Instead, keep it and draw one additional Task Card from the General Task Deck. Each player may only ever draw one card from the Task Deck. When you complete the next task, advance the Key of the color that was not used. Once that Key reaches the Key Acquisition Area, the Task Cards of the players who have already completed both of their tasks can no longer be used and are placed face up in the Discard Area.

2 players: Works the same as a 3-player game with one exception. Each time you complete a Task Card, you draw one card from the General Task Deck. When the Deck runs out, you can no longer gain Task Cards.

Example: The Red player has completed two tasks. The Blue player has completed one task. The Green player has not completed any tasks yet. The Yellow player has not completed any tasks yet.

Example: A 3-player game with Red, Blue, and Green as the player colors. The Red player has already completed two tasks. The Red player then completes one more task. The unused Yellow Key has been moved one space to the right.

**Silent**

Anyone can play the first two cards.* *All three cards must be in the same column.* *All three cards must be the same color.* *You can substitute cards with Rares, Minotaur, etc.*

**Play a Rare Card** — After you play a Rare Card to the right of another Rare Card already in the Field (immediately adjacent and in the same row), you will complete this task. *Cannot be completed by playing the Minotaur or Camp Bonus Cards.*

### Stage D+ — Destiny

*After encountering the Gatekeeper a third time, you learn of its' true identity - a former Explorer who visited the Eternal World! Let's evoke human emotions by colliding with the emotional energy of an Explorer.*

**Finisher**

There are no changes to the win condition for clearing Stage D+. You still need reduce the GK's hit points (HP) to 0. When setting up Stage D+, place one Key on 10 HP, another on 5 HP, and the last two on the 0 HP space.

- In a 2-player game, each player is responsible for the two colors they started the Stage with.

**Gatekeeper Counter**

When it's time to gain a Key, the Finisher should try to deal damage so the GK Disc lands directly on the Key. For every point of damage the GK Disc exceeds past the Key, the Finisher must discard one card from their deck, face up. Be sure to reset the GK Disc back to 20 HP after.

Example: The GK Disc is exactly 3 damage away from gaining a Key. The Finisher deals 5 damage, so they must discard 2 cards from their deck.

If a player with a different color than the next Key deals the finishing damage, they will not gain the Key and instead will place the GK Disc one space short of the Key. This means the Finisher is required to deal one damage next time.

Example: The GK Disc is 3 HP away from the red Key. The Yellow player deals 4 damage. Since they are not the Red player the GK Disc is placed 1 HP away from the red Key and the Yellow player does not discard any cards.

When gaining the last Key, the Finisher must deal exact damage to clear the Stage. They cannot exceed past the Key. If they do, they will place the GK Disc one space short of the Key. This means the Finisher is required to deal exactly one damage next time to win.

2. Move two cards in the Field to the Discard Area. Same effect as the Cyclops Curse.
3. Move 1 River Rare Card in the River Bonus Area to the Discard Area. Same effect as the Kappa Curse.
4. Return a Jewel from a Revived Eternal in the Revival Area. Same effect as the Golem Curse except the Jewel is returned to the box instead.

When setting up, each player should place their Starting Task Card face up in front of themselves. Then, place a Communication Disc so that it covers the three stars. This disc cannot be used throughout the Stage. Your Starting Task Card can only be completed when the 4th Star is gained.

There is a change to the win condition for clearing Stage E+. To win, you must do the following in order:

1. Advance all four Keys to the Key Acquisition Area.
2. Gain the 4th Star and everyone reveals their hand. At this point, all players must be able to complete their Starting Task Card.

- You may use a Two-Color Card to complete the Starting Task.
- Rares may not be used.

If you gain the 4th Star before advancing all four Keys to the Key Acquisition Area, the cycle of life will fail and everyone will immediately lose the game.

If you gain the 4th Star and one or more players cannot complete their Starting Task Card, the cycle of life will fail and everyone will immediately lose the game.

If you can clear Stage E+, you will have obtained something important that cannot be seen with the naked eye.

## Solo Play — 3 Golems

There are two modes for Solo Play: Basic Mode and Story Mode. In Basic Mode, you will play a single Stage. In Story Mode, you will play multiple Stages consecutively.

### Basic rules for Solo Play

In Solo Play, you will control three Golems by yourself. The game will play like a 3-player game with one player. If you have any questions about a 3-player game, please refer to the basic rules.

When setting up a Stage, prepare the 3-player Jewel Card in the Jewel Area.

Then, prepare the three Golems (Alpha, Beta, and Gamma). Of the three Golems, choose one as your Primary Golem (PG) and the other two as your Secondary Golems (SG). Select a player color for each Golem and prepare their Starting Deck based on the selected color. The PG will have a three card hand, while the SGs will only have a two card hand. The PG is the starting player, with turns going clockwise after that. The PG refills its hand until it has three cards, as usual, but the SGs refill their hands until they have two cards.

You can check the cards of all three Golems by laying them face up on the table.

### Changes to rules for Communication Discs and some Icons

When setting up a Stage, prepare two Communication Discs for each Golem. In Solo Play, there are extreme changes to the way Communication Discs are used.

- They are no longer used for communication. Instead, when a Golem uses a disc, the disc is returned to the game box and that Golem adds a card from its Player Deck to its hand.
- Each disc can only be used once per Stage. So, each Golem may only do this action twice.
- Each Golem can use their two discs at any time as a free action. For example, the PG uses one of their discs during an SG's turn. The PG draws one card from their deck into their hand and their disc is returned to the box.
- A Golem may not use another Golem's disc.

The "reveal hands" icon, which appears on the Ghost Ability Card and in Stage E, now means "draw one card from each Golem's deck and add it to their hand" instead of "reveal all cards in player's hands."

The Task icon, which appears in Stage E, now means "when you draw a card from the General Task Deck, turn the entire deck face up and pick any Task Card of your choice" instead of "all players will reveal their Task Cards." *All Task Cards held by each Golem in Stage E are revealed before the game is played.*

### Story Mode

Starting on the next page, we will introduce the Story Mode in which you play through successive stages while customizing your Golems. Please make a copy of the record sheet on the next page and use it while writing with a pen or pencil. The record sheet can also be downloaded from the QR code.

### Solo Play Record Paper — Story Mode "The Alchemist and the Cat"

The record paper tracks Stages A, B, C, D, and E. For each Stage you record Stars, Hearts, Rares, and Game Over (-1) results, scoring up to 8 points per Stage, and you convert unused Star Points to Victory Points (up to 15) for a maximum score of 100.

**Golem Alpha** — Disc abilities and upgrades:

- You can store cards in other Golems

- Red cards allow you to specify any number when generating Jewels
- When you have four cards in your hand, you can generate any of the six Jewels *Must spend all four cards, but can generate any Jewel*

**Golem Beta** — Disc abilities and upgrades:

- Draw two cards from your deck, add to your hand
- Give a card from your hand to another Golem
- Give any number of cards to one Golem instead
- Play a card ignoring all Curses
- Add a card from the River to your hand
- Give one disc to a Golem (2 discs required)
- Green card 1/5/9 can be given to a Golem without using a Heart

### Solo Play Story Mode "The Alchemist and the Cat"

Story Mode is a mode in which you play Stages A, B, C, D, and E in succession.

**A story about an Alchemist and her beloved cat researching Jewels.**

The Alchemist is controlling three Golems and researching the Jewels of the Eternal World.

In addition to the usual clearing conditions, every Stage requires all six Jewels on the three-player Jewel Card to be generated. For example, in Stage A, you cannot clear the Stage by simply gaining four Stars. You must also generate all six Jewels and give them to six Eternals to clear the Stage.

Each time you clear a Stage, you can strengthen the Golem's disc ability according to the number of stars you gained. In stage E, you must generate a special golem jewel and dedicate it to the Eternals. When you clear up to stage E, your performance will be evaluated on a scale of 100 points. Aim for 60 points or more.

In story mode, the Witch Eternal (B-3) will not be used. Return all cards from the Witch's Deck, except the Ability Card, back to the box. The Witch's Ability Card (referred to as the "Beloved Cat Card") will appear in Stage B and onward.

**Golem Alpha, Beta, Gamma**

The player colors of the Golems are red for Alpha, green for Beta, and yellow for Gamma. There is no distinction between primary and secondary Golems. Golems replenish their hand so that they have three cards at the end of their turn. Alpha is the starting player, followed by Beta and Gamma, who take turns in clockwise order.

During set up, place both Communication Discs on top of the Golem cards matching their color. Discs have different abilities for each Golem. The timing of using a disc differs from that of the Solo Basic Mode. It must be used during the turn of the Golem that owns it. It can be done before or after an action, and two discs can be used consecutively. However, it must be done before replenishing your hand at the end of your turn. Move the disc off the Golem after using it. Each disc can only be used once per Stage.

**Scoring and Disc Enhancements**

After clearing a Stage, you will calculate your score.

First, for each Star you gained during the Stage, draw on the star-shaped dotted lines (do not fill in) and record that same number in the top square to the right of that. You earn 1 point for each Star. Stage A will always be 4 points. If you earn 5+ Stars in other Stages, you will only get a maximum of 4 points.

You earn 1 point for each unspent Heart (max. 2 points). Be sure to spend your Hearts actively, but wisely.

You earn 1 point for each unused Rare Card (max. 2 points). Rare Cards in the River and in your hand count, but Rare Cards in your deck do not count.

You can earn a maximum total of 8 points for Stars, Hearts, and Rares. The maximum is 40 points for five Stages.

You can allocate gained Stars to a Golem to strengthen their disc abilities. Each time you fill in a previously drawn Star, you also fill in a solid diamond of any Golem. Filling in a diamond gives you access to ability written to the right of it. If there are two diamonds, you must fill in both before you can use the ability. You can strengthen discs at any time, while playing a Stage or in between Stages, so consider which discs to strengthen when you're in a pinch. *Prerequisites must be filled in first. For example, "Draw two cards instead of one" must be filled in before you can fill in "Draw from the Random Deck."*

After clearing Stage E, you earn 1 point for each gained Star that was not filled in. Up to 15 points.

Example: You have filled in one Star point and filled in the diamond for Golem Alpha's "Draw two cards instead of one." This allows you to draw two cards from your deck and add them to your hand when you use the red disc. With two more Star points, you can choose to draw a card from the Random Deck instead of your deck. Meaning you can take one card from each deck or two cards from the Random Deck.

- *When setting up the Stage, prepare the Random Deck by shuffling all cards together and placing it near the main game board.*
- *Each Golem has a diamond that is filled in black from the beginning. These abilities can be used from the very beginning.*

If you lose a Stage once, a "-1 point" will be recorded in the Game Over check box. For example: You clear the Stage on your second try. Your score for Stars, Hearts, and Rares is 6 points. Then, you deduct -1 point. Your score for that Stage will be 5 points.

If you lose the same Stage twice, a score of 0 will be recorded and you will be forced to move on to the next Stage. In this case, the calculation of negative points will not be relevant, and the Stage will be treated as "0 points" out of a maximum of 8 points.

*If you lost a Stage and spent any Star points to strengthen a Golem's disc abilities, the Star points will stay spent and you will keep the upgrades.*

**Golem Modification**

Each Golem has access to a new fourth action, "Store a card." Each Golem can only perform this action once per Stage. To store a card, place one one Number Card or Rare Card from the Golem's hand under the Golem Card. All cards stored must be the same number or be a Rare Card. The number of the stored card is written down on the record sheet. Each stored card is worth 2 points. Storing a Rare Card in Stage C/D earns an additional 3/2 points (total of 5/4 points). Each Golem is worth a maximum of 15 points, with a total of 45 points for all three Golems.

Stored cards will stay under the Golem Card. This means some Eternals will have less than eight cards in their deck in future Stages.

Storing cards in Stages A and B allows you to unlock new abilities for your Golem. Choose one of three abilities you want to unlock and fill in the hexagon. You can unlock up to two abilities. You can unlock an ability at any time, while playing a Stage or in between Stages. *Abilities that specify the use of red, green, or yellow cards cannot be replaced with Two-Color Cards.*

Storing a card in Stage E allows you to immediately place a Jewel on the Golem Card (take from the extra supply and not from the Jewel Card). This special Jewel can be given to an Eternal by having the Golem take the "Generate a Jewel" action. Giving this Jewel to an Eternal is an additional condition for clearing Stage E.

**Changes to Stages B, C, and D**

In Stage B, your cat is lost in the Ghost Town. When setting up the Stage, prepare only the Beloved Cat Card (Witch's Ability Card) in the top row of the Field. Return the Witch Eternal and the other 7 cards from their deck back to the box. Place a key on the Beloved Cat Card. You must Revive the three Eternals before you can gain your Beloved Cat and the Key it is holding. If you have not generated the six Jewels by the time you have gain all four Keys, move your Beloved Cat to the Stage B Card, just like when the B Eternals are Revived. Your Beloved Cat has no ability and no Curse.

You can only use your Beloved Cat once between all three Stages C-E, so choose very wisely when to use it. Once used, return it to the box. When preparing for the Stage, place the Jewel that is normally used on the Stage B Card on top of the Beloved Cat and place it near the Golems. The Beloved Cat Jewel can be given to an Eternal by having any Golem take the "Generate a Jewel" action on their turn.

In Stage C Expert Mode, normally you prepare the C, A, and E Eternals. However, instead of the E Eternals, you prepare the B Eternals.

In Stage D, if the GK's LP is reduced to 0 (you have gained 4 keys), but the 6 jewels are not yet gained, the GK will not take any more turns.

**Stage E Changes and Ending**

No Starting Tasks are used. Each Golem prepares two Tasks. Set up the game in the same way as for 3 players. If you have unlocked the "Add one blue disc" option for Golem Beta, place the blue disc on the Stage E Card along with the other discs.

In Story Mode, Stage E will not be cleared even if you advance four Key to the Key Acquisition Area. In addition, you will clear the Stage by offering Jewels to the nine Eternals. To do this, you will need to generate the six Jewels on the Jewel Card, as well as the Golem Jewels you create by storing cards and the Beloved Cat Jewel.

*If you have already given the 3 Golem Jewels and 1 Beloved Cat Jewel to the Eternals, you can give the last Jewel from the Jewel Area to your Beloved Cat. Otherwise, you would not be able to clear the Stage (the 6th Jewel could never be gained since you have already given Jewels to all 9 Eternals). You cannot give a Jewel to your Beloved Cat in any other way.*

*ENDING*

*The Alchemist has succeeded in unraveling the mystery of the Jewel. In the future, it may be possible to create Jewels in the Eternal World without the help of the Golem. My final mission is to send at least the results of this research back to our world. I hope that one day future researchers will unravel the mystery of human lifespan.*

After completing the five stages, calculate and record your score out of 100.

- Below 60 points: The mystery of unravelling the Jewel only deepens.
- 60-69 points: Yes! I've finally succeeded in unravelling the Jewel.
- 70-79 points: My beloved cat was so happy that he started wearing my hat!
- 80-89 points: I'm in high spirits. Maybe I can now solve the mystery of 1/5/9 recipes.
- 90 points or more: I've not only discovered the Jewel, but I've also come closer to unravelling the Eternal World itself. *Get creative with your Stage!*

## Creative Mode — More ways to enjoy

Creative Mode adds the ability to complete achievements and earn titles, making Eternal Decks even more fun.

Expand the ways you can have fun with Eternal Decks in Creative Mode. Please make a copy of the record sheet on the next page and use it while writing with a pen or pencil. The record sheet can also be downloaded from the QR code.

### Completing Achievements

After completing an achievement, check the box next to it to show it is complete. There are easy achievements, such as "Eternal Decks you've acquired" and "Jewel recipes you've used." There are also difficult achievements, such as "Have a maximum hand size of nine cards." Aim to complete all achievements.

### Earn Titles and Customize Your Stage

There are two types of titles: "Titles achieved by everyone" and "Titles achieved by individuals."

By aiming for a "Title achieved by everyone", you can enjoy customizing your Stages. For example, if you are aiming for the "All for One" title in Stage A, the same player must earn all of the Stars. Furthermore, if you are aiming for the "All Star" title, you must earn all six Stars, instead of four.

"Individual Titles" are earned by adding to or restricting the player's personal abilities. For example, if there is only one player who is a Beginner, that player will replenish their hand so that they have four cards at the end of their turn. Conversely, if there is only one player who is an Expert, that player will not be able to participate in conversations and will try to express their intentions only with their Communication Discs.

### Create Your Own Stages

In Eternal Decks, you can customize your game by combining Stages, Eternals, Field Cards, and Titles. Create your own Stages and publish them on websites and other platforms. Players can discover new ways to enjoy Eternal Decks together. We may officially release Stages that you have created, with you credited. Be the first to discover the depths of Eternal Decks.

# Pokémon Trading Card Game Rulebook

## Table of Contents

*The Pokémon Trading Card Game lets two players become Pokémon Trainers, sending their Pokémon into battle to knock out the opponent's. Build a 60-card deck of Pokémon, Energy, and Trainer cards, then attack, evolve, and outlast your rival. Take all six of your Prize cards — by Knocking Out enough of their Pokémon — and you win. This rulebook covers the standard rules of a two-player game.*

## Object of the Game

Be the first to achieve any one of these three victory conditions:
- Take all of your **Prize cards** (by Knocking Out your opponent's Pokémon).
- **Knock Out all** of your opponent's Pokémon in play.
- Your opponent **cannot draw a card** at the start of their turn (their deck is empty).

## The Deck

A deck is **exactly 60 cards**, containing three types of cards, with no more than **4 copies** of any card sharing the same name (basic Energy cards are exempt — you may include any number):

- **Pokémon cards:** your fighters. **Basic Pokémon** can be played straight from your hand; **Evolution Pokémon** (Stage 1, Stage 2) are played on top of a Pokémon to evolve it.
- **Energy cards:** attached to Pokémon to power their attacks. **Basic Energy** provides one unit of a type (Grass, Fire, Water, Lightning, Psychic, Fighting, Darkness, Metal, Fairy). **Special Energy** has extra effects.
- **Trainer cards:** one-time or ongoing effects — **Item**, **Supporter**, **Stadium**, and **Tool** cards.

## Parts of a Pokémon Card

- **HP** (Hit Points): how much damage it can take before being Knocked Out.
- **Type:** its Energy type (colored symbol).
- **Attacks:** each lists an Energy cost (symbols), a name, damage, and any effect.
- **Weakness:** damage from this type is increased (usually ×2).
- **Resistance:** damage from this type is reduced (usually −30).
- **Retreat Cost:** Energy that must be discarded to switch this Pokémon out of the Active spot.

## Setup

1. Both players shake hands and flip a coin; the winner decides who goes first.
2. Shuffle your 60-card deck and draw **7 cards**.
3. Place **one Basic Pokémon face down** as your **Active Pokémon**.
4. Place up to **5 more Basic Pokémon** face down on your **Bench**.
5. **Mulligan:** if you have **no Basic Pokémon** in your opening hand, reveal your hand, reshuffle, and draw 7 again. Your opponent may draw one extra card for each of your mulligans.
6. Take the top **6 cards** of your deck and set them aside face down as your **Prize cards**.
7. Both players flip their Active and Benched Pokémon face up. **The player going first cannot attack or play a Supporter on their very first turn** (they also do not take a Prize on turn one, since no attack occurs).

## Taking a Turn

Each turn proceeds in this order:

### 1. Draw
Draw **1 card** from the top of your deck. (If you cannot draw because your deck is empty, you lose.)

### 2. Main actions (in any order, as many as allowed)
You may do the following during your turn:

- **Put Basic Pokémon** from your hand onto your Bench (up to 5 Benched Pokémon total).
- **Evolve** your Pokémon by playing a matching Evolution card on top of it. A Pokémon can't evolve the turn it was played or the turn it evolved, and not on the first player's first turn.
- **Attach one Energy card** from your hand to one of your Pokémon — **only once per turn**.
- **Play Trainer cards:** any number of **Item** cards, but only **one Supporter** and only **one Stadium** per turn.
- **Use Abilities** as their text allows.
- **Retreat** your Active Pokémon **once per turn:** discard Energy equal to its Retreat Cost, then swap it with a Benched Pokémon.

### 3. Attack
Finally, your Active Pokémon may use **one attack** (if it has enough of the right Energy attached). Choose an attack, check that its Energy cost is met, apply the damage and effects:

- Place **damage counters** (each = 10 damage) on the Defending Pokémon.
- Apply **Weakness** (usually double the damage) and **Resistance** (usually −30) if applicable.
- After you attack, **your turn ends** (even if the attack does nothing else).

## Knock Outs and Prizes

When a Pokémon has **damage equal to or greater than its HP**, it is **Knocked Out**: its owner puts it and all cards attached to it into their discard pile.

- Each time you Knock Out an opponent's Pokémon, **take 1 Prize card** into your hand (some powerful Pokémon, like Pokémon-ex/V, award **2 Prizes** when Knocked Out).
- If your **Active** Pokémon is Knocked Out, you must promote one of your **Benched** Pokémon to become the new Active Pokémon. If you have no Pokémon left in play, you lose.

## Special Conditions

Attacks may inflict Special Conditions on the Defending Pokémon (only the Active Pokémon can be affected; retreating or evolving removes them):

- **Asleep:** cannot attack or retreat. Flip a coin between turns; heads, it wakes up.
- **Confused:** to attack, flip a coin; tails, the attack fails and it does 30 damage to itself.
- **Paralyzed:** cannot attack or retreat; recovers at the end of its owner's next turn.
- **Poisoned:** place 1 damage counter (10 damage) on it between each turn.
- **Burned:** place 2 damage counters (20 damage) between turns, then flip a coin — tails, it stays Burned.

## Winning the Game

You **win immediately** when you take your **last Prize card**, when your opponent has **no Pokémon in play** after a Knock Out, or when your opponent **cannot draw** at the start of their turn. If both players would win simultaneously, tournament tiebreak rules apply.

## Deckbuilding Notes

A balanced deck usually runs around **12–20 Pokémon**, **8–15 Trainer/Supporter/Item cards**, and enough **Energy** (often ~12–15) to reliably power your attacks. Evolution lines should include the Basics they evolve from. Supporters that draw cards and search your deck keep your hand flowing.

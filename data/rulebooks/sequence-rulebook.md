# Sequence Rulebook

## Table of Contents

*Sequence is a board-and-card game for 2–12 players (as individuals or teams). Play a card from your hand and place your chip on a matching space on the board. When you have five of your chips in a row, you've made a Sequence. Be the first player or team to make the required number of Sequences to win. It's exciting, challenging, and fun!*

## Components

- 1 game board
- 50 green marker chips
- 50 blue marker chips
- 35 red marker chips *(red chips are used only with three players/teams)*
- 104 Sequence cards (two standard 52-card decks; jokers are not used)

## Object of the Game

A **Sequence** is a connected series of five of the same-colored chips in a row—up or down, across, or diagonally on the board. To win: with **2 players or teams**, score **two** Sequences; with **3 players or teams**, score **one** Sequence.

## Setup

Choose a chip color (green, blue, or red). Each board space (except the corners) shows a standard playing card, and each card in the deck appears on the board **twice**. Shuffle the cards and deal each player a hand based on the number of players:
- 2 players → 7 cards each
- 3 players → 6 cards each
- 4 players → 6 cards each
- 6 players → 5 cards each
- 8 players → 4 cards each
- 9 players → 4 cards each
- 10 players → 3 cards each
- 12 players → 3 cards each

Place the remaining cards face down as the draw deck (keep a discard pile beside it).

## How to Play

Players take turns clockwise. On your turn:
1. Play one card from your hand **face up** onto your discard pile.
2. Place one of your marker chips on **either** board space showing that card (if both are already covered, see Dead Card).
3. **Draw a replacement card** from the deck. *(If you fail to draw before the next player begins their turn, you lose the right to draw and must play the rest of the game with fewer cards.)*

### The Corners

The four corners of the board are **free spaces** that count for **all** players. A player may use any corner as though it were their own chip—so a Sequence that uses a corner needs only four of your own chips.

### The Jacks

There are two kinds of Jacks:
- **Two-eyed Jacks (Diamonds and Clubs) are WILD.** When you play a two-eyed Jack, place one of your chips on **any open space** on the board.
- **One-eyed Jacks (Hearts and Spades) are anti-wild.** When you play a one-eyed Jack, **remove one of an opponent's chips** from the board, then discard the Jack (you do not place a chip that turn). *You cannot remove a chip that is part of a completed Sequence.*

### Dead Card

If you hold a card and **both** of its board spaces are already covered by chips, it is a "dead card." On your turn, you may exchange it: place it on the discard pile, announce "dead card," and draw a replacement. You may do this only once per turn, and you must then play a normal turn.

### Completing a Sequence

Once you have five of your chips in a row, place a marker (or announce it) to indicate a completed Sequence—it **cannot be broken** by a one-eyed Jack, nor used by opponents. **One chip** from a completed Sequence may be used as part of a **second** Sequence in a different direction, but a Sequence may share **only one** chip with another (i.e. each new Sequence needs at least four new chips).

## Team Play

For 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, or 12 players, play in teams. Teammates must be seated so that players **alternate** around the table (no two teammates sit consecutively). Teammates may not talk about or coach each other's plays; if a team does, the offending team must forfeit a card of the other team's choosing. All players on a team work toward the team's Sequences.

## Winning

The first player or team to complete the required number of Sequences (two for 1–2 players/teams, one for 3 players/teams) wins the game.

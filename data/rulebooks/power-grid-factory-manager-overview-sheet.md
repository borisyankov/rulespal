# Power Grid: Factory Manager Overview Sheet

## Table of Contents

*The overview sheet included in the game holds the two supplements the rules booklet asks you to read before your first game: supplement A, "the player's factory", which explains the factory board and the tile symbols, and supplement B, "the market mechanism", which explains the supply and the market.*

## Supplement A: The Player's Factory

*(Each player owns a factory and tries to expand it during the game more effectively than the others. This is an example for a starting factory.)*

1. Each player's factory has the following areas: the factory floor (7), (11) with factory tiles (2), the production value (3), storage (4), and energy consumption (5), canteen (9) and control room (10). Available workers are next to the factory (12).

2. **Factory tiles:** there are 6 different kinds of factory tiles — storage, machine, working robot, personnel robot, control and optimization. These tiles have 2-3 different values. The back side of each tile has numbers which correspond to the number of players in the game. Thus, all tiles with a "2-5" and "3-5" are used in a three-player game, and so on.

   Symbols on tiles (a short overview):
   - A: cost in Elektro
   - B: storage units
   - C: increase of production
   - D: needed workers
   - E: increased energy consumption
   - F: released workers
   - G: reduced energy consumption
   - H: only 1 robot per machine
   - Dark number on green: positive for players — improvement of the factory
   - White number on red: negative for players — degradation of the factory

3. **Production:** the player sums the dark numbers C on all his factory tiles. This sum represents the production value of his factory. The production is on 2.

4. **Storage:** the player sums the dark numbers B on all his storage tiles. This sum is the storage value of his factory. The storage is on 3.

5. **Energy consumption:** the player sums the white numbers E on his factory tiles and subtracts the dark numbers G from this sum. This result is the energy consumption of his factory. The energy consumption of a factory may never go below 1. The energy consumption is on 4.

6. There is no maximum for the values. However, the tracks offered should be large enough for all but the most unusual cases.

7. **Factory floor I:** this is where the player places his machines, robots, and storage tiles. It does not matter where the player places each tile on his factory floor.

8. **Earnings:** the earnings for the factory is equal to the lowest of the values for production and storage (in this factory it is now 20). Players should plan to keep these values close to each other for this reason.

9. **Canteen:** each machine needs a worker to run it. The players place their workers in the canteen. The player adds the white numbers D on his factory tiles and subtracts the dark numbers F to determine how many workers he needs. At this time, there are 4 workers in this factory's canteen.

10. **Control room:** on these two spaces, players can place one optimization tile and one control tile. Thus, each player may only have one of each type at one time!

11. **Factory floor II:** players pay nothing when placing tiles on the 10 spaces of the factory floor. However, the players pay 10 Elektro each when first placing a tile on either of these two spaces.

12. **Available workers:** the player places his available workers next to his factory. These are workers that are not currently needed to operate his machines. They can be used during the auction of the player order tiles and the purchase of the factory tiles. Each player must always have at least one available worker (now there are 3 here).

## Supplement B: The Market Mechanism

### Setup

1. The energy price is 1 Elektro per unit at the beginning of the game.

2. Sort the factory tiles by type and cost and place them face-up on the corresponding spaces in 6 columns. The number of tiles used depends on the number of players. Also, some spaces will have no tiles based on the number of players.

3. The factory tiles in a column have different values and prices. The less expensive tiles are at the bottom of the columns and the most expensive are at the top. They are arranged in cost order. In phase 2 (preparing the market), a player can only place the cheapest tiles from a column into the market. Tiles from the next higher price are only available after all tiles of the lower prices from a column have already been placed!

4. Players should leave some space below the market board so they can place factory tiles there to be purchased.

5. Shuffle the 6 factory tiles with an X on the back (one for each type) face-down and choose 3 at random and put these back in the box. Place the other 3 tiles face-up below the market board (into the market).

The supply is sorted according to purchase price. The market starts with the 3 X-tiles.

### Preparing the Market — A First Overview

The central mechanism in Factory Manager is in selecting the factory tiles from the supply, which are available to all players in the market. Both the selection of the tiles in phase 2 and the purchase of the tiles in phase 3 is always begun by the player with the smallest turn order number.

Each player selects 1 factory tile **for each of his available workers** from any of the six columns of the supply and puts them into the market (when taking a tile from a column, the player must always choose the cheapest tile in that column). During his turn, a player may take tiles from different columns or from the same column as he chooses. When a player, in turn order, has completed his entire selection, the next player takes his turn.

A player may not choose to pass on selecting a tile. Each player must always select a number of tiles equal to the number of available workers he has!

The last player (the player with the highest turn order number) may place additional tiles into the market, depending on the number of players:

| # of players | Additional factory tiles |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | 0 |
| 3 | 1 |
| 4 | 2 |
| 5 | 3 |

**The addition of these tiles is voluntary.** The last player can choose to add fewer tiles than he is allowed, including adding no additional tiles to the market!

In the first round, three tiles with an "X" are included in the market!

The challenge for players with this mechanism is the turn order. The first player with the lowest turn order number places the cheapest (and thus weakest) factory tiles into the market (in phase 2), and the last player in turn order selects from the most expensive and best tiles for the market. In phase 3, players acquire the factory tiles beginning again with the first player with the lowest turn order number, so he gets first choice from all those chosen for the market while the last player can choose only from what is left over.

Thus, players must choose carefully what tiles they place in the market so that they have good choices, but also keep the best choices from their opponents. Sometimes it is better to offer something good to another player to get this favor back. As a trade-off the player order tokens with a higher turn order give players bigger discounts, allowing them to purchase multiple factory tiles in one turn.

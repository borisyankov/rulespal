# People Power: Insurgency in the Philippines, 1981-1986 — Bonifacio Card-Based Non-Player Rules and Reference Booklet

## Table of Contents

*Bonifacio is the Non-Player (NP) system for People Power that may be used to replace any player Faction with an NP Faction. A deck of cards and several player aid tables guide the decisions made by each NP Faction. Bonifacio is named in honor of Andrés Bonifacio, a 19th century Filipino revolutionary leader, often called "The Father of the Philippine Revolution", and considered one of the national heroes of the Philippines. This booklet is a companion to the main People Power Rules of Play, and its section numbering (8.0) continues from that rulebook's 7.0 Victory section.*

## 8.1 Introduction & Components

### 8.1.1 Overview

Bonifacio is the Non-Player (NP) system for People Power that may be used to replace any player Faction with an NP Faction. A deck of cards and several player aid tables guide the decisions made by each NP Faction. Bonifacio is named in honor of Andrés Bonifacio, a 19th century Filipino revolutionary leader, often called "The Father of the Philippine Revolution", and considered one of the national heroes of the Philippines.

> For players familiar with Arjuna, Trưng, or Tây Sơn, the Bonifacio system should be easy to use, but there are a few small differences you should be aware of. Most importantly, the NP Sequence of Play priorities (8.5) have been adapted for the three-player system introduced by People Power—make sure to read this section carefully! There are also additional NP instructions for the advanced Personality and Act of Desperation rules (8.6)—we would recommend only introducing these once you are comfortable with the base NP system.

### 8.1.2 Components

- **Bonifacio Cards.** Each NP Faction has six Bonifacio cards that provide instructions for selecting and executing Operations and Special Activities.
- **Priorities Tables.** Bonifacio includes three types of Priorities tables: Space Selection, Move, and Pieces. Each Faction uses its own Space Selection Priorities table. All Factions share the Move Priorities and Pieces Priorities tables.
- **Eligibility Table.** The Eligibility table determines how an Eligible NP Faction will act on the current Event card.
- **Effective Events Table.** The Effective Events table specifies the conditions under which an NP Faction will treat an Event as 'Effective'.
- **Election Round Instructions.** The Election Round instructions guide the actions of each NP Faction during Election Rounds.

The Priorities Tables, Eligibility Table, Effective Events Table, and Election Round Instructions can all be found on the Non-player Aid Card.

## 8.2 Golden Rules & Key Terms

### 8.2.1 Golden Rules

- **NP Factions Follow the Rules (Almost).** NP Factions abide by all applicable People Power rules, with these exceptions:
  - NP Factions never remove pieces from the map to Available when lacking pieces for an Operation, Special Activity, or Event. If this means that an Operation would have no effect, draw a new Bonifacio card to select a different Operation.
  - NP Factions do not track or spend Resources.
  - NP Factions roll against an Activation Number to limit total spaces selected for Operations (8.5.4).
- **Skip Illegal Instructions.** Bonifacio will only tell you what to do, it will not tell you how to do it or even if its instructions constitute a legal move given the state of the board and available pieces. Always carry out Bonifacio's instructions as much as possible, but if Bonifacio ever gives you an instruction that cannot be carried out legally, just skip it.
- **Implement All Instructions Literally.** Do not infer more to an instruction than what the text actually specifies.
- **Placing Friendly Pieces.** When executing Operations, Special Activities, or Events that allow the acting Faction to place pieces in multiple spaces, NP Factions usually place pieces one at a time. In such cases, select the destination for each piece one at a time, using the NP Faction's Space Selection Priorities table. However, during Train, Rally, and Recruit Operations, NP Factions will place as many pieces as possible in each selected space, following the directions on the Bonifacio card and the limits of the given Operation and their Available pieces.
- **Removing Friendly Pieces.** When given a choice of where to remove their own pieces from, NP Factions remove pieces one at a time, from the space with the most pieces belonging to the affected NP Faction. Use the Piece Priorities table to select pieces to remove from each selected space.
- **Ops & SA to the Maximum Extent.** NP Factions always execute a selected Operation or Special Activity to the maximum extent allowed, given the state of the board, available pieces, and Activation Number die rolls.
- **When in Doubt, Randomize.** Whenever NP Factions need to select from among several options and supplied instructions have been exhausted, select one option at random.

### 8.2.2 Important Bonifacio Terms

**1983 Campaign:** All turns during which the Ninoy Aquino Murdered Election card is in effect take place during the 1983 Campaign. This will be the first campaign of the Standard Scenario, or the second campaign of the Extended Scenario.

**1984 Campaign:** All turns during which the 1984 Parliamentary Election Election card is in effect take place during the 1984 Campaign. This will be the second campaign of the Standard Scenario, or the third campaign of the Extended Scenario.

**1d6, 2d6, 3d6, 1d3:** Die or dice roll to generate a random number. 1d6 means roll the die once; 2d6 means sum two dice, etc. 1d3 means roll one die and halve the result, rounding up.

**Activation Number:** A limit on the total number of spaces selected by NP Factions for all Operations. An NP Faction must roll greater than its Activation Number to select another space (8.5.4).

**Critical Event:** An Event is Critical for an NP Faction if that Faction's Eligibility symbol is marked with a shaded icon on the Event card (8.9). NP Factions will always perform Critical Events if possible, provided that the Event is also Effective (8.5.5).

**Effective Event:** An Event is Effective if one or more of its effects are listed for an NP Faction on the Effective Events table (8.5.5).

**Get:** Move just enough pieces to the destination to meet the instruction, taking into account any pieces already present in the space. If no additional pieces are needed to meet the instruction, move none. If there are insufficient pieces to meet the instruction, move as many as possible. When a get instruction might involve several spaces, select the one where it is possible to accomplish as much of the instruction as possible.

**Government Firepower:** The maximum number of NPA pieces that could be removed during a Government Assault in a given space, including any modifications due to terrain and force composition, independent of whether any NPA pieces are present.

**Keep:** Leave just enough pieces in the origin to meet the instruction. If there are insufficient pieces to meet a particular instruction, ignore it.

**NP:** Non-Player.

**Vulnerable Bases:** NPA Bases in spaces without Underground Guerrillas, Reformers Bases in spaces without Inactive Activists, and Government Bases in spaces with fewer than 2 cubes.

**Vulnerable Enemies:** Enemy pieces that could be removed by a Government Faction Assault or Roundup: Active NPA Guerrillas and Reformers Activists, NPA Bases in spaces without Underground Guerrillas, and Reformers Bases in spaces without Inactive Activists.

**Within Reach:** A space is within reach of moving pieces if those pieces could move to that space during the selected Operation (adjacent spaces for NPA March or Reformers Assemble, any space on the map for Government Sweep).

## 8.3 NP Victory

### 8.3.1 Playing Solitaire

When playing solitaire against Bonifacio, the player may only win during the final Election Round. NP Factions may win during the Victory Phase of any Election Round.

### 8.3.2 Playing With Two Players

When playing with two players, any Faction may win during any Election Round.

## 8.4 NP Setup

### 8.4.1 Setting Up NP Factions for any Scenario

- **Create the Bonifacio Deck.** NP Government, NP NPA, and NP Reformers each use six Bonifacio cards marked with their Faction color and symbol. Shuffle the cards for the NP Factions that you are using into one deck, face up.
- **Select Personality Cards.** If applicable to the scenario that you are playing, select a Personality card for each NP Faction using the instructions in section 8.10.5 and on the Non-player Aid Card.
- **Resource Cylinders.** Remove and set aside the Resource cylinder belonging to any NP Faction.

## 8.5 NP Sequence of Play

### 8.5.1 Overview

Generally, NP Factions make the same decisions that player Factions do. First, Eligible NP Factions determine if and how they will act on the current Event card. The Bonifacio card deck is used to select Operations and Special Activities and provide instructions to execute that action. Event card icons and the Effective Events table guide an NP Faction's selection and execution of Events. When performing Events, NP Reformers and NP NPA will always apply the shaded Event text, and NP Government will always apply the unshaded Event text. Priority tables are used to select spaces where NP Factions will act, how NP Factions move pieces, and which friendly and enemy pieces are affected by an action.

### 8.5.2 NP Sequence of Play and Eligibility Table

NP Factions follow the base game Sequence of Play and Eligible NP Factions choose from the same options as player Factions. Use the Eligibility table and the Critical Event shadings (8.5.5) on the current Event card (if any) to determine the choice made by an Eligible NP Faction. If the NP Faction selects an Operation plus Special Activity or Limited Operation, use the active NP Faction's Bonifacio cards and the various Priorities tables to select and execute the Operation and any Special Activity (8.5.3). If the NP Faction selects an Event, execute the Event using the various Priority tables (8.5.5).

> **Important!** If any NP Faction ever performs an Operation in a single space with no SA, place their cylinder in the 'Limited Operation: Stay Eligible' box. If a 1st Eligible NP Faction ever performs an Operation in multiple spaces with no Special Activity, place their cylinder in the 'Op Only or Event' box. A 2nd Eligible NP Faction will sometimes perform an Operation in multiple spaces with no Special Activity—in this case still place its cylinder in the 'Op + Special Activity' box as usual.

> **PLAY NOTE:** Remember that a Faction that chooses to perform a Limited Operation will remain Eligible to play on the next Event card. If the 1st Eligible Faction chooses to perform a Limited Operation, then the 2nd Eligible faction (if any) becomes 1st Eligible and should use that row of the NP Eligibility Table (if they are an NP Faction). If the 2nd Eligible Faction chooses to perform a Limited Operation, then the 3rd Eligible Faction (if any) becomes 2nd Eligible and should use that row of the NP Eligibility Table (if they are an NP Faction).

### 8.5.3 Executing Operations and Special Activities With Bonifacio Cards

Each NP Faction has six Bonifacio cards that provide instructions for selecting and executing Operations and Special Activities.

> A sample Bonifacio card is annotated with: Faction and card ID (top left); an Activation Number (top right); a Condition (green arrow if true, red arrow if false) leading to an Operation; a "draw new card" icon; a "flip card" icon; a Special Activity list; and a Stop icon at the bottom of the card.

- **Face Up and Face Down.** Face up sides are identified by a single letter and face down sides by a double letter. Except when acting as the active card, cards in the Bonifacio deck should be face up.
- **Setting Up the Bonifacio Deck.** At the start of the game and during the Reset Phase of each Election Round, shuffle together all Bonifacio cards for all in-use NP Factions into a single deck, face up.
- **Drawing Bonifacio Cards.** First, move the top card to the bottom of the deck (even if this top card matches the active NP Faction). Continue moving cards to the bottom, one at a time, until a card matching the active NP Faction is the top card. Always place cards on the bottom of the deck face up.
- **Reading Bonifacio Cards.** Start with the first blue box at the top of the card. If the condition in a box is true, follow the green arrow; if it is false, follow the red arrow. Continue down until you reach an Operation or Special Activity.
- **Drawing a New Card and Flipping a Card.** If an arrow points to the "draw new card" icon, draw a new card. If an arrow points to the "flip card" icon, flip the card to its face down side and continue following the instructions there.
- **Executing Operations.** Execute each numbered instruction in order, as much as possible (i.e., execute instruction ① in as many spaces as possible, then ②, and so on—remembering that NP Factions follow all of the usual rules about space selection, etc.). Follow the instructions on the Bonifacio card to select spaces, using the active NP Faction's Space Selection Priorities table when directed. Text in bold specifies the column of that NP Faction's Space Selection Priorities table to use. Arrowed instructions serve as reminders for the implementation of the preceding instruction. Starred instructions are executed immediately when they are reached on a card, and do not require any Activation Number rolls (8.5.4). Instructions that follow a red conditional are only executed if that condition is true. If there are no legal spaces for the selected Operation, draw a new Bonifacio card to select a different Operation. In rare cases there might be no Bonifacio cards with eligible Operations, in which case the NP Faction will Pass. See 8.7 Operations Reference for detailed instructions for each Operation.
- **Executing Special Activities.** Execute one Special Activity from the list, selecting the first possible in letter order (i.e., select Ⓐ if possible, then Ⓑ, and so on). Underlined text specifies the Special Activity. Within each Special Activity, execute each numbered instruction in order, as much as possible (i.e., execute instruction ① in as many spaces as possible, then ②, and so on—remembering that NP Factions follow all of the usual rules about space selection, etc.). Text in bold specifies the column of the Space Selection Priorities table to use when selecting spaces if multiple spaces could be selected within the instructions given on the card. If a Special Activity cannot be executed, or its red condition is not true, skip it and execute the next one. If no Special Activity can be executed, select either Limited Operation (if no Operations have yet been performed, or an Operation has been performed in only one space) or Operation Only (if an Operation has been performed in two or more spaces). See 8.8 Special Activities Reference for detailed instructions of each Special Activity.

### 8.5.4 Activation Numbers

The total number of spaces that NP Factions select for Operations are limited by an Activation Number. Activation Numbers are found on the upper right of all Bonifacio cards and indicated by a die icon.

- **Activation Number.** After executing an NP Operation in a selected space, roll 1d6. If the result is less than or equal to the Activation Number, the NP Faction will not select any additional spaces; implement any starred Operations instructions and then proceed to the next instruction on the card. If the result is greater than the Activation Number, the NP Faction will select another space for Operations. Continue rolling as above after executing the Operation in each selected space until the Activation Number roll is less than or equal to the Activation Number, or there are insufficient pieces or spaces to continue the Operation.
- **Activation Number Exceptions.** Government Train allows one additional space to be selected for Civic Action at the conclusion of the Operation, either after failing an Activation Number roll or after all eligible spaces have been selected. NP Government will use Shift Towards Support to select one eligible space for Civic Action (i.e., with Control, Troops, and Police, and no Protest or Strike), removing Terror and shifting towards Support for 1d3 steps total. This can be either a space already selected for Train or a new space, in which case it is assumed that the NP Faction selected the space for Train without placing any cubes there (8.7.1).

  NP Reformers and NP NPA will place Bases in all eligible spaces when performing a Recruit or Rally Operation, without needing to make any Activation Number rolls. After doing this they will then proceed to Recruit or Rally as usual, making an Activation Number roll after executing each space (8.7.2, 8.7.3).

  NP Factions do not make Activation Number rolls when executing a free Operation granted by an Event (8.5.5).

  > **Important!** NP Reformers treat all Activation Numbers as being one pip lower while the "Opposition Parties Gain Seats" Momentum is in effect (i.e., during the final campaign of any game). This is indicated on the NP Reformers Bonifacio cards by an alternative Activation Number labeled "1984 Campaign".

- **Resources.** NP Factions do not track or spend Resources. NP NPA and NP Reformers will still perform Extort and Appeal Special Activities when instructed to by their Bonifacio Card, but will not gain any Resources for doing so.

> **DESIGN NOTE:** It might seem strange that these NP Factions will 'waste' Special Activities for no purpose, but this is an abstraction intended to simulate their reliance on these actions to gain Resources during a campaign, and can also sometimes serve to block other Factions from taking Special Activities. On average, each Faction should perform as many Operations and Special Activities as a player using Resources might be able to.

### 8.5.5 Executing Events

- **Critical Events.** NP Factions will only ever perform Events that are Critical for them, marked by a shaded icon behind their Faction Eligibility symbol on the Event card (8.9). To be performed, an Event must also be Effective (i.e., it must do something useful for the Faction).
- **Effective Events.** A Faction will execute an Event if it is marked Critical for the acting NP Faction and, if executed, would result in one or more of the effects listed for the acting NP Faction on the Effective Events table.
- **Unshaded or Shaded?** NP Government always selects unshaded Event text, while NP NPA and NP Reformers always select shaded Event text.
- **Place Friendly, Remove Enemy.** When given a choice, NP Factions only place friendly pieces and only remove enemy pieces.
- **Selecting Spaces.** Select spaces using the column of the active NP Faction's Space Selection Priorities (8.5.6) table that matches the Effective Event action. If the Event includes more than one Effective action, use the leftmost matching column.
- **Events That Allow Operations or Special Activities.** Draw a Bonifacio card for the active NP Faction to select an Operation or Special Activity. If the Event specifies the Operation or Special Activity, draw Bonifacio cards until a matching Operation or Special Activity is drawn (check both sides).

### 8.5.6 Space Selection Priorities Tables

There are three Space Selection Priorities tables, one for each NP Faction. Use an NP Faction's Space Selection Priorities table to select spaces for Operations, Special Activities, Events, and during Election Rounds. The type of action the NP Faction will execute determines the column to use on the Space Selection Priorities table. During Operations and Special Activities, use the column specified by the bold text on the Bonifacio card. During Events, use the column that matches the Effective action specified in the Event text. If an Event specifies more than one type of Effective action, use the leftmost column on the Space Selection Priorities table. During Election Rounds, follow the NP Election Round Instructions (8.10).

- **Selecting a Space.** When executing an Operation, Special Activity, or Event, select each space one at a time:

  Step 1. Start at the top of the column and read down. For each row with a dot in that column, check its condition against all spaces that could be selected. Some rows contain conditional statements, marked by a red checkmark, that must be true for a red dot to apply. If no spaces meet the condition, skip that row and continue to the next. If only one space meets this condition, execute the Operation, Special Activity, or Event in this space. Otherwise:

  Step 2. If more than one space meets this condition, continue down the column to the next condition that is met. Continue to skip conditions that are not met or whose red conditional statement is not true. If only one space meets both the original condition and the new condition, execute the action in this space (as in Step 1).

  Step 3. Continue this process, checking each row below and applying all met conditions, until a single space is selected.

  Step 4. Repeat this process from Step 1 for each space to be selected.

- **Random Spaces.** To select a space at random, assign equal probability to each candidate space and roll a die.

### 8.5.7 Move Priorities Tables

Whenever an NP Faction needs to move its pieces on the map, use the Move Priorities table. Read down the column that matches the active NP Faction, executing each instruction in a dotted row. Dotted rows with a red conditional only match if the condition is true. See 8.7 Operations Reference and 8.8 Special Activities Reference for detailed instructions of Operations and Special Activities that move pieces.

- **Step A.** Select a destination space using the instructions on the Bonifacio card, and if none, use the column of the active NP Faction's Space Selection Priorities table that matches the selected Operation.
- **Step B.** Select an origin space with the most friendly cubes, Activists, or Guerrillas, and not already selected as a destination (or as an origin for NPA March).
- **Keep Pieces in Origin.** Reading down the column that matches the active NP Faction, keep pieces in the selected origin as instructed by each dotted row. Instructions that follow a red conditional are only executed if that condition is true.
- **Get Pieces to Destination.** Reading down the column that matches the active NP Faction, move pieces into the selected destination as instructed by each dotted row. Instructions that follow a red conditional are only executed if that condition is true.
- **Step C.** After all moving pieces have been moved from the first origin, check to see if additional origins or destinations will be selected. To select a new destination, return to Step A. To select a new origin, return to Step B. Note that the NP Government and NP Reformers may need to pass an Activation Number roll to select additional destination spaces during Sweep (8.7.1) or Assemble (8.7.2), and NP NPA may need to pass an Activation Number roll to select additional origin spaces during March (8.7.3).

> **Important!** Unless otherwise specified by the current Bonifacio card or Move Priorities table instructions, select moving pieces using the Piece Priorities table (within the constraints of the Operation, Special Activity, or Event being executed).

### 8.5.8 Piece Priorities Table

When an NP Faction needs to select pieces from among several different types, use the Piece Priorities table. When placing, moving, or using friendly pieces or removing, replacing, or Activating enemy pieces, start from the top and read down; within boxes, read left to right. When removing friendly pieces, start from the bottom and read up; within boxes, read right to left.

### 8.5.9 Election Rounds

Follow the Election Round instructions for all NP Factions (8.10).

## 8.6 NP Personalities and Acts of Desperation

Use the following instructions if playing with the advanced Personalities (5.3) and Act of Desperation (5.4) rules. Each NP Faction will select and use Personalities and Acts of Desperation as described below and in the NP Election Round Instructions (8.10), while the players should select and use their Personalities and Acts of Desperation as described in the relevant sections of the main rules.

### 8.6.1 NP Faction Personalities

Each NP Faction will select a Personality using the priorities listed in the NP Election Round Instructions (except during the first campaign of the Extended Scenario, where no Faction uses Personalities). First, each player should secretly select a Personality for their Faction as usual, then consult the priority list for each NP Faction (8.10.5) and select the indicated Personality from those still available (each Personality may still only be selected once during the course of a game). Select a new Personality in this manner during the Newsmakers phase of each Election Round. Each selected Personality will modify the specified NP Operations and Special Activities in the usual way, unless noted otherwise below:

- **Juan Ponce Enrile (#41):** NP Government will use every Special Activity to gain 3 Patronage without having to spend any Resources.
- **Imelda Marcos (#42):** NP Government will never select this Personality.
- **General Fabian Ver (#43):** NP Government will use Piece Priorities to remove one piece from each space selected for Reprisal, or to remove up to two pieces if only one space is selected.

  > **PLAY NOTE:** NP Reprisal can still only target up to two spaces with Government Control, and will never select spaces with Support.

- **General Fidel V. Ramos (#44):** NP Government will count Troops as Police when checking if it will perform Roundup (and then remove additional Reformers pieces with Troops when performing Roundup, as per the usual rules for this Personality).
- **Corazon Aquino (#45):** No additional rules, NP Reformers will just place an additional Activist in each City selected for Recruit.
- **Jaime Cardinal Sin (#46):** NP Reformers using Convert will first check if a Government Base can be replaced, then immediately replace up to two cubes with one Activist using Remove or Replace in any remaining Convert spaces.
- **Salvador Laurel (#47):** NP Reformers will never select this Personality.
- **Col. Gregorio Honasan (#48):** No additional rules, remove one Troop from each space selected for Government Roundup if possible.
- **Jose Maria Sison (#49):** When Rallying, NP NPA will first Place Bases in Countryside spaces where 2+ Guerrillas or 1+ Guerrilla and no cubes (and remove only one Guerrilla to do so, as per the usual rules for this Personality).
- **Rodolfo Salas (#50):** NP NPA will select up to two spaces for Ambush when instructed to perform this Special Ability by a Bonifacio card, using the usual space selection priorities listed on the card.
- **Rolly Kintanar (#51):** NP NPA will use Piece Priorities to remove one Activist or one Police from the first possible space selected for Terror during each Terror Operation (i.e., remove a player piece if possible, prioritizing Police first and then Activists if both other Factions are player controlled).
- **Leandro Alejandro (#52):** NP NPA will set the first selected space with Support or Opposition directly to Resistance when using Strike.

### 8.6.2 NP Acts of Desperation

During the Newsmakers phase of the 1984 Election Round, each NP Faction will select an Act of Desperation using the priorities listed in the NP Election Round Instructions (8.10). First, each player should secretly select an Act of Desperation for their Faction as usual, then consult the priority list for each NP Faction (8.10.5) and select the indicated Act of Desperation. Reveal all selected Acts of Desperation (do not use the optional Hidden Acts of Desperation rule if using any NP Faction), then execute them during the Victory? phase of the final Election Round in the normal sequence. Use the Space Selection and Piece Priorities tables when deciding which spaces to target, or which pieces to remove or replace, while executing NP Acts of Desperation.

> **Important!** The sections that follow are written out versions of procedures found on the Bonifacio cards and various play aids, and are intended to be used as a reference.

## 8.7 NP Operations Reference

> **Important!** NP Factions always follow all rules when executing an Operation (exception: NP Factions do not spend Resources; see 8.5.4). Never select a space for an Operation if that space is not eligible for that Operation (8.2.1). If a Bonifacio instruction contradicts the rules, skip it!

When directed by Bonifacio to execute an Operation (8.5.3), follow the directions in the Operations instructions box on the selected Bonifacio card. Implement all ① instructions first, then ② instructions, and so forth. For each, carry out the instructions as much as possible.

PROCEDURE: Consult the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) for the active NP Faction to select the first space for the Operation. Once a space has been selected, execute the first Operation in that space. Only perform instructions marked with a star symbol if the conditions are met. If the NP Faction passes its Activation Number roll and may select additional spaces (8.5.4), return to the Space Selection Priorities table to select another space. Continue selecting spaces until the NP Faction fails an Activation Number roll or no more spaces are eligible for that Operation.

After an Operation has been executed in all selected spaces, complete any further instructions for that Operation marked by the starred symbol on the Bonifacio card.

> **EXAMPLE of Instructions:** NP Government executes Train. After placing pieces on the map, use Shift Towards Support to select one space that meets the criteria for Civic Action (could be an additional space not yet selected for Train), and roll 1d3 to see how many Terror markers to remove and steps to shift towards Support.

If no spaces are eligible for the Operation, instead draw a new Bonifacio card (8.5.3) to select another Operation. In this case, if a Special Activity has already been executed, ignore any instructions to execute another Special Activity on the new Bonifacio card.

> **PLAY NOTE:** This may occasionally mean that an NP Faction performs a Special Activity followed by an Operation that is not usually permitted with that Special Activity. In this case execute the Operation anyway—this is simply one of the small prices we have to pay for analog artificial intelligence.

### 8.7.1 NP Government Operations

NP Government will execute an Operation as much as possible, given available pieces and the state of the board. Follow all procedures for the selected Operation (3.2).

NP GOVERNMENT TRAIN (3.2.1) PROCEDURE: Use the Place Cubes column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) to select Cities or Countryside spaces with a Government Base. In each selected space, place up to four cubes total, alternating Police then Troops if possible (8.5.8). After all Train Operations are complete, use the Shift Towards Support column to select one space that meets the criteria for Civic Action, then shift it 1d3 steps towards Support. The space selected for Civic Action may be an additional one that was not selected for Train, with no Activation Number roll required to the select the space. If it is a Limited Operation, only the single space selected for Train may be shifted towards Support.

NP GOVERNMENT SWEEP (3.2.2) PROCEDURE: Use the Move Priorities table (8.5.7). First, select a destination space (Step A) using the Sweep Destination column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6). Next, select as an origin the space with the most cubes (Step B). Keep cubes in the origin and get cubes to the destination as instructed by the Move Priorities table. After all eligible cubes have moved from the first origin, check to see if there is now Government Control in the destination space (Step C). If not, return to Step B to select another origin space and continue moving cubes to the same destination. Once there is Government Control in the destination space, roll to see if NP Government may select another destination space (or stop if this is a Limited Operation). If no more cubes can be moved but additional destination spaces may still be selected, use the Sweep Destination column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) to select additional spaces just to Activate Underground Guerrillas, prioritizing spaces with NPA Bases. Once all destination spaces have been selected, Activate one Guerrilla for every two Troops in each destination space (or one Guerrilla for every two cubes in destination Cities).

> **Important!** Protest or Strike spaces may not be selected as origins during Government Sweep (3.2.2).

NP GOVERNMENT ROUNDUP (3.2.3) PROCEDURE: Use the Remove or Replace column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) to select Roundup spaces with both Police and Vulnerable Reformers pieces (i.e., where Roundup would have some effect). If a Reprisal Special Activity is then performed, also remove a now Vulnerable Reformers Base in one additional Roundup space (if possible), with no Activation Number roll required to select the space (this may not be a space already selected for Roundup).

> **PERSONALITIES NOTE:** If General Fidel V. Ramos (#44) is in play, count Troops as Police when performing Roundup. If Col. Gregorio Honasan (#48) is in play, remove one Troop from each space selected for Roundup (if possible).

NP GOVERNMENT ASSAULT (3.2.4) PROCEDURE: Use the Remove or Replace column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) to select Assault spaces with both Troops (or cubes in Cities) and vulnerable NPA pieces (i.e., where Assault would have some effect).

### 8.7.2 NP Reformers Operations

NP Reformers will execute an Operation as much as possible, given available pieces and the state of the board. Follow all procedures for the selected Operation (3.3).

NP REFORMERS RECRUIT (3.3.1) PROCEDURE: First, place Bases in all eligible spaces with three or more Activists, or with two Activists and no Police, using the Place Bases column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) if there are more eligible spaces than Available Bases. No Activation Number roll is necessary to continue selecting additional spaces after placing Bases. Then, select additional spaces to place Activists using the Place Activists column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6). Continue to select spaces for Recruit until NP Reformers fail their Activation Number roll or no eligible spaces or Available Activists remain.

> **PERSONALITIES NOTE:** If Corazon Aquino (#45) is in play, place one additional Activist in each City space chosen for Recruit (even if a Base was placed there).

NP REFORMERS ASSEMBLE (3.3.2) PROCEDURE: Use the Move Priorities table (8.5.7). First, select a destination space (Step A) using the Assemble Destination column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6). Next, select as an origin the adjacent space with the most Activists and not already selected as a destination (Step B). Keep Activists in the origin and get Activists to the destination as instructed by the Move Priorities table. After all eligible Activists have moved from the first origin, check to see if the combined total of Reformers and NPA pieces in the destination is greater than or equal to Government pieces there (Step C). If not, return to Step B to select another origin space and continue moving Activists to the same destination. Once there are equal or more Reformers plus NPA pieces than Government pieces in the destination, or if no more Activists may be moved to that destination, roll to see if NP Reformers may select another destination space (or stop if this is a Limited Operation). Then, after performing any Special Activity, remove Terror from all destination spaces and remove Protest from all spaces at Opposition, unless doing so would add Government Control.

NP REFORMERS PERSUADE (3.3.3) PROCEDURE: Use the Persuade column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) to select Cities with Strike first, then other spaces where Guerrillas could be removed. Continue to select spaces for Persuade until NP Reformers fail their Activation Number roll or no eligible spaces remain.

NP REFORMERS PROTEST (3.3.4) PROCEDURE: Use the Protest column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) to select spaces with Government Bases and no cubes first, then other spaces. Select spaces only where the Protest Operation would have an effect (i.e., do not select spaces that both already have a Protest marker and are already at Opposition). Continue to select spaces for Protest until NP Reformers fail their Activation Number roll or no eligible spaces or Protest markers remain (but note that Protest may select spaces that already have Protest markers and are not yet at Opposition).

> **PLAY NOTE:** If Ninoy Aquino Murdered (#38) in play, set each space selected for Protest directly to Opposition.

### 8.7.3 NP NPA Operations

NP NPA will execute an Operation as much as possible, given available pieces and the state of the board. Follow all procedures for the selected Operation (3.4).

NP NPA RALLY (3.4.1) PROCEDURE: First, place Bases in all eligible spaces with three or more Guerrillas, or with two Guerrillas and no Government cubes, using the Place Bases column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) if there are more eligible spaces than Available Bases. No Activation Number roll is necessary to continue selecting additional spaces after placing Bases. Then, select additional spaces to place Guerrillas using the Place Guerrillas column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6). NP NPA will never use Rally to flip Guerrillas Underground. Continue to select spaces for Rally until NP NPA fail their Activation Number roll or no eligible spaces remain or Available Guerrillas remain.

> **PERSONALITIES NOTE:** If Jose Maria Sison (#49) is in play, place Bases in Countryside spaces with two or more Guerrillas, then Countryside spaces with one Guerrilla and no cubes, and only remove one Guerrilla for each Base placed in a Countryside space.

NP NPA MARCH (3.4.2) PROCEDURE: Use the Move Priorities table (8.5.7). First, select a destination space (Step A) using the March Destination column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6). Next, select as an origin the adjacent space with the most Guerrillas and not already selected as an origin (Step B). Keep Guerrillas in the origin and get Guerrillas to the destination as instructed by the Move Priorities table. Then, if any moveable Guerrillas remain in the selected origin space (Step C), continue using the March Destination column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) to select additional destinations adjacent to that origin. Finally, roll to see if NP NPA may select another origin space (or stop if this is a Limited Operation), and if so return to the start of this procedure.

> **Important!** NP NPA will first select a March destination space, and then look for an adjacent origin space with moveable Guerrillas. However, as the NPA March Operation selects origin spaces rather than destinations, they will then continue to look for additional destinations adjacent to the selected origin. Once no moveable Guerrillas remain in the selected origin, they will roll to see if they can select an additional origin space, then look for a new destination space.

NP NPA ATTACK (3.4.3) PROCEDURE: Use the Attack column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) to select spaces with three or more Guerrillas, and then spaces with no Underground Guerrillas. Continue to select spaces for Attack until NP NPA fail their Activation Number roll or no eligible spaces remain.

NP NPA TERROR (3.4.4) PROCEDURE: Never select any space already at Resistance for Terror. Use the Place Terror column of the Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) to select spaces not already at Resistance. Select spaces with NPA Bases only if two or more Underground Guerrillas are also present. Continue to select spaces for Terror until NP NPA fail their Activation Number roll or no eligible spaces remain.

> **PERSONALITIES NOTE:** If Rolly Kintanar (#51) is in play, remove one Activist or Police from the first possible space selected for Terror each turn (a player piece if possible, Police first, then Active Activist, then Inactive Activist).

## 8.8 NP Special Activities Reference

When directed by Bonifacio to execute a Special Activity (8.5.3), follow the directions in the Special Activities instruction box on the selected Bonifacio card. An NP Faction will implement the first listed Special Activity that it can. If a specified Special Activity cannot be carried out (because no spaces are eligible for that Special Activity, or a check condition is not met), skip it and continue to the next. Once a Special Activity has been selected, carry out ① instructions first, then ② instructions, and so forth. Implement each instruction as much as possible. If executing a Limited Operation (2.3.5), ignore all Special Activity instructions on the selected Bonifacio card.

PROCEDURE: Use the active Faction's Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) to select the first space for the Special Activity. Once a space is selected, execute the Special Activity in that space. If the NP Faction may select additional spaces, return to the Space Selection Priorities table to select another space. Continue selecting spaces until the maximum number of spaces has been selected, or no eligible spaces remain. When a Special Activity results in removing enemy pieces, consult the Piece Priorities table (8.5.8) to determine the piece(s) removed from the selected space.

## 8.9 NP Events Reference

NP Factions execute Events per the NP Eligibility table (8.5.2) only if the Event is both Critical and Effective. Critical Events are marked for each Faction with a shaded icon behind their Faction Eligibility symbol on the card, and the conditions for an Event to be counted as Effective are indicated on the NP Effective Events table (8.5.5). If given a choice of Event effects, an NP Faction will perform the first Effective option available, reading top down and left to right. NP Factions always use their own Space Selection Priorities table (8.5.6) to select the space(s) where the Event will be executed. Use the column on the Space Selection Priorities table that matches the Event action that makes this Event Effective for the NP Faction. If an NP Faction will execute multiple Effective actions for an Event, use the leftmost relevant column on the Space Selection Priorities table to select spaces for all Event actions. If an Event instructs an NP Faction to perform an Operation or Special Activity, draw through the Bonifacio deck until you find a card with instructions for that Operation or Special Activity. If in doubt, perform the Event actions in the way that seems most effective for the NP Faction.

## 8.11 NP Example of Play

Set up the board for the Standard Scenario, then place two additional Troops cubes and a Terror marker in Zamboanga, move one Police cube from Eastern Mindanao to Zamboanga and one Guerrilla from Eastern Mindanao to Western Mindanao (place an NPA Control marker in Western Mindanao), and replace one Police cube in Davao with a Troops cube. Then set up the Event deck as follows, from top to bottom:

1. Kampanyang Ahos (#18)
2. Bataan Nuclear Power Plant (#26)
3. Pope JPII Visit (#34)
4. Salvaging (#7)
5. San Miguel Corporation (#20)
6. Opposition Alliance (#27)
7. Chinese Aid (#24)
8. Manotoc Kidnapping (#32)
9. Evelio Javier Murdered (#16)
10. 1984 Parliamentary Election (#39)

All three Factions will be controlled by the Bonifacio Non-Player system for this Example of Play (usually a human player would be controlling at least one Faction, and possibly two). Remove all three Resource cylinders from play, as Non-Player Factions do not need to spend or track Resources. Then gather together the Bonifacio cards for each Faction, keeping them in alphabetical order for easy reference (in a real game the cards for all Non-Player Factions would be shuffled together), and find the Non-player Aid Card.

We will use the optional Personality and Act of Desperation rules for this Example of Play, so before beginning play we must select a Personality for each Non-Player Faction (any human players would select their own Personalities first as usual). Consult the Newsmakers section of the Election Round Instructions page of the Non-player Aid Card—this lists Personality and Act of Desperation selection priorities for each Non-Player Faction, based on the current board state:

- NP Government first checks if a roll of 1d3 is greater than the number of 2-Population spaces with Control and Support. There is only one such space (Cebu), but we roll a 1 and so proceed to the next priority, checking if a roll of 1d3 is less than or equal to the number of spaces with Control, no Support, and enemy pieces. There are two such spaces (Northern Luzon and Davao), and we roll a 2, so the NP Government selects General Fabian Ver (#43) as their Personality for the first Campaign. If we had rolled a 3 then they would have proceeded to the final priority and selected General Fidel V. Ramos (#44) instead.
- NP Reformers first check if the upcoming Campaign is the final one and if a roll of 1d6 is less than or equal to Available Activists—as the upcoming Campaign is not the final one, we do not need to bother making this roll. Next, they check if a roll of 2d6 is less than or equal to the number of Available Activists. There are eight Available Activists, and we roll a 6, so they select Corazon Aquino (#45) as their Personality for the first Campaign. If we had rolled 9 or higher then they would have selected Col. Gregorio Honasan (#48) instead.
- NP NPA first check if a roll of 1d6 is less than or equal to the number of Available NPA Bases. We roll a 3, and there are three Available NPA Bases, so they select Jose Maria Sison (#49) as their Personality for the first Campaign. If we had rolled a 4 or higher we would have proceeded down the rest of their Personality priority list, first checking if a roll of 3d6 was less than or equal to Government Control, then checking if a roll of 1d3 was less than or equal to the number of Cities without Resistance, and finally defaulting to selecting Rolly Kintanar (#51).

Now that we have selected Personalities for each Faction, we begin play by drawing the first Event card from the top of the deck as usual.

### Card 1: Kampanyang Ahos (#18)

**NPA Rally + Extort:** NPA are 1st Eligible, and consulting the NP Eligibility Table on the Non-player Aid Card we see that because the Event is not Critical for them (marked by the presence or absence of a silhouette behind the Faction symbol on the card) they will select Op + Special Activity (place their Eligibility cylinder in the '1st Faction Op + Special Activity' box now). We draw a Bonifacio card from the deck to determine which Operation they will perform, redrawing until we find a light red NPA card.

The first NPA card we draw is 'J'. Reading downwards from the top of the card, the first blue box asks if there is Government Control in any 2+ Population spaces—there is, so we follow the green arrow down to the next blue box, which asks if there are 4+ Guerrillas in any space with a Vulnerable Government Base (one with fewer than two cubes) or Government Control. There are not, so we follow the red arrow and flip the card over to its reverse side, labeled 'JJ'.

The first box on this card asks us to check if a roll of 1d6 is less than or equal to the number of Available Guerrillas. There are currently seven Available Guerrillas, so this roll will automatically succeed and we follow the green arrow to find an NPA Operation to execute: **Rally**. The Rally box instructs us to first place Bases in any eligible spaces with three or more Guerrillas, then in eligible spaces with two or more Guerrillas and no cubes, without yet making any Activation Number rolls. There are no such spaces—however the special NP instructions for Jose Maria Sison tell us to treat each of these as values as being one lower in Countryside spaces while the Personality is in play, and therefore we can place Bases in both Southern Luzon and Visayas. As there are sufficient Available Bases to place and we do not yet need to make Activation Number rolls, there is no need to check which space takes priority—we simply replace one Guerrilla (due to the Personality effect) with an NPA Base in each space (Southern Luzon and Visayas), and increase the NPA victory total by 2, to 5.

There are no more spaces where we can place Bases, so we proceed to the next instruction in this box, which is to place Guerrillas. We use the Place Guerrillas column of the NP NPA Space Selection Priorities table to decide where to do this, first checking for any Vulnerable NPA Bases (i.e., without at least one Underground Guerrilla). The newly placed Bases in Southern Luzon and Visayas are Vulnerable, but we have already Rallied in both spaces so we proceed to the next priority, which is Vulnerable Government Bases. There are no Vulnerable Government Bases, so we proceed to the third priority, Government Control. Manila and Cebu both have Government Control but are at Support, so are not eligible targets for Rally. There are several other spaces with Government Control and no Support, none of which are at Resistance (the fourth priority is to avoid spaces at Resistance), so we check which has the most Population (fifth priority in this column), which leaves us with Northern Luzon, Zamboanga, or Davao (each with Government Control, no Support, and 2 Population). Continuing down through the next rows, we see that Northern Luzon and Davao both have the most NPA Bases (one each), both spaces are Neutral, and Davao has the fewest Government pieces, so we finally select Davao as the first space to place Guerrillas. We place three Guerrillas there from Available (NPA Base + 2 Population), replacing Government Control with NPA Control and lowering the Government victory total by 2, from 18 to 16.

We now need to make an Activation Number roll to see if NP NPA will select an additional space for Rally, selecting an additional space if we roll greater than the Activation Number of 2 (in the top right corner of the card). We roll a 3 and so select an additional Rally space, which we already know will be Northern Luzon (using the same priorities as before). Place three more Guerrillas there (NPA Base + 2 Population), replace Government Control with NPA Control and lower the Government victory total by another 2, from 16 to 14. Our next Activation Number roll is 5, so we select a third space to place Guerrillas, this time placing one Guerrilla in Zamboanga. Our third Activation Number roll is a 4, and there are no more spaces with Government Control where we could Rally, so we move down to the next priorities on the chart: spaces not at Resistance (Western, Central, or Eastern Mindanao), then most Population, which narrows down our options to just Eastern Mindanao—place one Guerrilla and an NPA Control marker there now. Our fourth Activation Number roll is a 2, this time not greater than the Activation Number on the card, so the Rally Operation is now complete.

Next, we follow the black arrow down on the Bonifacio card to see which Special Activity will be performed. In this case the only option is Extort, which NP NPA will always use to Activate one Guerrilla (only) in an eligible space with the most Underground Guerrillas (and only selecting a space with an NPA Base if it has 2+ Underground Guerrillas). Northern Luzon and Davao are both eligible for Extort (no Government Control) and have the most Guerrillas, so we roll a die to decide between them and select Northern Luzon, flipping a single Guerrilla there to its Active side. (Note that NP NPA doesn't track Resources and so doesn't directly gain anything for doing this, but it has prevented any other Faction from performing a Special Activity on this card.) The NPA Operation and Special Activity is now finished, so we proceed to the next Eligible Faction, which is the Government.

**Government Limited Sweep:** Consulting the NP Eligibility Table, we see that a 2nd Eligible NP Faction going after a 1st Eligible Faction that performed Operation + Special Activity will still only perform the Event if it is marked Critical (it is not) and is also Effective, and will otherwise perform a Limited Operation (in order to stay Eligible for the next card). We place the Government Eligibility cylinder in the 'Limited Operation' box, and then draw a Bonifacio card from the deck to determine which Limited Operation they will perform, redrawing until we find a light blue Government card.

The first Government card we draw is 'C', which asks us to check if there are any 2+ Population spaces without Government Control (there are several), and then if a roll of 1d6 is less than or equal to the number of Available cubes. We roll a 6, which is greater than the four Available cubes, so we flip the card to its reverse side, labeled 'CC'.

Card 'CC' immediately tells us to perform a Sweep Operation, first using the Move Priorities table.

Consulting the Move Priorities table, Step #A instructs us to select a destination space using the Sweep Destination column of the NP Government Space Selection Priorities table. The first priority in this column is no Government Control, which eliminates only Manila, Cebu, and Zamboanga. The second priority is Vulnerable Base (any Faction), which narrows things down considerably, to either Southern Luzon or Visayas (both with a Vulnerable NPA Base). Both spaces have the same Population value, but Southern Luzon has the fewest enemy pieces (due to the Reformers Activist in Visayas), so we select Southern Luzon as our destination. Next, we move back to Step #B of the Move Priorities table, which instructs us to select an origin space with the most friendly cubes, in this case Manila or Zamboanga. However, there is a Protest in Manila, so it is not possible for Sweep to move any cubes out of it, and therefore we select Zamboanga as our origin space.

The 'keep' instructions on the Move Priorities table require us to keep at least two cubes in Zamboanga to avoid losing Government Control of a 2+ Population space. Consulting the 'get' instructions next, we will first move one Troop from Zamboanga to Southern Luzon in order to get Government Firepower (i.e., the number of NPA pieces that could be removed by an Assault Operation) equal to the number of NPA pieces in the space. We must move a Troop because Police cannot remove enemies in the Countryside, and thus contribute no Firepower there. We now have sufficient Government Firepower in the destination, and the next priority instructs us to get Government Control, which will require one additional cube—the Piece Priorities table indicates that the Government will always move a Police cube first if given a choice, so we move one Police from Zamboanga to Southern Luzon, replacing NPA Control with Government Control there and increasing the Government victory total by 1, from 14 to 15. The only other relevant 'get' instruction, to get one Police and one Troop, is already fulfilled, and there is now Government Control in the destination and no Guerrillas to Activate there, so the Sweep Operation is complete. As this is a Limited Operation we do not need to make an Activation Number roll to see if we will select any more spaces for Sweep, so we move on to the next eligible Faction, NP Reformers.

> NP Government Sweep moves one Troop and one Police from Zamboanga to Southern Luzon.

**Reformers Event:** Reformers are now 2nd Eligible, as Government performed a Limited Operation, and the Event is Critical for them (marked by a silhouette behind their Faction symbol), so they will perform it if it is Effective. We check this by looking at the Effective Events table, which tells us that an Event is Effective for NP Reformers if it removes any enemy pieces, which this one does.

We place the Reformers Eligibility cylinder in the '2nd Faction Op Only or Event' box and perform the shaded event text (NP Reformers and NP NPA will always perform the shaded Event text, and NP Government will always perform the unshaded text). To decide which four Police to remove from the map to satisfy this Event card we consult the Remove or Replace column of the NP Reformers Space Selection Priorities table, removing one cube at a time in case the priorities change while we do so. We first check for spaces with Government Control, then Vulnerable Reformers Bases, which selects Manila. We remove one Police from Manila, but this also removes Government Control (lower the Government victory total from 15 to 10), so we select a new space for the next Police cube. Cebu and Zamboanga both have Government Control and the most Population of the remaining spaces (there are no other Vulnerable Reformers Bases), so we apply the next condition (Support) and select Cebu and remove the only Police cube there. Then we have to select another new space for the third and fourth Police cubes, which will be Zamboanga (the only remaining space with Government Control, Police, and the most Population). We can take two Police from Zamboanga, removing Government Control there (lower the Government victory total from 10 to 8). All four removed Police cubes are placed to one side on the Event card, as instructed by the Event text. The Reformers turn is complete and all Eligible Factions have now acted, so we adjust Eligibility and reveal the next Event card.

### Card 2: Bataan Nuclear Power Plant (#26)

**Government Train + Enrich:** Government is the 1st (and only) Eligible Faction and this Event is not Critical for them, so they select Op + Special Activity (place their Eligibility cylinder in the '1st Faction Op + Special Activity' box now). The first light blue Government Bonifacio card we draw is 'D,' which instructs us to check whether there are Vulnerable Reformers pieces (Active Activists or a Reformers Base with no Activists) in any space with Police. There are Active Activists and Police in Manila, so we follow the green arrow to the next box, which instructs us to check whether a roll of 1d6 is less than or equal to the number of Available cubes. We roll a 4, and there are four Available cubes, so we again follow the green arrow and see that Government will perform a Train Operation, first selecting eligible spaces using the Place Cubes column of their Space Selection Priorities table.

The first priority in the Place Cubes column is spaces with no Government Control, which gives us Northern Luzon, Manila, Western Mindanao, Zamboanga, and Davao (of the spaces that Train could place cubes in, i.e., Countryside spaces with Bases and Cities). **Election Round:** The next card is an Election, so we pause to perform the Election Round (turn to this page of the Non-player Aid Card, and also turn to the Election Round page of the regular Player Aid foldout). We follow the usual steps in order, modified where relevant by the Non-Player instructions:

- **Victory?** First, we remove 1 Patronage due to the Strike in Davao (reduce Patronage from 5 to 4, and also lower the Government victory total from 15 to 14). Then we check to see if any Faction has won, noting that a human solitaire player may not win until the final Election Round, even if they exceed their victory threshold. No Faction is currently winning, so we proceed with the rest of the Election Round.
- **Resources:** As all three Factions are Non-Players and do not track Resources, we can skip this phase (remember to apply it for any human players as usual).
- **Support:**
  - *Civic Action:* NP Government rolls 1d6 to see how many markers (Terror, Strike, Protest) they can remove and steps they can shift towards Support, using the Shift Towards Support column of the Government Space Selection Priorities table to determine where they do this. In this case they roll a 2, but Manila is the only space eligible for Civic Action and is already at Support, so they just remove the Protest there (flip the Activists back to Inactive).
  - *Agitation:* NP NPA similarly roll 1d6 to see how many markers (Terror, Protest) they can remove and steps they can shift towards Resistance, using the Shift Towards Resistance column of the NPA Space Selection Priorities table. They roll a 5, but Visayas is their only possible target for Agitation (Davao and Eastern Mindanao are both already at Resistance), so they just set it to Resistance and increase their victory total by 1, to 9.
  - *Foundation:* NP Reformers will simply use the Place Bases column of the Reformers Space Selection Priorities table to determine where they place a Base (if possible). In this case they place a Base in Eastern Mindanao, with the most Population, most Activists, and fewest Government pieces (increase their victory total to 7).
- **Redeploy:** NP Government will use the Place Cubes column of the Government Space Selection Priorities table to break ties at any point in the Redeploy process, and will never move cubes if doing so would remove Government Control of a 2+ Population space.
  - NP Government will first redeploy Police to exceed enemy pieces in any Countryside spaces with Government Control and no Government Base, in this case just Southern Luzon, taking Police one at a time from spaces with the most Police. Zamboanga has the most Police, but moving any cubes from there would remove Government Control in a 2-Population space, so we cannot use any of those. Manila and Western Mindanao have one Police each, so we roll to select randomly and move the Police cube from Western Mindanao to Southern Luzon (add NPA Control in Western Mindanao).
  - Then they try to get one Police to each space with Government Control, but the only Police cube that could be moved is already in Manila, where it would be placed first, so we skip this step.
  - Next they move Troops to get Control in Manila and spaces with Government Bases, first moving Troops from Countryside spaces without Government Bases, then from spaces with the most Troops. There are three spaces with Government Bases and no Government Control (Cebu, Western Mindanao, and Davao), and Cebu has both a Vulnerable Base and more Population than Western Mindanao, so we prioritize Cebu, first moving in one Troops cube each from Southern Luzon and Central Mindanao, then moving in one Troops cube from Manila, which is enough to add Government Control (increase the Government victory total by 2, to 16). Western Mindanao and Davao now both have Vulnerable Government Bases, but Davao has more Population, so we move two more Troops cubes from Manila and the one remaining Troops cube from Western Mindanao to Davao, which is sufficient to add Government Control there (increase the Government victory total by 2, from 16 to 18). There are no more Troops cubes that can be moved, so this step is complete.
  - If additional Troops could be moved, they would first try to get one Troop in each space with Government Control (where Troops could be moved to), then use Place Cubes to move any Troops still remaining in Countryside spaces.
- **Newsmakers:** First, each player would secretly select a new Personality, then we will use the same priority tables as before to select new Personalities for each NP Faction, remembering that their previous choice is no longer able to be picked. Then, as this is the 1984 Parliamentary Election, we will follow the same process for selecting Act of Desperation cards for each NP Faction.
  - *Personalities:* Any result on a 1d3 will be greater than the number of 2-Population spaces with Government Control and Support, so NP Government will select Juan Ponce Enrile. The 1984 Campaign is upcoming and we roll a 1 on 1d6 (equal to the number of Available Activists), so NP Reformers will select Jaime Cardinal Sin. We then roll 14 on 3d6, less than Government Control, so NP NPA will select Rodolfo Salas.
  - *Acts of Desperation:* NPA is a Non-Player Faction, so NP Government will select COMELEC Hides Election Tampering and NP Reformers will select People Power. Reformers are also a Non-Player Faction, so NP NPA will select National Strike.
- **Reset:** We conduct the Reset phase as normal, remembering to return the 4 Police cubes on the Kampanyang Ahos card to Government Available, then shuffle the deck of Bonifacio cards before continuing play.

This Non-Player Example of Play is now complete, and you have seen examples of almost every possible Non-Player Operation and Special Activity. We skipped only three, but these are all very simple and can be explained briefly here, along with two other points that we have not yet mentioned:

- The Government Roundup Operation will use the Remove or Replace column of the Government Space Selection Priorities table to select spaces to remove Reformers pieces from.
- The NPA Attack Operation will use the Attack column of the NPA Space Selection Priorities table, first selecting spaces with three or more Guerrillas, then spaces where all Guerrillas are already Active, and never selecting any spaces with fewer than three Guerrillas where any Guerrillas are Underground.
- The Reformers Appeal Special Activity is always skipped in the 1981 Campaign (first Campaign of the Extended Scenario), and after that just removes one Activist from a space with the most Activists (deciding randomly in case of ties).
- During the 1981 Campaign (first Campaign of the Extended Scenario) NP Government will never use the Enrich Special Activity (as it is less effective due to the Election Momentum effect).
- During the 1984 Campaign (final Campaign of both scenarios) Activation Numbers are all one lower for NP Reformers, as noted in the top right of their Bonifacio cards.

You now know all that you need to use the Bonifacio system to play People Power either solitaire or with one other player (using an NP Faction in place of the third player).

## 8.12 NP System Design Notes

I stumbled into designing this non-player system almost by accident, when I foolishly suggested to Jason Carr that I could begin working on some of the simpler aspects so that the foundations would be ready for him to build on when he had time. Little did I know then that Jason never has time, and once he saw that I was making steady progress he was happy to leave me to it. I already had some experience testing the Tây Sơn non-player system for Fall of Saigon, and even came up with the name 'Jacquard' as a generic term for the COIN card-based non-player framework, but working on a new system from scratch was an entirely different ballgame. Luckily I was able to draw on plenty of support and advice from Jason and Bruce Mansfield, the original designer of the first three iterations of the Jacquard system, Arjuna, Trưng, and Tây Sơn.

The first thing that became apparent was that the Non-Player Eligibility instructions would need to be adjusted to fit with People Power's new three-faction Sequence of Play, and while this was fairly straightforward it did require a change in mindset with regard to Events. In the previous four-faction iterations of the Jacquard system it was relatively common for 2nd Eligible NP Factions to perform somewhat weaker Events, or even for 1st Eligible NP Factions to forego a Special Activity in order to block the next Faction from performing a powerful 'Critical' Event. In People Power neither of these options are so appealing, as instead of performing a weaker Event you can perform a Limited Operation and remain Eligible on the next turn (where hopefully you will be 1st Eligible), and choosing Operation Only to block an Event carries the heavy cost of allowing 2nd Eligible to perform a Special Activity. The end result is that 2nd Eligible NP Factions in People Power often choose to perform a Limited Operation, just as a player would, and that each NP Faction only has a fairly small range of Events that they will choose to perform, all of which are marked as 'Critical'. The good news for you is that this makes it very straightforward to determine what kind of action an NP Faction will perform: just check if the current Event card is marked as Critical for them, in which case they will play it, and if not they will perform either an Operation + Special Activity (if possible) or a Limited Operation (if not). Easy!

The next challenge we faced was maintaining the very delicate balance of People Power's ecosystem while still ensuring that each Non-Player Faction would aggressively defend its own interests. For NP Government this was not so difficult, but for NP NPA and NP Reformers it was a careful balancing act to get them cooperating well enough to keep the Government under pressure, while still aiming to increase their own scores and occasionally hit each other. The system also had to be capable of handling both one player (solitaire) and two player (where one NP Faction takes the place of a third player) games, which we managed in the end by introducing a number of priority checks that only activate against a player-controlled Faction. So, if you are playing Government solitaire against NP NPA and NP Reformers, they will both tend to prioritize targeting you, whereas if Reformers were controlled by a second human player then NP NPA will still focus primarily on Government but take more opportunities to lower Opposition and remove Activists than they would otherwise. Against a solitaire Reformers player, on the other hand, NP Government will focus primarily on the human-controlled Reformers, and NP NPA will still contribute a bit to the shared struggle against the Government, but also won't pass up any opportunities to remove Opposition. The way I like to think about this is that the NP Factions 'assume' that a human player is better at the game than they are (which is probably true), so if there are two NP Factions then they will gang up on the human player (as two weaker human players might agree to do), while if there are two human players then the NP Faction will split their attention more naturally between them (still tending to prioritize Government if it is player-controlled, as is necessary for the game's balance).

Once we had figured out these major issues there still remained many months of tinkering with smaller details, aided eventually by a handful of very dedicated and enthusiastic playtesters (without the likes of whom none of our games would be possible). At this stage of the design I began to see working on the non-player system as a game in its own right: an extremely intricate and frustrating solitaire game where you are trying to maintain an incredibly complex and unstable system, a kind of Rube Goldberg machine where the smallest tweak will create ripples that might cause the whole thing to collapse. This is fun and rewarding in its own way of course, but definitely challenging and time consuming – if this sounds like the kind of game you might be interested in playing, please contact GMT One to volunteer for future design work! As I write these notes I am currently in the very final stages of the 'game', writing up the Non-Player Example of Play, where again the slightest adjustment to initial conditions will cascade through the whole piece of work in unexpected and inconvenient ways…

*— Joe Dewhurst, Glasgow, UK, September 2022*

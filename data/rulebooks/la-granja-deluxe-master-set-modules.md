# La Granja: Deluxe Master Set Module Book

## Table of Contents

*The Deluxe Master Set adds 12 brand new modules to the game, ranging in complexity and scope from just a few tiles or cards usable by players of all experience levels to modules which challenge even the most skilled and experienced players. You can freely mix and match the various modules. They are all compatible with one another. Below you will find a brief overview of all modules, followed by an in-depth description of the rules governing each module. All other rules from the base game remain unchanged, unless otherwise noted.*

## Module Summary

**Betwixt** (by Andrei Novac) — This module adds a layer of direct interaction between players, as well as an extra location where goods can be delivered. It is best for experienced players who want to add even more options to the game, as well as an extra degree of direct competition between players. While it works well with 2 players, it is recommended for games with 3 or 4 players.

**Broken Roofs** (by ode.) — This module features a new type of roof tile: the broken roof. A broken roof has no ability, but comes with another advantage: it costs 1 silver regardless of the round it is bought in. Add this module if you are looking for a slightly easier, more friendly game.

**Bustling Town** (by Adam Kwapiński) — This is one of the biggest and most significant modules that changes the game in multiple ways. It remodels the revenue spaces, and introduces buildings which can be added to the main board. By offering direct profits for placing markers adjacent to buildings in the market, this module also introduces stronger area control aspects to La Granja. It is recommended only for experienced players.

**Colchonera** (by MiKe) — This module offers players a new delivery option, a new reason to move up the siesta track (even if becoming the new start player is no longer an option), as well as a new way to create scoring opportunities for the end of the game. Recommended for players with at least moderate experience with La Granja.

**Counselors & Outlaws** (by Stefan Feld) — This double module allows players to give an extra function to revenue dice, and it is up to them to decide if they want an easier game with more resources, or one that is more punishing and a bit more random. Use the counselors if you want an easier game, perhaps even one geared towards more casual players, or introduce the outlaws to make La Granja more of a vicious and unpredictable game.

**Donkey Song** (by Andrei Novac) — This module gives more prominence to the siesta track, focusing especially on spaces previously not as attractive as others - those that offer no new victory point benefit when entering them. Now players have reasons to make precise siesta track movements, along with a tableau of new donkey tiles to make use of during the Transportation Phase. Recommended for experienced players.

**Grand Markers** (by ode.) — This module adds an extra layer of decisions to the delivery processes of the game. Using the grand markers adds extra functionality to craft buildings, and allows players to create stronger, more lasting positions on the market area of the board.

**Hired Hands** (by Błażej Kubacki) — Hired Hands is a module that introduces more flexibility into the game, by allowing players to make use of special workers that can help with deliveries and keeping their markers on the market board. It is recommended for players who want to be able to make more deliveries, and easier use of craft buildings.

**Ladies of La Granja** (by Karina Weening and Dirk Schröder) — Ladies of La Granja introduces asymmetrical player powers to the game. It is recommended for experienced players looking to help their strategies with unique abilities.

**Long Distance Trade** (by ode.) — Long Distance Trade opens up a new option of using goods to receive immediate victory point rewards without having to spend deliveries, and introduces a new way to score leftover goods at the end of the game. As delivery spaces are limited, players have to race in order to take the best positions, with some of them offering trade commodities to be used during the game. Recommended for experienced players.

**New Farm Cards** (by Kevin Hendranata, Julius Kündiger, Tony Boydell, David Waybright, Rainer Åhlfors, Filip Głowacz, Matthias Nagy, Martin Zeeb, Rebecca Zeeb, Isra Cendrero, Shei Santos, Nick Case, MiKe, and ode.) — This module adds 60 new farm cards to the game. While six of these cards had been previously released in the form of a small promo pack, the majority of the cards are brand new to the La Granja Deluxe Master Set, introducing new helper abilities to enrich and enhance your gameplay experience. These cards can easily be used by players of all experience levels.

**Trade Boards** (by Andrei Novac) — Trade Boards introduces both direct interaction between players, as well as a chance to manipulate revenue dice (or gain a bit of extra cash). This new opportunity to deliver resources to other players can make the game a bit easier, but still requires at least one game of base La Granja to easily integrate into the game.

## Betwixt

*by Andrei Novac*

This module adds a layer of direct interaction between players, as well as an extra location where goods can be delivered. It is best for experienced players who want to add even more options to the game, as well as an extra degree of direct competition between players. While it works well with 2 players, it is recommended for games with 3 or 4 players.

### Components

* 8 betwixt cards

### Changes in Setup

Set up the game following normal rules. Then:

1. Shuffle all betwixt cards.
2. Draw and place one card between every 2 players. In 2-player games, place 2 cards between the players.
3. Return any unused betwixt cards to the game box. They will not be used for the remainder of the game.

### Rules

When you use deliveries from any source, you may choose to deliver to any of the betwixt cards that are between you and the players on your immediate left or right.

When delivering, you must go in order from the goods closest to you on the card to the goods that are furthest.

Once all goods have been delivered, the card is considered fully supplied and is immediately resolved as follows:

* If both players contributed, the player who delivered more goods receives the reward on the left (with the number 1), and the other player receives the reward on the right (with the number 2). The active player receives their reward immediately. The other player takes the betwixt card and places it in their play area. Their reward will not be taken on the active player's turn. Instead, on the next turn of the player with the betwixt card they will gain the reward at any time during that turn. After taking the reward discard the betwixt card to the box.
* If only one player contributed, they receive both rewards. In this case both rewards are taken immediately.
* If the game ends before the card is fully supplied, neither player receives any of the depicted rewards.

Rewards are always resolved from left to right (the higher value before the lower). Once rewards are distributed, players take their markers off the card and the fully supplied betwixt card is removed from the game. Do not replace it with a new one.

*Example: Purple has just delivered the last good needed to fully supply their and Blue's betwixt card. Purple delivered 4 (A) out of 7 goods needed, while Blue delivered only 3 (B). The card is immediately resolved: Purple scores 6 victory points and receives a trade commodity (C). Blue takes the card and on their turn receives 3 victory points and a trade commodity (D) as well. After all rewards have been resolved, Purple and Blue take their markers back to their player areas and the card is discarded. Blue can now deliver only to a betwixt card shared with another player (not Purple).*

## Broken Roofs

*by ode.*

This module features a new type of roof tile: the broken roof. A broken roof has no ability, but comes with another advantage: it costs 1 silver regardless of the round it is bought in. Add this module if you are looking for a slightly easier, more friendly game.

### Components

* 6 broken roof tiles

### Changes in Setup

After setting up the regular roof tiles, stack all broken roofs next to the main board, and add 1 to the roof tile offer for the first round, flipping it face up. During each Scoring Phase, after flipping new roof tiles face up, add 1 broken roof tile (making sure that there is exactly one broken roof tile available each round).

### Rules

A broken roof tile may be purchased just like any other roof tile. It always costs 1 silver (regardless of the round). When placed on the player board, it is immediately flipped face down. A broken roof tile may never be flipped face up. It does not have an ability, but otherwise works exactly the same way as any other, regular roof tiles.

## Bustling Town

*by Adam Kwapiński*

This is one of the biggest and most significant modules that changes the game in multiple ways. It remodels the revenue spaces, and introduces buildings which can be added to the main board. By offering direct profits for placing markers adjacent to buildings in the market, this module also introduces stronger area control aspects to La Granja. It is recommended only for experienced players.

### Components

* 10 building cards
* 10 building markers
* 6 double-sided revenue tiles

### Changes in Setup

Set up the game following normal rules. Then:

1. Pick one side of the revenue tiles to use, either A or B. Mixing sides is not recommended.
2. Shuffle the revenue tiles and place them on the printed revenue spaces, covering them.
3. Shuffle all building cards together and form a face down deck. Place it next to the main board, leaving some space for an offer.
4. Draw 3 building cards and place them face up in a row next to their deck, forming a building offer.
5. Place the matching building marker on each revealed card. Keep the remaining buildings aside, as some of them will be needed later.

### Revenue Tiles — Side A

* **A01a** — Perform 1 delivery OR take 1 pig.
* **A02a** — Move 3 steps on the siesta track OR play or draw a card.
* **A03a** — Take 1 harvest good OR perform a building action. *The building action may not be chosen as a common third action. Players must use the alternative provided on the revenue tile.*
* **A04a** — Upgrade any 2 goods OR take 1 harvest good and upgrade any 1 good OR take 2 different harvest goods.
* **A05a** — Take 4 silver OR move 2 steps on the siesta track.
* **A06a** — Receive 1 harvest good on each of up to 2 different empty dens.

### Revenue Tiles — Side B

* **A01b** — Carry out 1 free delivery and (optionally) 1 extra delivery for a cost of 2 silver OR play or draw a card.
* **A02b** — Pay 2 silver to upgrade any 3 goods OR take 4 silver.
* **A03b** — Take 1 pig OR perform a building action. *See the Building Action section below for more details. The building action may not be chosen as a common third action. Players must use the alternative provided on the revenue tile.*
* **A04b** — Upgrade any 2 goods OR take 2 silver and upgrade any 1 good OR take 4 silver.
* **A05b** — Take 2 different harvest goods OR spend 1 harvest good to move 3 steps on the siesta track.
* **A06b** — Take 1 pig OR exchange 1 trade commodity choosing one of the regular exchange options and gain 2 victory points.

### Building Action

When you choose a building action you may either elect to construct a new building, or to use the special ability of one of the existing buildings. When you decide to construct a new building, perform the following steps:

1. Select a building card from the offer, and place its building marker on any legal space on a corner where 3 adjacent market spaces intersect. If there are already any other building markers on the board, the new building marker may not share any market space with any of the already existing ones.

   **Exception:** When playing with 2 or 3 players, every intersection of 2 tiles that is adjacent to the outer edge of the market area is a valid building spot.
2. Resolve the special ability of that building.
3. Choose one of the craft buildings (open or closed) that does not have a building card placed next to it, and place the card in its dedicated card space. From now on, this building is considered adjacent to this craft building for the purposes of checking presence and domination.

*Example: In a 4-player game, as their action, Blue decides to build a church. There is already one building marker on the board (A), so when placing the church, Blue has to choose an intersection that will not have any common market spaces with an existing building. Even if the 2 marked intersections do not share a space with any other building marker (B), it is still not a legal move to place a building marker there, as only intersections of 3 tiles can be used to place building markers.*

Once built, building markers or cards may not be moved. There are only 6 spaces where a building card can be placed. This means that there can never be more than 6 buildings constructed at the same time. Once all 6 spaces for building cards are occupied by cards, players may only use the building action to use a special ability of a building already on the board.

When you choose the building action and decide not to build, you may use the building's special ability (the same way you would if you had just built it).

### Activating Buildings

Before step 1 of the Scoring Phase, before the market spaces and siesta track are scored, all building cards activate. Resolve each building card in the order of the numbers of the craft buildings they are assigned to, starting from lowest and proceeding to highest.

### Domination and Presence

When resolving a building, perform its special ability. Each building will grant its special ability either to the single player who has domination by majority of their markers in market spaces adjacent to the building, or to all players with presence.

**Domination:** the player with the most markers adjacent to the building marker (including any markers on the player ownership area on the building card), is the beneficiary of the building's special ability. In case of a tie between the players with the most markers, nobody gets to use the ability.

**Presence:** every player with at least 1 marker adjacent to the building marker (including markers on the player ownership area on the building card) receives the special ability. Carefully read the description of the special ability, as it will tell you how benefits are calculated for each player with presence.

### Refilling Building Cards

Immediately after revealing new roof tiles, draw and add one card to the building offer. Skip this step if all possible building spaces are filled with buildings already.

### Revenue Tiles Without Buildings

This module may be played without using the buildings (although it is not recommended). Players may simply leave the building cards in the box and ignore the action that allows them to construct or use the abilities of buildings.

## Colchonera

*by MiKe*

This module offers players a new delivery option, a new reason to move up the siesta track, as well as a new way to create scoring opportunities for the end of the game. Recommended for players with at least moderate experience with La Granja.

### Components

* 1 Colchonera sideboard

### Changes in Setup

After placing the main board, place the Colchonera sideboard near the main board, easily accessible by every player.

### Rules

Whenever a player performs a delivery, they have a new delivery option - they may deliver the good to the Colchonera sideboard. Depending on the position of the player on the siesta track, they may deliver only specific goods. After making a delivery to the Colchonera, the player immediately receives a reward, depending on the delivery space they filled.

* Regardless of their position on the siesta track, a player may deliver 1 silver to the delivery space of their color. This delivery grants no reward, and is available to each player only once per game.
* A player that is in the 1 victory point area of the siesta track (or higher), may deliver 1 olive, grain or grape to immediately receive 1 silver.
* A player that is in the 2 victory point area of the siesta track (or higher), may deliver 1 food, pig, or wine to immediately receive 1 olive, grain or grape.
* A player that is in the 3 victory point area of the siesta track, may deliver 1 cured meat or 1 trade commodity to immediately receive 1 food, pig, or wine.

**Important:** A player may deliver to the Colchonera:

* up to 1 good in the Revenue Phase by using an appropriate die or helper offering an immediate delivery,
* up to 1 good using a free delivery in the Transportation Phase,
* up to 1 good using a purchased delivery in the Transportation Phase.

In other words, a player may never deliver more than 3 times per round to the Colchonera sideboard.

*Example: Green has reached the 2 victory points area on the siesta track (A). They deliver 1 olive to the Colchonera sideboard using a free delivery during the Transportation Phase (B). Then, using a purchased delivery, Green delivers 1 food (C). Green had more free and purchased deliveries available, but each of them could be used no more than once per round by a player to deliver goods to the Colchonera sideboard, so Green ends the round with 2 of their player markers on the Colchonera sideboard (D).*

### New Trade Commodity Option

The trade commodity can be returned to the supply to gain 2 upgrades, 2 steps on the siesta track, or a combination of both (1 upgrade and 1 step up on the siesta track). This option is depicted on the Colchonera sideboard, and only available when using this module.

### End Game Scoring

In addition to the normal scoring of silver, players gain victory points from the Colchonera sideboard for each of their player markers:

* For 1/2/3 markers on the Colchonera the owner of the markers gains 1/3/6 victory points.
* For every player marker beyond the third, the owner scores 3 additional victory points.

*Example: With 5 of their player markers on the Colchonera sideboard, Green scores 6 victory points (for the first 3 markers), and then 3 victory points each for the fourth and fifth player marker. Green scores a total of 6+3+3=12 victory points.*

## Counselors & Outlaws

*by Stefan Feld*

This double module allows players to give an extra function to revenue dice, and it is up to them to decide if they want an easier game with more resources, or one that is more punishing and a bit more random. Use the counselors if you want an easier game, perhaps even one geared towards more casual players, or introduce the outlaws to make La Granja more of a vicious and unpredictable game.

### Components

* 4 double-sided Counselors & Outlaws mini boards
* 5 gray dice

### Changes in Setup

1. Give each player one Counselors & Outlaws mini board.
2. The players should first decide if they will be playing using the counselors side (day) or the outlaws side (night). All players should use the same side of the mini boards.
3. When preparing revenue dice, use both white and gray dice.
4. When playing with the counselors, prepare the revenue dice as follows:
   * 4 players — 4 gray and 5 white dice
   * 3 players — 3 gray and 4 white dice
   * 2 players — 2 gray and 3 white dice
5. When playing with the outlaws, prepare the revenue dice as follows:
   * 4 players — 5 gray and 4 white dice
   * 3 players — 4 gray and 3 white dice
   * 2 players — 3 gray and 2 white dice
6. After preparing the pool of revenue dice, put all the unused dice (both white and gray) back to the box as they will not be used in this game.

### Rules

Whenever a player selects a gray die during the Revenue Phase:

**With counselors:** the player receives both the revenue from the revenue space, and the reward associated with the corresponding space on your counselors board. To receive the additional reward, the player must pay either 1 silver or 1 victory point. If a player is unable to pay, they can still take the gray die, but they may not receive the reward associated with the die value. When receiving the reward, a player may choose to pay and receive it before or after resolving the revenue space. After receiving or executing the reward, a player places one of their player markers on the corresponding space on the mini board. Until the end of the Revenue Phase, taking another gray die of that value has no additional effect (it is treated as a white die). At the end of the Revenue Phase, players should remove all of their markers from the mini boards.

*Example: Green takes a value 4 revenue die (A). Since the value 4 space on their mini board is empty, they decide to pay 1 silver (B) to place the green player marker there, and immediately receive 1 grain (C).*

**With outlaws:** The player carries out the following 3 steps:

1. Receives the revenue from the revenue space.
2. Pays the penalty shown on the corresponding spot on their mini board or loses 1 victory point. If neither is possible, the gray die cannot be taken (unless there are no white revenue dice available).
3. Places 1 of their markers on the corresponding spot on their mini board.

From now on, taking another gray die of that value will only trigger Step 1.

Anytime during their turn a player may remove 3 directly adjacent markers from their mini board to receive 1 trade commodity. The spaces 1 and 6 are considered adjacent.

*Example: Green takes a value 1 revenue die (A), and decides to pay 1 victory point (B) to place a green marker on the value 1 space on their mini board (C). From now on, at any time during their turn, Green may remove the 3 adjacent markers (D) from their mini board (note that 1 and 6 are considered adjacent!) to receive 1 trade commodity (E).*

## Donkey Song

*by Andrei Novac*

This module gives more prominence to the siesta track, focusing especially on spaces previously not as attractive as others - those that offer no new victory point benefit when entering them. Now players have reasons to make precise siesta track movements, along with a tableau of new donkey tiles to make use of during the Transportation Phase. Recommended for experienced players.

### Components

* 4 level 1 (blue) Donkey Song tiles
* 4 level 2 (orange) Donkey Song tiles
* 4 level 3 (purple) Donkey Song tiles
* 1 Donkey Song display

### Changes in Setup

Set up the game following normal rules. Then:

1. Place the Donkey Song display next to the main board in such a way that colors on it exactly match colors pre-printed on the right of the siesta track.
2. Place all the level 1 Donkey Song tiles face up on the blue section on the display.
3. Place all the level 2 Donkey Song tiles face up on the orange section on the display.
4. Place all the level 3 Donkey Song tiles face up on the purple section on the display.

### Rules

During the Scoring Phase, when players receive points based on their position on the siesta track, each player high enough on the track may also pay 1 silver to receive a Donkey Song tile:

* A player with the marker in the blue section of the siesta track may pay 1 silver to take any available blue Donkey Song tile.
* A player with the marker on the orange section of the siesta track may pay 1 silver to take any available orange Donkey Song tile.
* A player with the marker on the purple section of the siesta track may pay 1 silver to take any available purple Donkey Song tile.

A player is allowed to buy only one tile per Scoring Phase, and only from the section their marker is on. Only if their section does not hold any Donkey Song tiles, is a player allowed to buy a tile from the section immediately below - if any are still available. A player below the 1 victory point section of the siesta track does not have access to any Donkey Song tiles.

The regular donkey tiles are separate from your Donkey Song tiles. Regardless of which donkey tiles you use in the game, you still reclaim any used regular donkey tiles (but never used Donkey Song tiles) just as you do in the regular game.

### Using Donkey Song Tiles

A Donkey Song tile may be used instead of a normal donkey tile. However, the tile is single use - after it is used, it is returned to the game box. Its effects can always be executed in any order, but every effect type has to be resolved in full at once. Using a Donkey Song tile offers the following benefits (one tile per bullet group):

* Move 3 steps on the siesta track. Perform up to 2 free deliveries. You may upgrade 1 resource.
* Move 1 step on the siesta track. Receive 3 silver. Perform up to 4 purchased deliveries during Step 3 of the Transportation Phase. This has no influence on your purchased deliveries during Step 4.
* Move 2 steps on the siesta track. Perform up to 3 free deliveries. Each of those deliveries must be to a different location. *You may deliver to different cards or buildings of any type. No 2 goods from these deliveries may end up on the same goods track.*
* Move 4 times on the siesta track. Perform up to 2 free deliveries.
* Perform 2 free deliveries. Those deliveries will yield you 1 victory point each. Perform up to 2 purchased deliveries during Step 3 of the Transportation Phase. This has no influence on your purchased deliveries during Step 4.
* Move 3 steps on the siesta track. Perform up to 2 free deliveries. You may upgrade up to 2 resources.
* Move 4 steps on the siesta track. Perform up to 2 free deliveries. Receive 2 silver.
* Perform 3 free deliveries. Draw or play a card.
* Move 2 steps on the siesta track. You may upgrade 1 resource. Perform up to 3 free deliveries.
* Move 1 step on the siesta track. You may pay 2 silver to move 3 additional steps on the siesta track.
* Perform 3 free deliveries. Receive 3 silver. Draw or play a card.
* Perform up to 4 purchased deliveries during Step 3 of the Transportation Phase. This has no influence on your purchased deliveries during Step 4.
* Perform up to 4 free deliveries. Each of those deliveries must be made to a different location. *You may deliver to different cards or buildings of any type. No 2 goods from these deliveries may end up on the same goods track.*

## Grand Markers

*by ode.*

This module adds an extra layer of decisions to the delivery processes of the game. Using the grand markers adds extra functionality to craft buildings, and allows players to create stronger, more lasting positions on the market area of the board.

### Components

* 8 grand player markers (2 markers in each player color). Each grand marker is double-sided, with a market barrow side (left) and a craft building side (right).

### Changes in Setup

1. During setup, each player receives their 2 grand markers together with the rest of their player components.
2. Instead of placing a normal marker of each player on the starting positions on the market, place 1 grand marker. Players will thus start with 1 grand marker on the market.
3. Leave the second grand marker in each player's personal supply.

### Rules

A grand marker can only be placed on a craft building or a market barrow. When a player performs 2 simultaneous deliveries to the same craft building or market barrow and the demand spaces that are supplied this way are adjacent, the player may choose to place the grand marker from their supply on the demand spaces in a way that it covers both spaces at the same time.

**Important:** The goods are spent as normal. So instead of placing two normal markers on the demand spaces the player uses the grand marker.

*Example: During the Transportation Phase, Purple uses 2 free deliveries to deliver goods to the market barrow card on their farm (A). Purple decides to place the grand marker, so the delivered goods are removed (B), and a grand marker is placed over 2 sequential spaces on the market barrow card (C).*

When a craft building or a market barrow with a grand marker is scored, the following rules apply:

**Craft building:** In addition to all normal effects from scoring a craft building, the player scores 1 additional victory point. Furthermore, the player may use the immediate effect of a craft marker from an adjacent building (instead of the one just fully supplied) even if they do not have earned that marker yet, or if that craft building is closed. The grand marker is returned to the personal supply of the player and can be used again right away.

**Market:** The player may send the grand marker to the market instead of a normal marker. The grand marker will be placed like a normal marker. When the grand marker removes any markers from other players it scores 2 victory points instead of one for each marker removed.

*Example: The player removes 2 markers from other players. When this is caused by a grand marker the player gets 4 victory points instead of 2 like normal.*

### Additional Rules

A grand marker may be removed from the market only by another grand marker. Regular player markers have no effect on it. A worker (from the Hired Hands module) does not protect a grand marker from another grand marker.

If the grand marker is still on the market in the Scoring Phase the player gains 2 victory points for the marker.

A player may not place 2 grand markers on the same market barrow card or on the same craft building.

Remember that players may not use their last free delivery and their first purchased delivery to place a grand marker, as they are not considered to create a single sequence of deliveries.

Grand markers may not be used on the Colchonera board or the Long Distance Trade board. A player may use a grand marker on a Betwixt card, in which case the grand marker grants the same benefits, as when it is placed on a market barrow card.

In the unlikely event that a player must place a regular marker on a market space with a victory point value exclusively occupied by grand markers, the player must select a value lower than the one printed on the card and place their marker there. The player still receives the victory points printed on the card they have just fully supplied but any opponent markers are removed using the value of the actual market space taken.

## Hired Hands

*by Błażej Kubacki*

Hired Hands is a module that introduces more flexibility into the game, by allowing players to make use of special workers that can help with deliveries and keeping their markers on the market board. It is recommended for players who want to be able to make more deliveries, and easier use of craft buildings.

### Components

* 8 workers (2 in each of 4 player colors)
* 4 donkey tiles

### Changes in Setup

Set up the game normally, with one change: when giving players their set of 4 donkey tiles, instead of a donkey tile with 4 deliveries, give each player a donkey tile that allows for 4 steps on the siesta track.

Additionally: give each player 2 worker meeples in their player color. For now, they should be kept next to their player boards.

### Rules

Whenever a player drafts a revenue die, in addition to performing the corresponding action, they may decide to use its value to send a worker from their player area to:

* A market barrow card attached to their board.
* A craft building on the main board (open or still closed).
* A market marker that belongs to them.
* A betwixt card (if using the Betwixt module).

To be able to place a worker on any of those, the value of the die must either be the same as the value on the market barrow card, the craft building, or the market space the worker is being sent to (or equal to one of the two values on the betwixt card), or it must be value 1, which can be used to match any number (in other words, 1s are wild for this purpose). However, in order to use a value 1 to send out a worker, the player must pay 1 silver.

**Important:** A player is not allowed to send their worker using the value of the 3rd die.

*Example: A player drafts a value 3 revenue die (A). They may take their worker from their player area and place them on a market barrow (B) with a value of 3, the number 3 craft building (C) or their market stall placed on a value 3 space on the market (D). Since the Betwixt module is not in use, these are the only 3 options available.*

Every market barrow card and every market space may have only one worker assigned. A craft building and a betwixt card may have multiple workers assigned, but each of those workers must belong to a different player.

*Example: Later in the game a player draws a value 5 revenue die. They want to send their worker to one of the available spaces. They already have one worker placed on their market barrow, so they cannot choose to place another worker there. They also do not have any market stall of value 5. This means that they are only able to send their worker to the value 5 craft building.*

You may use your workers in the following ways:

* A worker on your market barrow may be moved back to your player area to perform a free delivery to that card (in the Carrying out Free Deliveries step of the Transportation Phase).
* A worker on an open craft building may be moved back to your player area to perform a free delivery to that craft building (in the Carrying out Free Deliveries step of the Transportation Phase).
* A worker on a closed craft building may be moved back to your player board to temporarily open that craft building to deliveries for you throughout the Transportation Phase of the current game round (see below).
* A worker on a market stall may be removed instead of the market stall, whenever the market stall would have to be removed (the removing player does not receive any points).

If a player decides to make any deliveries to a closed craft building, they should lay down their worker as they start their free deliveries. From now on until the end of the Transportation Phase, that player may deliver to that craft building, as if it was open (but only for that player, and only for the duration of the current round).

If a closed craft building is fully supplied by any player while still closed, that player receives rewards normally. The fully supplied building opens immediately. However, another craft building is not opened, and the player also does not receive the extra victory point normally awarded for opening a new craft building.

Immediately after revealing new roof tiles, each player (in turn order) may return any of their workers back to their player area. All workers that are lying down must be returned to player areas. Returning a worker does not have any effect beyond simply making them ready to be sent out during the next turn.

## Ladies of La Granja

*by Karina Weening and Dirk Schröder*

Ladies of La Granja introduces asymmetrical player powers to the game. It is recommended for experienced players looking to help their strategies with unique abilities.

### Components

* 6 Ladies of La Granja cards
* 1 Ana's special die
* 1 Felipa's helper holder

### Changes in Setup

Set up the game following normal rules. Then:

1. Shuffle the Ladies of La Granja cards and reveal a number equal to the number of players plus 1.
2. Before the first Farm Phase, in reverse turn order, each player selects 1 available Lady of La Granja card, and places it next to their farm.
3. Players should take any extra components belonging to their Ladies of La Granja cards.
4. Any unused Ladies of La Granja cards should be removed from the game.

### Rules

Your Lady of La Granja card provides a special ability, which is active during the whole game. Some ladies come with their special components:

**Nina:** You have 1 free upgrade each round. After using this upgrade, flip Nina's exhaustion marker to the inactive side. Flip Nina's exhaustion marker back to the active side at the end of the Scoring Phase.

**Carmen:** On the Carmen card, there are 2 additional spaces for roof tiles. When you buy the 6th or 7th Roof, place it on the Carmen card.

**Felipa:** You have 1 additional space for a helper. Place this frame below your farm. As long as the frame token is empty (you have fewer than 4 helpers) keep an exhaustion token on Felipa. You may flip it to the inactive side to gain either 1 silver or 1 extra free delivery each round. Remove the exhaustion marker from the card at the end of the Scoring Phase.

**Ana:** You have your own revenue die. Add this die to the dice pool (thus making the number of revenue dice 1 higher) and roll normally during the Revenue Phase. You can use it together with (and in addition to) the first or second revenue die you draft to perform an extra action. After you choose this die (or if you do not perform its action with the first or second revenue die you draft), place it on Ana's card until the next Revenue Phase. The value of this die cannot be manipulated by Helper effects belonging to other players.

**Reina:** Once per round, you can exchange 1 victory point for 4 silver. After performing this exchange, flip Reina's exhaustion marker token to the inactive side. Flip Reina's exhaustion marker back to the active side at the end of the Scoring Phase.

**Savannah:** Check the End Round Scoring section.

### Changes in Scoring

**End round scoring:** The player holding the Savannah card receives additional benefits while scoring the siesta track:

* 1 harvest good (olive, grain or grape) if they score 1 victory point from the siesta track.
* 1 trade commodity if they score 2 or 3 victory points from the siesta track.

**End game scoring:** The player holding the Nina card sells upgraded goods on their farm as follows:

* food = 3 silver
* wine = 4 silver
* cured meat = 5 silver

## Long Distance Trade

*by ode.*

Long Distance Trade opens up a new option of using goods to receive immediate victory point rewards without having to spend deliveries, and introduces a new way to score leftover goods at the end of the game. As delivery spaces are limited, players have to race in order to take the best positions, with some of them offering trade commodities to be used during the game. Recommended for experienced players.

### Components

* 1 Long Distance Trade sideboard

### Changes in Setup

After placing the main board, place the Long Distance Trade sideboard near the main board, easily accessible by every player.

### Rules

When using this module, players should resolve the Scoring Phase in player order. During their turn each player scores victory points for the market and the siesta track as normal. Then, they may return exactly 1 set of resources to the supply to place their marker on the appropriate unoccupied space of the Long Distance Trade sideboard:

* If the player returns 3 silver to the supply, they place their marker on 1 of these spaces and receive 2 victory points.
* If the player returns 3 olives, 3 grain, or 3 grapes to the supply, they place their marker on 1 of these spaces and receive 2 victory points and 1 trade commodity.
* If the player returns 3 food, 3 pigs, or 3 wine to the supply, they place their marker on 1 of these spaces and receive 4 victory points and 1 trade commodity.
* If the player returns 3 cured meat to the supply, they place their marker on 1 of these spaces and receive 5 victory points and 1 trade commodity.

**Important:** A player may return the same set of goods more than once (during different rounds), as long as the corresponding delivery spaces remain available. Note that some spaces are unavailable when playing solo or with only 2 players.

*Example: After scoring victory points for the market and the siesta track, Purple returns 3 olives to the supply (A), and places 1 purple player marker on the unoccupied olive delivery space on the Long Distance Trade sideboard (B).*

### Changes in Scoring

At the end of the game, before players convert their leftover goods into silver, they can trade their goods with the long distance trader. Each player may discard 1 farm good per marker of that type they previously delivered to the Long Distance Trade sideboard for the indicated amount of victory points.

* Silver, olives, grain, and grapes may be traded in for 1 victory point each.
* Food, pigs, wine, and cured meat may be traded in for 2 victory points each.

*Example: At the end of the game, Purple has 2 olives (A), 1 food (B), and 1 wine (C). Purple can trade in the 2 olives (D) for 1 victory point each (E), and 1 wine (F) for 2 victory points (G). Purple has no cured meat, so their marker on the cured meat delivery space cannot earn them any victory points (H). In total, Purple receives 4 victory points from the Long Distance Trade sideboard.*

## Trade Boards

*by Andrei Novac*

Trade Boards introduces both direct interaction between players, as well as a chance to manipulate revenue dice (or gain a bit of extra cash). This new opportunity to deliver resources to other players can make the game a bit easier, but still requires at least one game of base La Granja to easily integrate into the game.

### Components

* 20 trade markers (5 in each of 4 player colors)
* 20 modifier markers (5 in each of 4 player colors)
* 4 trade boards

### Changes in Setup

Set up the game following normal rules. Then:

1. Give each player 5 trade markers and 5 modifier markers in their player color.
2. Randomly deal players trade boards, removing any unused boards from the game if playing with fewer than 4 players.
3. Each player places their trade markers on the 5 designated spaces at the top of their player board.
4. Each player places their modifier markers face up on the 5 designated spaces on the bottom side of their player board, matching the symbol on the trade board and marker.

### Rules

Once per round, a player (exporter) may deliver the depicted good(s) to another player's (importer) trade board to score additional victory points.

To do that, the exporter spends 1 delivery and the required goods depicted next to the leftmost available modifier marker on the importer's trade board to the supply. If there are 2 options, the exporter can choose which goods they want to deliver. Regardless of the number of goods that must be delivered, the exporter always needs just 1 delivery.

*Example: As one of their deliveries, Blue decides to deliver to Yellow's trade board. The only available slot is the second one from the left, as it is the leftmost one with the modifier marker still on it. Blue can choose to deliver either 1 olive or 2 silver.*

After the exporter has spent the goods, they take their leftmost trade marker and place it on the leftmost space on the importer's board without such a marker (A).

*Example continued: Blue decides to deliver 1 olive to Yellow. Blue discards 1 olive from their player board (B) to the supply, takes the leftmost available trade marker (C), and places it on Yellow's trade board (D), covering goods that were just delivered.*

As a result, the importer immediately gains their modifier marker from the slot where goods have just been delivered (the importer does not receive the goods!).

*Example continued: Immediately after the delivery, the importer receives a modifier marker (E) from the last delivery.*

**Important:** After a player has exported 5 times - thus using all their trade markers - they can no longer perform this type of delivery. After all modifier markers leave a player's trade board, no one can deliver to that trade board.

### Modifier Markers

Modifier markers can be used to change the value of a die in the Revenue Phase. A player may only change a die they are drafting by physically changing its value to the new value, and effectively changing the action they will perform. Multiple modifier markers may be used per 1 die. Once used, a modifier marker is permanently removed from the game. Alternatively, a modifier marker can be discarded at any time to receive 1 silver.

**Important:** A modifier marker may be used in part or in its entirety. For instance, a +2 marker may change the revenue value by 1 or 2. A value 6 die rotates to 1 after a +1 modifier marker is used to change its value. A die of value 1 rotates to 6 after a -1 modifier marker is used to change its value, etc.

### Scoring Victory Points

Whenever a player uncovers any victory points on their own trade board, they receive those victory points immediately.

# Star Trek: Captain's Chair – Solo Rulebook

## Table of Contents

*"Computer, load program: Officer Training" — Solo Rules*

*Officer Training is a holodeck program for 1 player that lets you take on Star Trek: Captain's Chair against "the computer" at increasing difficulty. This mode requires a few additional rules. You should play your very first solo game in the simpler Cadet Training Mode (in the main rulebook) first — there you play without an opponent.*

*On the following pages, "you" always refers to you, while "he" or "the computer" refers to your computer opponent. The base rules always apply, unless explicitly stated otherwise.*

*The following is generally true for playing against the computer: it ignores the text on its cards — instead you must look up its so-called Computer Program Cards (CPK, for short). These determine what happens, based on the type or trait of the computer's card. It gains glory and specialty just like you do — but normally no other resources…*

*You win against the computer by having the most Victory Points at Final Scoring — but if Red Alert is triggered, you automatically lose the game.*

## Setup

1. Choose your difficulty level. The available levels are (from easy to hard): Ensign, Lieutenant, Commander, Captain, and Admiral.
2. Carry out Central Setup as described in the main rulebook, but:
   - Use the Stardate cards for solo play (marked "Solo vs.") matching your chosen difficulty (example: "Solo vs. Ensign" for the easiest difficulty).
   - Choose which Captain the computer will play.
   - The computer gets neither action tokens nor mission completion tokens.
   - You are always the starting player.
3. Carry out Player Setup as described in the rulebook.
4. You do not need the solo Directive cards Reinforcements and Conspiracy. (These are only for the Five-Year Mission campaign, see p. 16, and the Conspiratorial Admirals challenge, see p. 7.)
5. Place the computer's captain board to the left of the computer's Briefing Room, in the computer's play area — with the Simple side up. Place 3 specialty markers on the "0" of its specialty tracks.
6. Sort the computer's deck as usual into: Incident, Development, Discard, Mobilized, Reserve, Controlled Location, Available. The Captain card has no marking at the bottom center. Cards marked Incident at the bottom center should already be in the Incident pile in the Market.
7. Place the computer's Captain card next to its Briefing Room. Status cards from the computer's deck are returned to the box.
8. Shuffle all of the computer's Developments face down and set them face down above its Briefing Room. Then also shuffle its Reserve cards and place them face down on top of the Developments (do not shuffle further from here on). This is the computer's Auxiliary Deck.
9. Shuffle all its cards marked Available and place them below its Captain card. This is the Computer Deck. If the computer has cards marked Mobilized or Controlled Location, place them (shuffled together, if there are several) face down on top of the Computer Deck (instead of laying them out, as you would).
10. If the computer has cards marked Discard, place them face up in the computer's discard pile; otherwise leave room next to its deck for its discard pile.
11. Leave two rows open for the computer's cards: its Control Zone (where its bridge officer, controlled Locations, and mobilized Ships are placed) and its Briefing Room (where cards being actively carried out are placed).
12. Place the stated number of away teams on the computer's Captain card.
13. Place the 2 Computer Program Cards matching the computer's chosen captain next to its play area — each with its Traits and Types sides showing — no bridge officer laid out yet.

**No Computer Resources** — Note: unlike you, the computer starts with no resources and no glory.

## The Value of Cards

Before we go deeper into the rules for playing against the computer, we need to briefly explain how the concept of a card's "value" works in the Market and the Neutral Zone. Whenever gaining cards, or securing a Location, comes up here in solo mode, the computer usually chooses the card with the "highest value." But what does that mean?

A card's value is derived from how many VP the computer could score with it if the game ended immediately afterward. To determine the value, proceed as follows:

- Add up the number of VP printed on the card (if any).
- Add the value of a focus symbol (Research/Influence/Military), if present, by taking the computer's current specialty multiplier on the matching track.
- Add exactly 5 to the value if the card has an end-game operation.
- Add 1 to the value for each glory on the card. Other resources (such as dilithium placed by Burnham) do not count.

The final result is the card's value — and the card with the highest value calculated this way counts as the most valuable card. In case of a tie in value, the card with more resources on it (including glory, dilithium, and latinum) is considered more valuable. If there is still a tie among these cards (in the Market/Neutral Zone), the computer chooses whichever of these cards is furthest to the left (regardless of whether it is looking for the lowest or highest value).

Sometimes, instead, a card's value needs to be determined for you. The same criteria apply as for the computer, except that of course your current specialty multipliers are used instead of the computer's. If the computer is instructed to choose the card with the lowest value for you (e.g., when placing glory during the Clean-Up Step, see p. 4), it chooses the card with the lowest value calculated this way; for the card with the highest value for you, it chooses the one with the highest.

**Example: Determining Value** — The computer wants to gain 1 Cargo/1 Ship/1 Ally. In the Market are Mek'leth, Pakled Freighter, and Kelpiens (1 glory sits on the Pakled Freighter). The value of Mek'leth is 2, of the Freighter is 3, and the Kelpiens are worth as much as the computer's Research multiplier. If the multiplier is 3 or less, the computer takes the Pakled Freighter (since in a tie, the number of glory tokens is decisive); if it is 4 or higher, it takes the Kelpiens. Were it, in the same situation, trying to scrap a card, it would have to ignore the Pakled Freighter (since cards with markers or resources cannot be scrapped). If your Research multiplier is 3 or lower, it scraps Mek'leth (in case of a tie, it chooses the leftmost card). If your multiplier is 4 or higher, it scraps the Kelpiens.

If the computer tries to place 1 glory on the card with the lowest value for you (see p. 4 for details), your Influence multiplier decides its choice. At 3 or lower, the glory is placed on the Tachyon Grid, since the other cards are worth more. Also at 3, since the Tachyon Grid is the leftmost card among those with a value of 3. But if your multiplier is 4 or higher, the glory is placed on the Binars, since that is the card (among those with value 3) with the fewest tokens/markers on it.

If the computer tries to send 1 away team to the Neutral Zone (see p. 9 for details), it must choose the Location with the highest value. Memory Alpha has a value of 5, while Regula I has 6 (1 for the VP, 5 for the end-game operation). So if the computer has a Research multiplier of 4, it must choose Xahea (both have a value of 6, but Xahea is further to the left); if it's lower, it will choose Regula I.

## Sequence of Play

All rules of the base game apply. Carry out the computer's turn as follows:

**Resupply Step** — The computer does not carry out a Resupply Step.

**Control Step** — If the computer has secured 1 Location in the Neutral Zone, it takes control of it (everything there is removed, you gain glory as usual). If it has secured 2+ Locations, it chooses the one with the highest value. Refill the Neutral Zone from the Location deck afterward, as usual.

Place the Location the computer gained into its Control Zone. Then carry out the card using its CPKs, as usual (see Computer Program Cards, p. 5). In rare cases this results in the card being logged; otherwise it stays in the Control Zone.

**Action Step** — Draw face down as many cards from the Computer Deck as indicated in the lower right of the active Stardate card. Watch out: this can mean you have to shuffle the Computer Deck (see "Shuffling the Computer Deck" on this page). Place the cards face down in a row in the computer's Briefing Room.

Then reveal the first card of the row and carry it out now using the computer's CPKs (see The Computer Program Cards (CPK), next page). Then reveal the next card of the row, carry it out with the CPKs, and repeat this for every further card. An effect can cause another card to have to be carried out instead (see p. 7); once that other card has been carried out, return to the active card and continue there. Once all face-down cards in the Briefing Room have been revealed and carried out, proceed to the computer's Clean-Up Step.

**Clean-Up Step**

1. Is there a Stardate card in the computer's Briefing Room (because you emptied it during your turn)? Carry out its effect now.
2. Discard all cards from the computer's Briefing Room.
3. Determine the card in the Market with the lowest value for you. In case of a tie, choose the card with fewer resources; if still tied, choose the card further to the left. Place 1 glory (from the Stardate card, as usual) on this card.
   - Is the computer playing Michael Burnham? Discard 1 glory from the Stardate card and instead place 2 dilithium on the card with the lowest value for you.
   - If a Stardate card is emptied during the computer's turn, you place it in your Briefing Room (and vice versa) — the Stardate card here says "of the other captain."

**Shuffling the Computer Deck** — If the Computer Deck is empty when it needs to be interacted with (drawing, discarding, or looking at a card from the deck), shuffle the computer's discard pile (but not the cards in its Briefing Room) to form a new Computer Deck. Then take the top card of the Auxiliary Deck and place it on top of the Computer Deck. Then continue with the interaction that caused the shuffle. Note: this is like your recruiting, but the computer makes no distinction between Reserve and Development cards, and pays no costs either.

## End of the Game

If the game ends with Red Alert, the computer always wins, no matter how many Incidents you have. Otherwise, the game's end is triggered when the 5th Stardate card is emptied — finish playing the current round, then play 1 more round. The game always ends at the end of the computer's turn. Then calculate the score for you and the computer, as described in the base rules. For the computer, note the following:

- The computer gains exactly 5 VP for each end-game operation it possesses (even on its Captain card) — instead of carrying it out — and regardless of whether the card was in play.
- Although the computer does not complete missions: score its specialty multipliers and focus symbols as if it had completed them.
- The computer gains 1 VP for every 2 dilithium/latinum it collected during the game.
  - Is the computer playing Michael Burnham? Then instead 1 VP for every 1 dilithium and latinum, as usual.
- However, if the computer has cards with an asterisk in their VP value (because the card has a SPECIAL operation), score them as indicated.

Note: if this feels like an exception to the rule "ignore all text on the computer's cards" (see below) — Final Scoring happens outside the game! If you have more points than the computer after Final Scoring, you win the game — in case of a tie (or fewer), you lose!

## Carrying Out Computer Effects

### The Computer Program Cards (CPK)

The computer ignores all text on all of its cards during the game. To carry out a card, instead handle its Computer Program Card as follows:

1. First look at the CPK: Traits. Go through the card from top to bottom — looking for the first trait that is present on the card being carried out. Found it? Go to step 3.
   - Does the card being carried out have the Wildcard trait? Then carry out the first trait on the CPK (skipping the Surprise row when doing so).
2. Found no matching trait on the CPK: Traits? Go to the CPK: Types (whichever side is showing). Go through the card from top to bottom — looking for the type of the card being carried out.
3. Found a matching row? Carry out the effects of that row, one after another, regardless of the card's actual text.

**Specialties** — The computer gains Research/Influence/Military on its captain board just like you do. If there is a tie between tracks (when it must raise the highest/lowest track), the computer always chooses the topmost one (i.e. Research > Influence > Military).

**Example: Carrying Out the CPKs** — The computer wants to carry out Worf, whose traits are Attack, Klingon, Security, and Starfleet; on Jean-Luc Picard's CPK: Traits, 2 of those appear. Since Klingon is the higher trait on the list, that's the effect the computer must carry out.

The U.S.S. Bozeman has the traits Anomaly, Starfleet, and Time Travel — none of which is on Jean-Luc Picard's CPK: Traits. So for the computer you must instead check the CPK: Types, and carry out the row for the type Ship.

**"This Card"** — If the effect of a CPK row refers to "this card," it always means the card currently being carried out that matches this row.

**The Surprise Trait** — If a card has the Surprise trait, it also has a "Surprise (computer only):" operation. In this case (as an exception) you carry out the Surprise operation printed on the card for the computer, and ignore the CPKs. This is noted as a reminder on the CPK: Traits. This is the only case in which the computer carries out card text.

**Location** — If the computer carries out a Location and it is still in its Briefing Room at the end of the effect (because it wasn't logged), move it into the computer's Control Zone. This rule applies regardless of which CPK row was carried out.

**Example: Carrying Out a Location** — Regardless of whether a Location was played from the Computer Deck, or just brought under control, the computer carries out the matching effect on the CPKs. If Picard plays the Daystrom Institute, he carries out the Synthetic/Android row and moves the card into the Control Zone. If Picard takes control of the Ancient Relay Station, he carries out the Alien row. This logs the carried-out card, tucking it under the computer's Captain card, instead of placing it in its Control Zone. However, if a Location without traits had instead been played/brought under control by the computer, the Location row on the CPK: Types would have to be carried out, and therefore the card would be moved into the Control Zone.

## Bridge Officers

If an effect promotes a Person to the computer's bridge officer, place the card from the computer's Briefing Room near its Captain card (at the front of the Control Zone). Then flip the CPK: Types from "– no bridge officer" to "– with bridge officer."

The computer can only have 1 bridge officer. If it promotes another, discharge the previous bridge officer (i.e. place it on the computer's discard pile).

If an effect (yours, or from a CPK) discharges the computer's bridge officer (or logs it), flip the CPK: Types back from "– with bridge officer" to "– no bridge officer" (unless it is replaced by a new one).

If an effect forces the computer to recall its bridge officer, it is discharged instead.

**The Computer and Its Bridge Officer** — In short: the computer can carry out stronger effects as long as it has a bridge officer — regardless of who that officer is, or what traits they have. Some of these stronger effects discharge the bridge officer itself, or log them, as an additional cost. It would certainly be better for you to find an Attack with which to discharge its bridge officer beforehand…

## Searching the Computer's Discard Pile

If an effect instructs you to carry out/promote/log/etc. a card from the computer's discard pile, choose the top (i.e. most recently discarded) card with that type/trait.

## Carrying Out Other Cards

If the computer is instructed by an effect to carry out another card (e.g. the top card of the Computer Deck, the Auxiliary Deck, or a card it just gained), place it in its Briefing Room (face up) and look up the matching row on the CPKs to carry it out immediately. This does not count toward the computer's actions for this turn. Once the card has been fully carried out, return to carrying out the original card.

**Incidents and Unspecified Choices** — If an Incident would be logged by an effect of the computer's, it is instead returned to the Incident pile. If one of your effects presents the computer with a choice to return an Incident, it never does so. For other effects of your cards that present the computer with an unspecified choice, it always chooses the first stated option it can carry out (even if that option has no effect).

**Example: Unspecified Choice** — The computer must decide here whether to discharge 1 Ship or take 1 Incident. Since discharging the ship is the first option, it chooses that.

## Market and Neutral Zone

### Gaining/Taking Cards

If a CPK effect instructs the computer to gain a card with a specific specialty or focus symbol, or a specific trait/type, the computer gains the card as follows:

- **"A > B":** Is there a card with "A" in the Market? The computer gains it; if not, it gains a card with "B" instead. If there are multiple cards with "A" (or then "B"), it gains the one with the highest value.
- **"A / B":** The computer gains the card with "A" or the card with "B" (or with both), whichever has the higher value.

For more complicated wording, ">" is resolved before "/". For example: "A > B / C" means "Gain 1 card with 'A' if possible; otherwise gain 1 card with either 'B' or 'C' (whichever is higher-value)." "A / B > C" instead means "Gain, if possible, 1 card with 'A' or 'B' (or both), whichever of those has the higher value; otherwise gain 1 card with 'C'."

**Example** — The computer will take the card with the highest value that has an Influence focus symbol and/or is a Ship. And even if no card in the Market has an Influence focus symbol, it definitely gains 1 Ship.

The computer never gains the top face-down card of a Market pile — it always gains a face-up one.

If the effect uses the keyword Gain, place the card in the computer's discard pile. If the effect uses the keyword Take, place the card on top of the Computer Deck; if an effect during your turn results in you giving the computer a card, or forcing it to take one (likely 1 Incident), place that card on top of the Computer Deck as well.

**Example** — If a Market card has the Klingon trait, the computer takes it, regardless of its type. Otherwise it takes 1 Ally. Should the Ally pile be empty, meaning none are left in the Market, it instead takes 1 Ship.

Watch out: computer effects can also say Take even though they concern cards in the Market (unlike your card effects), in addition to Encounters and Incidents!

If resources (usually glory) are on a card the computer takes/gains, it gains those too.

### Scrapping Cards

If a CPK instructs the computer to "scrap the card with the highest value in the Market (ignoring cards with resources)" — it chooses the card with the highest value for you (not for itself) and scraps it. The computer may not scrap a card with resources on it (just as you may not).

**Example** — If one of the Market cards has a Research focus symbol, the computer will gain it. If not, it will next gain a card with the Alien trait. If none of those are present either, it chooses 1 Ally or 1 Cargo, whichever has the higher value.

### Gaining Resources

Just like you, the computer collects glory, and any glory it gains (except from the Market) is taken from the Stardate card (or, at the end of the game, from the supply), as usual.

The computer only ever gains dilithium in two cases. First, if you or the computer plays Michael Burnham — in this case dilithium can be brought into the Market and thereby gained by the computer and scored at game's end. Second, if one of your card effects explicitly gives it resources.

### The Neutral Zone

When the computer chooses a Location to move a Ship to, or send its away teams to, it does so based on the total number of ship tokens and away teams there. In case of a tie, it chooses the card with the higher value. However, the computer never moves ships or away teams to a Location in its own Control Zone, or to a neutral Location it has already secured by 1 more than necessary.

In every case, the computer chooses the Location with the highest value in case of a tie. If the computer is forced to recall or discharge a mobilized Ship, discard the card instead.

If the computer can choose which Ship to discharge (e.g., if you play "force your opponent to discharge 1 Ship"), it discharges the one most recently mobilized.

### Sending Away Teams

If an away team is sent by a CPK, move 1 away team from the computer's Captain card to a Location in the Neutral Zone — the computer, too, may not send away teams to Locations where you have more ship tokens than it does, unless explicitly stated otherwise.

The computer prefers to send away teams to Locations where it already has the most ship tokens and/or away teams. In case of a tie, it sends the away team to the higher-value Location. However, if the effect specifies a preferred trait, that takes precedence.

If the computer has no away teams left available on its Captain card when it must place one (or receives an instruction to do so), it removes 1 from the Location where it has, in total, the fewest ship tokens and/or away teams (excluding the target Location) and places it on the target Location; in case of a tie, [text continues on the next source page].

### Mobilizing and Warping Ships

If a CPK effect states that a Ship should be mobilized, move the corresponding card into the computer's Control Zone and place the Ship's token on the card. Lay Ships out in the Control Zone in the order in which they were mobilized.

There are effects that state a Ship should explore or head for a Location — for these you move its ship token to a Location in the Neutral Zone, similar to the player's warp effect.

- When **exploring**, the ship warps to the Location with the fewest total ship tokens and away teams there (ideally none at all).
- When **heading for**, the ship warps to the Location where you have the most ship tokens and away teams.

In every case, in a tie the computer removes the away team from the Location with the lowest value (for it).

**Example: Sending Away Teams** — "Send 1 away team to 1 Starbase > Starfleet > Location in the Neutral Zone" means the computer will send away teams to a starbase before considering other Locations, even if those had more of its markers. If there are multiple Locations with the Starbase trait in the Neutral Zone, it chooses the one where it has the most markers. If no Location with the Starbase trait is available, the computer repeats this process, this time with the Starfleet trait. If neither trait is present, it chooses among the remaining cards in the Neutral Zone.

## Direct Interaction with the Computer

### Computer Attacks

If a CPK's computer effects negatively affect you, they count as an ATTACK operation, regardless of whether the card being carried out has the Attack trait or not. These effects are highlighted in red on the CPK. The part highlighted in red and bold is carried out by you yourself, while the rest is still read from the computer's perspective.

If you have REACTION operations that are triggered by attacks against you, they are triggered by such attacks.

If you have a way to defend against the attack, you ignore only the part that affects you (i.e. the red part) completely, while the rest of the effect (if any) is still carried out. However: attacks that were defended against still count as having been carried out by the computer for the purposes of CPK effects.

Note that other effects that concern you, but do not represent attacks, are also highlighted, but not in red.

### Attacking the Computer

Attacks that target the computer's hand (e.g. revealing it, forcing a discard, etc.) have no effect, since the computer has no hand. Attacks that force the computer to find a card, or to choose from its discard pile, also have no effect. If you carry out such effects, you instead choose yourself whether they were successful.

This is not the same situation as an attack that forces the computer to choose between two negative effects. In that case, it always chooses the first listed effect it can carry out (see Incidents and Unspecified Choices on p. 7). Whenever an attack — against the targets named above — has no effect, you may place the top card of the computer's discard pile on top of the Computer Deck. Note: this does not apply if you were allowed to decide that the attack failed.

**Example 1** — You play a card with the following effect: "Force your opponent to reveal their hand. You may discard 1 Attack from the opponent's hand to gain 1 glory." You gain 1 glory from this effect — otherwise nothing happens.

**Example 2** — You play a card with the following effect: "Force your opponent to log 1 Person from hand or discard pile. If they do, your opponent may draw 1 card. If they cannot, they take 1 Incident. You may promote this card to bridge officer." You may choose, for the computer, whether it "draws" the top card of its deck (in the computer's case, "discards" it), or must take 1 Incident — so you will presumably choose the latter.

### Helping the Computer

If one of your card effects presents the computer with the choice/forces it to draw a card, discard the top card of the Computer Deck instead.

If one of your card effects gives the computer the opportunity to return an Incident, it declines to do so.

If one of your card effects gives the computer a resource, it gains the resource as usual (dilithium/latinum from the supply, glory from the Stardate card).

Since the computer usually possesses no resources other than glory, treat Steal effects you carry out (including "steal glory") as automatically successful — but take the resources from the supply instead (this still does not count as gaining resources for purposes of reactions, as with the Horta). Even if the computer possesses dilithium/latinum, you can never take it away from the computer; if you steal resources from the computer, you always take them from the supply instead.

### Example: A Complete Computer Turn

You are playing against Koloth on "Commander" difficulty. Nothing happens during his Control Step, since he hasn't secured a Location yet. At the start of his Action Step you draw 3 cards from the Computer Deck (as shown on the top Stardate card) and place them face down in a row next to the deck.

The first revealed card is Krell. Since his only trait, Klingon, is not on Koloth's CPK: Traits, his CPK: Types — no bridge officer is used. You choose the Person row and follow the instructions. However, since the card to be logged is an Incident (Hostile Contact), it is instead slid back under the Incident pile — Incidents are not logged.

The top card of the Computer Deck is discarded (A Good Day to Die). The next card of the Computer Deck is logged. Since Koloth's Influence is lower than his Military, his Influence is raised by 1.

Then the computer tries to gain 1 Weapon or 1 Ship, preferring the former. It's in luck — the Lirpa is available. The computer gains the Lirpa and places it in its discard pile (even though the available Ship would have had a higher value). Finally, Krell is promoted to bridge officer, so the CPK: Types is flipped to the "– with bridge officer" side.

The second card is revealed. It's the I.K.S. Klothos. The Klothos is placed in the computer's Control Zone. The computer gains 1 Influence. He has instructions to head for a Location — so he moves to the neutral Location with the most opposing (i.e. your) ship tokens and/or away teams: in this case the Ancient Relay Station.

Now the computer sends an away team to the Location where it has the most: the Ancient Relay Station and Betazed each have 1; in that case it chooses Betazed, since it has the higher value (3).

The last card is Rura Penthe, Koloth's own Location. Krell's brief time as bridge officer ends, and the computer gains 1 glory, leaving only 1 glory on the Stardate card. Don't forget to flip the CPK back to the "– no bridge officer" side!

The top card of the Computer Deck is revealed and carried out. It's Klingon Disruptors. Klingon Disruptors has the traits Attack and Weapon. Both have executable effects on Koloth's CPK: Traits. But only the first effect is carried out, because it's listed higher.

The computer gains 1 Military. Since it doesn't yet have 9 Military, it removes 1 of your away teams (you choose which one is removed). Defended attacks still count as having been carried out, which is why the last clause is not carried out and the computer does not gain the additional Military.

First the computer scraps Biobed, since it would be the card with the highest value for you, as your Research multiplier is 3. (Biobed is preferred over Kelpiens because, at equal value, the left card is chosen.) However, since you have the Phaser mobilized in your Fleet Zone, you may rescue your away team on the Ancient Relay Station as a reaction. This completes carrying out Rura Penthe, and the card is placed in the computer's Control Zone.

During the Clean-Up Step, the one remaining card in the computer's Briefing Room is discarded. The computer decides to place the glory on Harry Mudd, since it only has a value of 1 for you, and since the Xindi-Reptilian Warship already has more tokens on it (and Picard's Military multiplier is low). This removes the last glory from the Stardate card, and it is placed in your Briefing Room. It will be triggered during your next Clean-Up Step. The computer's turn is over. You may now take your turn, but don't forget to place 7 glory on the new Stardate card first.

## Five-Year Mission: A Solo Campaign

You are fresh out of the Academy — an Ensign with stars in your eyes. Since you show promising talent, headquarters has assigned you a small ship and is sending you out into the infinite depths: you are on a five-year mission. To earn promotions, you will need to prove yourself during the mission in various engagements — against a series of opposing captains.

Each game represents a six-month tour of duty, after which you return to a starbase to maintain your equipment and have your performance evaluated. Your campaign ends after 5 years (i.e. 10 games), or when you are successfully promoted to Admiral (by defeating 5 different crews).

Begin by starting a new logbook (you can copy the logbook on the second-to-last page of this rulebook). Write down your captain for this campaign. Your starting rank is always Ensign.

Then choose a campaign mode (also note this in your logbook). From easiest to hardest, these are:

- Phasers on Stun
- Yellow Alert
- Gates of Sto'vo'kor
- Kobayashi Maru

**Determining the Computer's Difficulty Level**

| Your Current Rank | Phasers on Stun | Yellow Alert | Gates of Sto'vo'kor | Kobayashi Maru |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ensign | Ensign | Lieutenant | Commander | Admiral |
| Lieutenant | Lieutenant | Commander | Commander | Admiral |
| Commander | Commander | Captain | Admiral | Admiral |
| Captain | Captain | Captain | Admiral | Admiral |
| Commodore | Captain | Admiral | Admiral | Admiral |

Set up the game against a computer (choose the captain, or pick randomly). You may play on either side of the captain board, as usual. The computer's difficulty level is based on the campaign mode and increases as you get promoted; to determine the computer's difficulty level, use the table on this page. Log the result in your logbook after the game.

You return to the starbase after every game — whether you win or lose. This lets you improve your captain. Log these changes to your deck in your logbook — they remain in effect for the rest of the campaign.

Ties against the computer count as losses.

If you win the game, you are additionally promoted as follows: Ensign to Lieutenant, Lieutenant to Commander, Commander to Captain, Captain to Commodore, and Commodore to Admiral. If you reach the rank of Admiral (i.e. 5 wins), the campaign ends immediately (and you win). Otherwise it ends after 10 games.

In future games of this campaign, you may not choose a captain again that you have already defeated during this campaign.

## New Concept: The Reinforcement Pile and Upgrades

The Reinforcement Pile is a new concept in campaign mode. As soon as you have at least 1 card in your Reinforcement Pile, also shuffle the additional Directive card Reinforcements (marked "Solo Campaign") into your Available cards. This card gives you access to cards in your Reinforcement Pile. It is independent of your Reserve and your Developments — but, like the Developments, it is an open pile. Once a card is added to the Reinforcement Pile, it starts every future game there. Cards still in your Reinforcement Pile at the end of the game do not count for Final Scoring; they must have been taken via Reinforcements to count.

**Restrictions** — Important: you may never add an Encounter, an Incident, or a Location to your Reinforcement Pile.

**Upgrade Options** — After a game, your possible upgrades are:

**Option A — Reinforce:** Choose 1 common card you gained during the game (i.e. one you had from the Market during the game you just finished) that is allowed by your opponent's CPK (as listed on the "Five-Year Mission" CPK, Upgrades). Add it to your Reinforcement Pile. Have no matching cards? Choose Option B.

**Option B — Boost:** Choose 1 alternative bonus (as listed on the "Five-Year Mission" CPK, Upgrades).

The options change depending on whether you won or lost. Log the chosen upgrade in your logbook. If you choose a BOOST, it is carried out at the start of every future game of the campaign.

**Example: Choosing an Upgrade** — You've just faced Koloth, Dahar Master. Now the following upgrades are available to you:

- Option A lets you choose 1 common card gained during the game:
  1. If you won against Koloth, you may choose a Ship.
  2. If you lost, your choice must be more precise: you may choose a common card with at least one of the following traits: Weapon / Attack / Klingon — though the type of the card is not restricted — the traits represent what you learned from Koloth as he defeated you.
- Do you have no such common cards (or don't want to choose one)? You may choose Option B: an alternative bonus.
  1. If you lost, you may add 1 of your own cards from the Reserve or your starting deck to your Reinforcement Pile — the former gets you to your Developments faster, the latter lets you temporarily remove a card (that you think isn't useful early game) from rotation.
  2. If you won, you may choose 1 of the listed Boost effects: start every further game of the campaign with 3 additional Military or 2 additional glory (from the first Stardate card).

**Logging Upgrades** — If you choose a card as reinforcement, write its name in the Upgrades field of your logbook. If you choose a Boost, write the effect (or an abbreviation) in the same place. Consult this list when setting up further games of this campaign.

## Challenges

Anyone who wants to make their game even more intense can, in addition to the chosen (campaign) difficulty level, take on one or more challenges:

**Live Long and Prosper™** — After winning a game, start the next game with either no dilithium or no latinum (your choice). If you win again, start the next game, and every following one, with no dilithium and no latinum, until you lose. After losing a game, you start again with normal resources.

**That's Not a Sign of Weakness; That's Life** — If you are playing as Lieutenant or a higher rank, change your setup as follows: note how many cards are in your Reserve, then shuffle your Reserve and Available cards together and draw as many cards as your Reserve had: this is your new Reserve; shuffle the remaining cards — this is your deck. This challenge cannot be chosen for decks that have no Reserve.

**Two Weeks to the Next Outpost** — After winning a game, shuffle Reinforcements (from your deck) into your Reserve at the start of the next game. If you lose, start the next game with Reinforcements back in the deck.

**Runs Like a Baby Gazelle** — After winning two games in a row, shuffle 1 Incident from the Incident pile into your deck during setup. Repeat this for every further game (max. 1 Incident), until you lose.

**Ferengi Rules of Acquisition** — After winning a game, Upgrade Option B is unavailable to you. (I.e.: you must always choose Option A; if you have no such card, you receive no upgrade.)

**The Only Ship in the Quadrant** — As soon as your starting Ship is discharged or recalled by any source, you lose the game. (This challenge cannot be chosen for crew decks that don't start with a mobilized Ship.)

**They Don't Arrive Until Tuesday** — During setup, set aside 1 of your away teams — it is temporarily unusable. When your Reserve becomes empty, place the away team back on your Captain card. After winning a game, set aside 2 away teams during setup and proceed as described above (getting them back once your Reserve is empty). This challenge cannot be chosen for decks that have no Reserve.

## Solo Player Aid

**Computer Turn Flow**

1. Skip the Resupply Step.
2. Control Step (highest value if there are several; carry out the gained Location).
3. Action Step (draw # cards as shown on the Stardate card, carry them out one after another).
4. Clean-Up Step (carry out any Stardate cards, discard cards from the Briefing Room, place glory on the Market card with the lowest value for you).

**General Computer Terms**

- **Gain:** New card from the Market (highest value) to the computer's discard pile.
- **Take:** Onto the top of the Computer Deck. If from the Market, the card with the highest value.
- **Mobilize:** Place in the Control Zone (in order of mobilization).
- **Discharge, Discard, Recall:** Place the card in the computer's discard pile. If it was the bridge officer, flip the CPK: Types. If a Ship must be chosen, choose the most recently mobilized.
- **Promote:** Place the Person in the Control Zone. Flip the CPK: Types.
- **Log:** Place the card under the Captain card. If the bridge officer is logged, flip the CPK: Types.
- **Explore:** Warp the ship token to the Location in the Neutral Zone with the fewest total markers (in case of a tie: the most valuable).
- **Head For:** Warp the ship token to the Location in the Neutral Zone with the most of your markers (in case of a tie: the most valuable).
- **Send:** Place 1 away team on the Location in the Neutral Zone with the most of the computer's markers (in case of a tie: the most valuable) — where you do not have more ship tokens than the computer.
- **Scrap:** Choose the card from the Market (without resources) with the highest value for you and place it on the Junk pile.

**Determining a Card's Value — Highest/Lowest Value (for the computer)**

- VP.
- Each focus symbol (Research/Influence/Military), if present, increases the value by the computer's corresponding current multiplier.
- Every end-game operation increases the value by 5.
- Each glory on it increases the value by 1.

For the highest/lowest value for you, your specialty multipliers are used for the calculation.

**Reminders**

- Logging an Incident becomes returning the Incident.
- A Location is always moved from the Briefing Room to the Control Zone, regardless of which row was carried out.
- If the computer is forced (or allowed to choose) to draw a card, discard the top card of its deck instead.
- The computer declines to return Incidents via your operations.
- It never sends ships/away teams to neutral Locations where it already has more than enough markers to secure them (i.e.: 4+ markers and 3 more than you).

**Direct Interactions**

- Computer attacks you: ATTACK EFFECTS ARE HIGHLIGHTED THIS WAY.
- Computer affects you (without an attack): INTERACTIVE EFFECTS ARE HIGHLIGHTED THIS WAY.
- Steal from the computer: Automatic success — take resources from the general supply.
- Let the computer draw a card: Discard the top card of the Computer Deck.
- Unspecified choice for the computer: Always chooses the first possible option.

**Final Scoring for the Computer**

Ignore missions. Score Research/Influence/Military as usual. Score printed VP as usual. 1 VP per glory, 1 VP per 2 dilithium/latinum. 5 VP per end-game operation (even if not in play). Carry out SPECIAL operations if there is an asterisk in the VP value.

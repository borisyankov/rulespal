# Skull Rulebook

## Table of Contents

*Skull (also known as Skull & Roses) is an intense bluffing game for 3 to 6 players that requires nerves of steel, audacity, and a nose for subtle strategy. Players secretly place rose or skull cards face down, then bid on how many cards they can flip revealing only roses. Reveal a skull and you fail — and lose a card. Win two bets and you win the game. It's a game of pure psychology: how much risk are you willing to take? A game by Hervé Marly, published by Lui-même.*

## Goal of the Game

To win the game, a player must **win 2 bets**: make the most daring bet, then flip over that number of cards without revealing a single Skull.

## Setup

Each player takes a **game mat**, placed Skull-side up in front of them, and a **pack of 4 cards** with the same back (3 Roses and 1 Skull), held hidden in hand. Choose a first player.

## Playing a Round

### 1. Card Placement

Each player looks at their cards and places one face down on their mat. Then, starting with the first player and going clockwise, each player either places another card face down on top of their stack, or (on their turn) issues a **challenge** instead. Only one card is played at a time. Continue until a player chooses to challenge rather than place a card. (The first player may challenge as soon as the placement phase begins; a player with no cards left in hand must challenge.)

### 2. The Challenge (Pass or Bid More)

The challenger announces the number of cards they intend to flip over from among all those placed on the mats. A challenge can be for as few as one card. Going clockwise, each player must either **raise the bid** (to any higher number — bids need not increase by only 1) or **pass** (pushing their mat to the middle of the table; a player who passes cannot bid again). Continue until all but one player have passed. The highest bidder becomes the **challenger**.

### 3. Revelation

The challenger flips cards one at a time, following these rules:

- They must **start by revealing all the cards on their own mat** (from the top down).
- After their own cards, they may **freely choose which player's cards to flip** next, revealing from the top of each chosen stack, one at a time.
- The challenger is never forced to reveal all of another player's cards.

**If all revealed cards are Roses** and the challenger reaches their bid number, the **bet is won**: flip your mat to its Roses side. Winning **two bets wins the game**.

**If the challenger reveals a Skull**, the bet is **lost** and they stop flipping immediately.

## Losing a Bet

When a bet is lost, the challenger loses one card:

- If the revealed Skull was one of the challenger's **own** cards, the challenger discards one of their cards at random (they shuffle and lose one).
- If the revealed Skull belonged to **another player**, that player chooses which of the challenger's cards to remove.

The lost card is removed from the game. A player who loses their last card is **eliminated** (and being eliminated with a revealed skull is part of the special rules below).

## Special Situations (a player with one card, "stoned")

For a player who has only one card remaining:

- **Card placement phase:** if they are the first player or the turn reaches them, they **must** issue a challenge.
- **Revelation phase:** if their one revealed card is a Skull, that player is **eliminated** from the game.

If a player accidentally reveals a card on their mat, it remains visible until the end of the round.

## Winning the Game

A player wins immediately upon winning their **second bet**. (Alternatively, play continues until only one player remains.) Being the first player is very advantageous, so it's often worth taking risks — including bluffing by challenging or raising after playing a Skull — to earn that position.
